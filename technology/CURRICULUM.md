# PRIM3 Technology Curriculum

**Status:** PROPOSED  
**Purpose:** Keep the infotainment progression coherent across seven seasons without pretending one person literally completes every field of knowledge.

The protagonist develops a systems-level command of information technology, then increasingly specialized mastery through research, cohorts, missions and Prime's institutional architecture. Domain experts remain essential.

## Teaching rule

Every lesson must answer four questions:

1. What real problem does the character face?
2. What technical concept changes their options?
3. What mistake or limitation makes the concept memorable?
4. What remains simplified or fictionalized?

## Seven-season progression

### Season 1 — Foundations / Operator literacy

- Hardware and server components
- Operating systems
- Command line and scripting
- Networking fundamentals
- Data-center power, cooling and physical topology
- Monitoring, logs and incident response
- Identity, access and least privilege
- Backups, redundancy and recovery
- Documentation, evidence and professional ethics

**Story level:** The protagonist can diagnose and stabilize bounded systems while discovering how formal models sharpen practical knowledge.

### Season 2 — Infrastructure / Distributed systems

- Virtualization and containers
- Storage systems
- Cloud architecture
- Databases
- APIs and message systems
- Automation and infrastructure as code
- Reliability engineering
- Capacity and cost
- Supply-chain dependencies

**Story level:** Failures stop belonging to one machine and begin propagating across organizations.

### Season 3 — Cybersecurity / Adversarial systems

- Threat modeling
- Authentication and cryptography
- Network defense
- Secure software principles
- Digital forensics
- Social engineering
- Vulnerability management
- Zero-trust architecture
- Governance and lawful authority

**Story level:** Every system now has an intelligent opponent.

### Season 4 — AI / Perception / Robotics

- Statistics and uncertainty
- Machine learning fundamentals
- Computer vision
- Natural-language systems
- Edge AI
- Sensor fusion
- Robotics and control
- Model failure, bias and adversarial inputs
- Human-in-the-loop safety

**Story level:** Prime is revealed; intelligence becomes infrastructure but never certainty.

### Season 5 — Biotechnology / Human systems

- Molecular and cellular foundations
- Genetics and bioinformatics
- Biomedical sensing
- Epidemiology and public health data
- Laboratory automation
- Neural interfaces
- Prosthetics and assistive systems
- Biosafety, consent and dual-use risk

**Story level:** Technology can now alter bodies, populations and inheritance.

### Season 6 — Planetary infrastructure / Continuity

- Energy systems
- Water and environmental monitoring
- Transportation and logistics
- Communications resilience
- Industrial control
- Fabrication and supply networks
- Emergency operations
- Civic technology and governance
- Decentralized continuity systems

**Story level:** Site 0 becomes understandable as a coordinated machine rather than a building.

### Season 7 — Integration / Prime architecture

- Systems-of-systems engineering
- Formal risk and safety cases
- Mission assurance
- Multi-domain command
- Technology governance
- Succession, accountability and audit
- Limits of prediction
- Designing systems that survive their creator

**Story level:** The protagonist becomes capable of reaching Prime—and learns why capability alone is insufficient.

## Accuracy requirements

- Cite primary or authoritative sources in technical research notes.
- Mark fictional extrapolation explicitly.
- Do not publish actionable harmful procedures merely for realism.
- Do not let Prime bypass authentication, physics, uncertainty, energy or heat without explanation.
- Avoid “Ph.D. in everything” language inside formal canon. His distinction is exceptional integration; specialists can still exceed him within narrow domains.
