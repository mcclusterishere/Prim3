# The Agency Case Spine — PROPOSAL

Everything in this file is a proposal. Nothing here is a mission, a song, a death or a canon change. It is the one piece of **ordinary agency work** that the audience watches cross every floor of Equity Uprise over seven years, so that when the building is finally assaulted the audience knows what a Tuesday there was for.

Working name: **Case 0417 — "Resolved by Morning."**

---

## 1. Why a case, and why this one

Missions are the plot. The case is the job. Issue #19 asked for a fictional agency workflow with real artifacts, real review and real waiting periods, and said not to use private real client data as set dressing. So the case is small, fictional, and boring in exactly the way real institutional failures are boring: a ticket that keeps closing itself.

It is chosen because it touches every spotlight floor in the order the seasons reveal them, it can be carried by the owners `CANON.md` §21 already assigns, and it gives Helen Clarke's S7 governance payoff (`ENSEMBLE-EPISODE-CHARACTER-DEVELOPMENT-MAP.md`: "21 governance payoff") a concrete object to pay off on.

---

## 2. The complaint

A **community resource room** served by Equity Uprise's community-connectivity programme loses connectivity and metering every night between roughly 01:00 and 05:00. By morning the gateway has recovered on its own, so the agency's ticket system auto-resolves the ticket: *"service restored, no action required."* The complainant reopens it. It resolves again by morning.

The complainant is the resource room's volunteer coordinator. **No name, no place name, no organisation name is proposed here** (`AGENTS.md`: do not name what canon has not named). In drafts the person is **the Coordinator** and the site is **the resource room**. If the owner wants the Coordinator to become a recurring civilian, that person goes through the character creation gate.

What the resource room is used for at night is what makes it matter: a residents' telehealth kiosk, a late-shift worker's only reliable line, a cohort applicant's exam upload. The harm is a dropped call, not a catastrophe. The spine is built so that the dropped call is enough.

The artefacts that persist (and that `WORLD-MEMORY-CONTRACT.md` §2.1 requires the simulation to keep):

- the ticket, with its reopen count;
- the Coordinator's handwritten outage log (times, who was affected);
- one pulled gateway unit with a bench tag;
- the supply contract, and later its financing addendum;
- Li Wei's decision-rule card (canon residue of S5E2);
- the vendor-termination letter, unsigned until S7.

---

## 3. Season by season

The case moves in the order Issue #19 gives: F1 → F2 → F5 → F3 + B1 → F4 → F6 → L7 → F1. Each season it advances one floor's worth. Nobody relocates a mission for it. Each beat is a **workplace scene around the mission** (the "gain" and "fit" scenes in `SEASON-REVEAL-MAP.md` §2), two to four minutes, and each gives its floor's people a development write.

### Season 1 — Recognition — Floor 1 (Grace, Miguel, Valeria)

**Enters: S1E1 The Blip**, as background during the one working shift Issue #19 names as the first acceptance proof. Grace's participant-services desk has the ticket open on reopen number four. Valeria is asked to close it properly and cannot find the button that means *not resolved*. Miguel, at the access desk, has nothing to do with it and is the only one who asks who installed the gateway.

> **VALERIA:** It says resolved.
>
> **GRACE:** It says resolved every morning. Ask it what it means by *resolved*.
>
> **VALERIA:** It's a ticket. It doesn't mean anything.
>
> **GRACE:** Then it should stop saying things.

**S1E2 Permission Slip:** no case beat; Miguel's episode, and his last. **S1E3 Open Book (gain scene):** Grace carries the reopened ticket upstairs and is told, kindly, by Oksana that F5 does source review, not service tickets. Oksana nonetheless opens a browser tab. The Coordinator has been posting outage times publicly for six weeks. *"Open-source intelligence,"* Oksana says, *"is sometimes just a tired person with a notebook."*

Residue written forward: the ticket moves from Grace's queue to a folder on her desk that is physically there in every later F1 scene. Nusrat now works the access desk alone.

### Season 2 — Belonging — Floor 2 (Chioma, Sari, Thandiwe)

**S2E1 The Human Layer:** one of the F2 forum attendees is from the resource room. Chioma hears the outage story second-hand and files it under things she will deal with later. **S2E2 Four Colors:** Thandiwe, needing a real-world exercise, uses the ticket as a cohort case study without asking Grace; Grace is not pleased; the cohort produces the first timeline. **S2E3 Dead Air:** the F2 wireless symptom and the resource room's gateway share a model number. Sari notices it in the trace. It is one line, because the episode has a death to carry.

> **SARI:** *(on the trace)* Same gateway family as the community kits. Same firmware train.
>
> **CHIOMA:** Note it.
>
> **SARI:** I noted it in Season One. Nobody's reading my notes.

The Dead Air consequence that belongs to this spine: the F2 vacancy left by the casualty (identity still deferred, `episodes/README.md`) means the cohort case study is never finished. The half-finished timeline stays pinned on the F2 lead desk. Chioma reclassifies the ticket from *service* to *case*. That reclassification is the moment it gets a number the audience will hear again: 0417.

### Season 3 — Trust — Floor 5 (Oksana, Leila) and Floor 3 + B1 (Aya, Minh)

**S3E1 Exposure:** the breach exposes the case file, including the Coordinator's outage log with residents' names on it. Grace's question in the canon episode ("who is being hurt right now?") has a face. Leila takes the file to the authority-analysis desk because the question is now *who was authorised to hold these names at all*. Oksana wants provenance on the gateway supply chain.

**S3E2 Bad Input (fit):** Aya and Minh pull one of the resource room's gateways to the B1 bench. The firmware build string is one they have seen before: it is the same build family as the building's own occupancy and metering sensors. Minh writes it in his bench notebook. Aya tells him to tag the unit and stop; it is not their case. It stays on the bench, tagged, for two seasons.

> **MINH:** Our sensors. Their gateway. One build.
>
> **AYA:** One vendor sells to two customers. That's commerce, not conspiracy.
>
> **MINH:** Then why does it fail at one in the morning for them and never for us?
>
> **AYA:** *(beat)* Tag it.

**S3E3 Patient Zero:** no case beat; Dmitri's and Oksana's episode.

### Season 4 — Commitment — Floor 4 (Priya, Maren), B1 at the centre

**The supplier-conflict reveal lands here, two seasons after the case became a case in S2.**

**S4E1 Clean Install (gain scene):** Maren is asked by the F6 risk/sponsor office to draft a public "service improvement" notice for the community programme. Valeria, who just installed replacement hardware on the mission, is asked to confirm one sentence and will not, because the sentence is not true yet. Maren keeps the draft. **S4E2 Dumpster Signal (gain scene):** Priya is sent the same notice with a softer verb and refuses it. Grace brings her the whole folder.

**S4E3 House of Eyes (gain interlude):** the connected building's impossible states include the F4 recording suite reporting occupancy while Maren is alone inside it. Jonas and Pylon isolate the sensor family. Minh brings the tagged gateway up from the bench. Same build. Then Leila, reading the supply contract Oksana pulled in S3E1, finds the part nobody read: the community-programme gateways and the building's sensor estate were procured under **one supply contract**, sponsored through the F6 risk/mission-sponsor desk, and financed through a vehicle associated with **Grant Voss** (canon recurring "respectable-money antagonist/mirror", `episodes/S05E02-CAP-EX-VS-OP-EX.md` residue, pulled one episode earlier as a name on a page, not a scene).

The conflict, stated plainly for the writer and never in one line of dialogue: **the vendor that watches the building is the vendor the building bought for the community, and the desk that approved both is the desk that assesses the risk of both.** Marc Dubois did nothing illegal. He did what a sponsor desk does. That is the point of the Commitment season: the characters decide what they are bound to.

Priya's F4 decision is the season's spine beat: she will not publish the notice. Maren will, with the true sentence. They disagree in public, on their own floor, and both stay.

> **PRIYA:** We don't announce an improvement we haven't made.
>
> **MAREN:** We announce the failure, then. In our own words, before someone else does it in theirs.
>
> **PRIYA:** With the vendor's name?
>
> **MAREN:** With *our* name. The vendor is Leila's problem.

What is **not** revealed in S4: who else reads the sensor estate. Nothing here says PRIM2. See §8.

### Season 5 — Scale — Floor 5 (Oksana, Leila, Amira) and the F6 decision

**S5E1 Shared Responsibility (gain scene):** Oksana and Leila build the "not our layer" exhibit from the case: infrastructure (B1), platform (vendor), application (community programme), and nobody's layer in between. Amira's ethics-review desk receives it as the worked example for the episode's canon human problem (Grace: "people waiting while departments debate"). **S5E2 Own the Exit (fit):** the case is on the table when Amira and Marc argue dependency and cost. Marc's position is honest: terminating the vendor means re-buying the building's eyes. Helen defers. Li Wei's canon "durable decision-rule artifact" is written that night, and the proposal is that its text is one sentence:

> *No single supplier holds both the building's senses and a community's line.*

**S5E3 Elastic (gain scene):** the first invoice for elastic labour (replacement crews for the community kits) arrives at Leila's desk. Oksana files it beside the case. Scale has a price and the price has a line item.

### Season 6 — Disillusionment — Level 7 and the world

**S6E1 Road Kit (fit):** the deployment Issue #19's loop ends on. Yonas dispatches from the mobility station; the replacement kit for the resource room goes out with the road crew. Per Diem's temporary labour is who carries it. Valeria signs the manifest. **S6E2 Trust Boundary (gain scene):** Aya signs out from the roof station for the patch rollout that finally changes the gateway firmware train; Yonas logs the departure. After the mission the resource room stays up through a whole night for the first time in six years. Nobody at HQ is awake to see it except the Coordinator, who writes it in the notebook.

**S6E3 (open):** no case beat is proposed for the protected slot.

### Season 7 — Succession — Floor 6 (Helen; PRIM2's interfaces)

**S7E1–S7E2:** no case beats; RAID and Mirror Room are Site 0.

**S7E3 Last Process (fit, canon epilogue):** the executive decision that was deferred in S5E2 is made. Helen signs the vendor-termination letter and, with Li's rule now institutional policy, splits the building's sensor estate from any community supply forever. It is the smallest act in the episode and the one that proves the institution can survive its own leader (Helen's canon end-state). The case closes where it opened: **Floor 1, Grace's desk**, the Coordinator across from her, the folder finally moving from the desk to the archive. Valeria, no longer a trainee, is the one who closes the ticket, and the system lets her choose *not resolved → resolved* because somebody, somewhere between S1 and S7, built the button.

> **GRACE:** It says resolved.
>
> **VALERIA:** It means it this time.

## 6. Waiting periods (the realism Issue #19 asked for)

The case is slow on purpose. Minimum gaps the writing must respect so the institution feels like an institution:

- S1 → S2: one academic year of the ticket auto-resolving. Reopen count passes thirty.
- S3 bench tag → S4 reveal: two seasons with the unit on the bench. Anyone can walk past it. Nobody picks it up.
- S4 reveal → S5 decision: a full season of review, exhibit and deferral.
- S5 deferral → S7 signature: two seasons. The letter exists, unsigned, in Helen's office from S5E2 on. It is a persisted object (`WORLD-MEMORY-CONTRACT.md` §2.1) and may be visible in any F6 scene from then until S7E3.

## 7. Technology accuracy gate (summary; detail at scene writing)

| Gate | Answer |
|---|---|
| Sensor / input | gateway heartbeat and link telemetry; building occupancy/metering sensors; ticket system state |
| Compute / reasoning | vendor firmware scheduling a nightly maintenance window that the community kits, unlike the building estate, cannot ride through on battery; ticket automation that treats "link up at poll time" as "resolved" |
| Energy | the community kits have no UPS; the building's sensors do. That is the whole technical difference, and it is a procurement difference |
| Access / authorisation | the supply contract; the sponsor desk's approval authority; Leila's read access to contracts after S3E1 |
| Human decision owner | Marc (approval), Helen (termination), Li (rule), Priya/Maren (disclosure) |
| Failure mode | automation declaring success on the wrong signal; one vendor, two risk profiles, one approver |
| Real concept taught | shared-responsibility boundaries; firmware trains and maintenance windows; procurement concentration risk; why "resolved" needs a human definition |
| Fictional extrapolation | none required. Everything in the case can happen with ordinary equipment |

## 8. What the spine deliberately does not say (anti-spoiler)

- It never says PRIM2 reads the sensor estate. Whether the dual-supply vendor was one of PRIM2's *permitted interfaces* (`CANON.md` §17) is an **owner option**, not a proposal. If the owner takes it, it is disclosed no earlier than S7E2 and only as reinterpretation, not as the case's solution.
- Jonas's §5 SECRET beat is written so that it is true either way.
- The approval signatures in this case are ordinary, blue, dated, human. The approval field that "resolves above the desk" belongs to the Miguel arc and is not borrowed here. The two stories rhyme; they do not share a tell.
- No real vendor, product, programme, city or client. Grant Voss is already canon.
