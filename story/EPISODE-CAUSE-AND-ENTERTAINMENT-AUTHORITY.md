# PRIM3 Episode Cause & Entertainment Authority

## Core law

Every episode must answer three different questions with three different storytelling tools:

- **Human story:** WHY does this mission matter and why must these people enter it now?
- **Music film:** WHAT doctrine, vocabulary, risks and expected plan should the team/player understand before entry?
- **Mission:** CAN the player apply that model when reality deviates from the plan?

PRIM3 is an ensemble. The person who owns the emotional story does not have to be Jordan, the mission commander, or the most mechanically active playable character.

Canonical loop:

```text
INCITING HUMAN PROBLEM
-> EPISODE OWNER / PERSONAL STAKE
-> DECISION TO DEPLOY
-> MUSIC-FILM OPERATIONAL BRIEF
-> HUMAN ESCALATION / MISSION THRESHOLD
-> PLAYER ENTERS CURRENT PRIM MISSION PERSPECTIVE
-> PLAN BREAKS / INFORMATION CHANGES
-> PLAYER ADAPTS THROUGH AVAILABLE R/E/T/DEFENSE/WILDCARD ROLES
-> OPERATIONAL RESULT
-> HUMAN CONSEQUENCE
-> CHARACTER / RELATIONSHIP RESIDUE
```

Do **not** write `PLAYER BECOMES PRIM3` for ordinary missions. `PRIM3` is Jordan Vale's eventual succession designation under the hidden `Primary Root Identity Mantle` meaning. Before succession, PLAY represents entry into the current PRIM operational perspective.

See `story/ENSEMBLE-POV-AUTHORITY.md`.

---

# 1. Mandatory causality and ownership

Every episode must explicitly define:

1. **Inciting event** — what changed?
2. **Episode owner** — whose belief, relationship, obligation or identity receives the deepest write?
3. **Secondary owner(s)** — who else materially changes?
4. **Jordan presence** — A/B/C/D/E/F per `ENSEMBLE-POV-AUTHORITY.md`.
5. **Human stake** — whose life, trust, reputation, relationship or responsibility is affected?
6. **Why now** — why can this not wait?
7. **Why PRIM / McCluster** — why this organization/team is involved.
8. **Mission lead** — who has operational authority?
9. **Primary playable POV(s)** — whose capabilities make the mission mechanically legible?
10. **Why these roles** — why R/E/T/Defense/Wildcard or other lanes matter.
11. **Expected plan** — what the team believes will happen before deployment.
12. **Hidden complication** — what is unknown, stale, false, withheld or misunderstood.
13. **Mission threshold** — the exact decision/event after which the team is committed.
14. **First plan failure** — where reality attacks the briefing model.
15. **Operational consequence** — what changes on partial, poor or excellent execution.
16. **Human residue** — what relationship, belief, secret, guilt, authority or trust state persists.
17. **Past-hint write** — if any Jordan/PRIM2/Hitman/prequel hint appears, what does it reveal without spoiling the later show?
18. **Next-character write** — whose future episode can inherit the residue?

If these cannot be answered, the episode is not story-complete.

---

# 2. Episode ownership is not Jordan ownership

Jordan Vale is a central spine character and eventual PRIM3, but the series is not required to route every episode through him.

Allowed Jordan states:

```text
A — Jordan-owned
B — shared owner
C — supporting
D — cameo / edge
E — mentioned only
F — absent
```

Across Episodes 02–18, the default authoring bias is that **most episodes primarily deepen other characters**, even where Jordan participates.

A strong recurring character should be capable of carrying an episode in which Jordan never enters the room.

Global Episode 01 / High Alert is a deliberate exception because it opens inside the future succession conflict and therefore foregrounds **PRIM2, Jordan and Royce**.

# 3. Music-film briefing law

The music film is a stylized operational briefing, not a literal slide deck.

It communicates:
- the song's technical concepts;
- expected operating environment;
- intended doctrine;
- current assumptions;
- role responsibilities;
- major hazards;
- emotional thesis.

It shows **how the operation is supposed to work under the team's current model** without exposing exact hidden causes or exact mission solutions.

> **The briefing teaches the model. The mission attacks the model.**

A song does not automatically belong emotionally to Jordan. Its performer, episode owner, mission lead and playable focus can be different characters when the story benefits.

---

# 4. Mission-threshold law

Every episode requires a visually memorable point where story becomes operation: a channel dies, an alarm escalates, evidence starts disappearing, physical controls fail, a service crosses a critical threshold, a second actor appears, authority is granted/revoked, or a person becomes trapped/in danger.

After the threshold, the player is inside the consequence.

---

# 5. Technical problem must hurt something human

A mission objective may not stop at `fix the server`.

Technical state must affect at least one meaningful human/story value:
- a person or population;
- a relationship;
- public safety;
- evidence/truth;
- reputation/trust;
- money/resources;
- an important service;
- command legitimacy;
- survival;
- a secret the characters care about.

The human story must likewise affect mission decisions rather than exist as detachable melodrama.

---

# 6. Character-choice-before-crisis law

At least one meaningful complication per episode should normally trace to human/institutional choice rather than random failure.

Useful causes include:
- hidden mistakes;
- excess scope;
- deferred maintenance;
- cost optimized over resilience;
- convenience over verification;
- refusal to escalate;
- actions taken without consent;
- mission success prioritized over people;
- bad incentives;
- siloed responsibility;
- PRIM2 deliberately withholding or shaping information;
- a character repeating an old psychological reflex;
- or a character choosing a new response that creates an unforeseen cost.

---

# 7. Relationship residue law

Every mission writes at least one durable relationship/story state.

Possible writes include:
- trust;
- authority;
- attraction;
- grief;
- resentment;
- guilt;
- debt;
- shared secrets;
- protection;
- imitation/mentorship;
- institutional belief;
- family pressure;
- outside-life consequence;
- increasing visibility of PRIM2's influence.

Use `story/CHARACTER-RELATIONSHIP-STATE.md` and each character's psychology dossier.

---

# 8. Mission variety law

Do not let every technical episode become `reach server room and use terminal`.

Across the campaign, rotate:
- public events;
- campuses/corporate spaces;
- logistics chains;
- weather emergencies;
- operations centers;
- endpoint labs;
- remote sites;
- abandoned facilities;
- smart buildings;
- multi-provider environments;
- international/distributed events;
- recovery sites;
- live control environments;
- Site 0;
- dual-perspective reconstructions;
- post-assault continuity spaces.

Rotate pressure as well:
- armed opposition;
- environmental hazards;
- crowds/civilians;
- stealth/detection;
- service degradation;
- disappearing evidence;
- command conflict;
- time/capacity shortage;
- misinformation;
- unknown second actors;
- fatigue/injury;
- infrastructure cascades;
- emotional conflict that changes technical judgment.

---

# 9. Kinetic entertainment law

Combat remains first-class and satisfying, but a fight should answer what opportunity or state the gunfire is buying or destroying.

Examples:
- suppression buys T a clean validation turn;
- overwatch protects evidence or a fragile LAB_NODE;
- an enemy is stopped before destroying a rack or removing evidence;
- explosive entry is avoided because it would destroy UPS/service state or endanger civilians;
- restored lighting/door control creates a flank;
- the player must choose between pursuit and preserving service;
- an injured operator forces abandonment of an optional data objective.

Combat cannot substitute for the technical objective.

---

# 10. WATCH vs PLAY

- WATCH mission/action remains roughly three authored minutes inside the ~9-minute episode.
- PLAY expands the same canonical event into roughly 15–30 minutes for normal missions.
- Capstones may be longer/checkpointed.
- WATCH may move between emotional viewpoints.
- PLAY may center whichever operator/role is mechanically appropriate.
- Mission lead, emotional owner and playable POV may differ.

Jordan's Wildcard role allows him to cross lanes **when he is present**. It does not require the player to control Jordan, nor does it erase specialists.

---

# 11. PRIM naming law inside episodes

Lower-clearance characters know `PRIM` as:

> **Program for Resilient Infrastructure Management**

Root/executive truth is:

> **Primary Root Identity Mantle**

Early episodes may naturally use the visible program meaning without revealing the hidden one.

The current hidden holder is **PRIM2**. Jordan becomes **PRIM3** only after succession.

Do not casually expose the numbering truth early.
