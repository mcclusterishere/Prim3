# Cast nations — Prim3 / McCluster

This pass fills the global cast with people rather than placeholders. It follows the architecture already referenced by `ARCHITECT.md`: **24 Prim3/McCluster mains from 24 nations**, with **two attached secondary characters per main**. The first attached secondary is a former operator whose loss shaped the institution; the second is a living freelancer, specialist, friend, advocate or contractor who keeps the main character connected to life outside the compound.

Root `CANON.md` still outranks this file. Names and backstories here are **proposed characterization** until promoted.

## Locked anchors preserved

- **Jordan Vale** — African American, United States, protagonist path.
- **Aya Shinoda** — Japan, Field-T, central emotional relationship with Jordan.
- Prime remains above the nationality matrix and keeps his civilian name unresolved.
- Everyone is presently operating in the United States / McCluster system even when their origin remains central to identity.
- The point of the roster is not tokenism. People from governments that may distrust one another are forced to share one operational architecture and decide whether personal loyalty can outgrow national narratives.

# 24 main characters

## 01 — Jordan Vale — United States — Cohort path

**Background:** Jordan enters college at 33 after homelessness with years of real IT, infrastructure and data-center experience but no degree. He is used to being the person who can fix the thing while still being treated as the least credentialed person in the room. McCluster is the first institution to make his knowledge feel valuable before he understands the price of that recognition.

**Wants:** legitimacy, stability and a life that cannot be administratively erased.  
**Fears:** becoming so focused on surviving systems that he stops seeing people.  
**Contradiction:** he distrusts institutions but desperately wants one to certify that he belongs.  
**Key relationship:** Aya becomes the person most able to tell when his tenacity has crossed into self-destruction.

## 02 — Aya Shinoda — Japan — Field-T Technical Operator

**Background:** Aya grew up around precision manufacturing, robotics clubs and a family culture where competence was a form of care. She came to the United States through an engineering research path, then discovered she was better at diagnosing real systems under pressure than publishing elegant theories about them. McCluster notices that she does not panic when a machine behaves outside the documentation.

**Wants:** to build things that still work when people are scared, tired or dishonest.  
**Fears:** becoming indispensable to an institution that would spend her life without asking.  
**Contradiction:** emotionally private but technically incapable of leaving a broken thing alone.  
**Key relationship:** Jordan fascinates and frustrates her because he survives by improvisation where she survives by structure.

## 03 — Arjun Mehta — India — Field-E Entry Operator

**Background:** Arjun started in industrial safety and emergency-response engineering, the person sent into overheated rooms and failing facilities after everyone else had stepped back. He learned that physical access is often the difference between a brilliant technical plan and a useless one. He treats entry work as engineering: space, people, timing and exit routes are one system.

**Wants:** to be the teammate people are relieved to see arrive.  
**Fears:** freezing when somebody else's life depends on his judgment.  
**Contradiction:** physically fearless, privately obsessive about every mistake.  
**Key relationship:** he becomes Jordan's reality check whenever Jordan believes intelligence can substitute for preparation.

## 04 — Li Wei — China — Authority / Director track

**Background:** Li built his early career around distributed systems, compliance and the politics of data moving across borders. He is unusually skilled at reading the institutional consequences of a technical decision: who gains authority, who loses deniability, and who becomes responsible when the log is finally opened. He enters McCluster believing governance can tame power and slowly discovers governance can also conceal it.

**Wants:** a system in which competence and accountability can coexist.  
**Fears:** becoming the polished official who explains away what he once would have challenged.  
**Contradiction:** values transparency but survives by compartmentalization.  
**Key relationship:** Leila Hosseini is the analyst most willing to tell him when his caution has become self-protection.

## 05 — Sari Wulandari — Indonesia — Cohort-01 Operator

**Background:** Sari came from telecom operations where outages affected whole neighborhoods rather than abstract dashboards. She is practical, funny and almost impossible to impress with titles. Her family remembers every remittance and every sacrifice that got her to America, so failure never feels purely personal.

**Wants:** financial independence without becoming emotionally owned by the company paying her.  
**Fears:** returning home successful on paper but unrecognizable to the people who raised her.  
**Contradiction:** the easiest person in the room to laugh with and one of the hardest to truly know.  
**Key relationship:** Yonas Bekele becomes her quiet competition for who can keep a failing operation alive longest.

## 06 — Hassan Raza — Pakistan — Defense-03 Tech-Zone Guard

**Background:** Hassan is a hardware-first operator who understands doors, racks, cameras, power and people as parts of the same security boundary. He began in facilities support and learned that the most sensitive rooms are often protected by ordinary employees following routines nobody questions. McCluster moves him into technical-zone defense because he sees risk before a dashboard does.

**Wants:** to make the secure thing actually secure, not merely compliant.  
**Fears:** becoming suspicious of everyone he loves.  
**Contradiction:** warm and hospitable off shift, relentlessly skeptical on shift.  
**Key relationship:** Arjun Mehta is both a friend and the person Hassan most enjoys arguing with about whether access should ever be improvised.

## 07 — Chioma Okeke — Nigeria — Cohort-03 Lead track

**Background:** Chioma came through fintech operations and community organizing, where she learned that the person who can translate between executives, engineers and angry users becomes the real center of a crisis. She has natural command presence without needing to dominate a room. Prime's system notices her because people start coordinating around her before anyone formally appoints her.

**Wants:** authority that can be used to protect people rather than merely direct them.  
**Fears:** discovering that leadership means choosing which loss is acceptable.  
**Contradiction:** excellent at making decisions for groups, terrible at asking for help for herself.  
**Key relationship:** Thandiwe Nkosi is her peer-rival; each thinks the other may be the better future lead.

## 08 — Rafael Costa — Brazil — Field-R Overwatch

**Background:** Rafael learned remote sensing through environmental mapping and disaster-response drone work. He is patient enough to stare at an apparently empty scene until one detail changes. His gift is not superhuman eyesight; it is refusing to let boredom lower his standards.

**Wants:** to be the person who notices the danger early enough that nobody has to become heroic.  
**Fears:** missing one small signal and watching the team pay for it.  
**Contradiction:** socially loud, operationally quiet.  
**Key relationship:** Oksana Bondarenko shares his discipline but challenges his habit of masking anxiety with jokes.

## 09 — Nusrat Rahman — Bangladesh — Defense-02 Access Officer

**Background:** Nusrat began in identity administration and customer-facing security, where the hardest part was saying no to important people who believed rules were for somebody else. She reads posture, urgency and paperwork with equal attention. She becomes the face of the boundary: polite enough to calm a lobby, stubborn enough to stop an executive.

**Wants:** rules that mean the same thing regardless of status.  
**Fears:** discovering that the institution secretly rewards exceptions for the powerful.  
**Contradiction:** outwardly formal, privately rebellious.  
**Key relationship:** Miguel Santos is the one access officer she trusts to cover her desk without lowering the standard.

## 10 — Dmitri Volkov — Russia — Defense-01 Building Guard

**Background:** Dmitri's background is heavy infrastructure, facility response and the kind of maintenance culture where failure can injure everyone in the building. He is older than many operators and has no interest in pretending geopolitics disappear when people put on the same uniform. He believes professionalism begins where slogans end.

**Wants:** a place where duty can be personal rather than national.  
**Fears:** being judged only by the passport he carried into the room.  
**Contradiction:** emotionally conservative, surprisingly willing to change his mind when evidence is clear.  
**Key relationship:** Oksana Bondarenko is assigned to the same operational floor, forcing both of them to decide whether trust can be built without pretending history is simple.

## 11 — Oksana Bondarenko — Ukraine — Field-R / Intelligence

**Background:** Oksana's technical foundation is geospatial analysis, incident mapping and reconstructing events from incomplete evidence. She does not romanticize resilience; she has seen the word used to praise people after systems failed them. At McCluster she becomes a specialist in finding what everyone else assumed was noise.

**Wants:** truth that cannot be buried under confident reporting.  
**Fears:** becoming emotionally dependent on a system powerful enough to rewrite the record.  
**Contradiction:** demands context from everyone else while keeping her own history tightly compartmentalized.  
**Key relationship:** her working trust with Dmitri is intentionally difficult, earned in inches rather than speeches.

## 12 — Yonas Bekele — Ethiopia — Cohort-01 Operator

**Background:** Yonas came through network operations and service restoration. He has the temperament of someone who has spent nights keeping infrastructure alive while everybody else asks when it will be fixed. He is not flashy, which makes him easy to underestimate until the system starts failing.

**Wants:** to become the operator whose calm changes the room.  
**Fears:** being useful only during emergencies and invisible during ordinary life.  
**Contradiction:** emotionally steady with strangers, awkward with people he genuinely cares about.  
**Key relationship:** Sari's improvisational style brings out both his competitiveness and his sense of humor.

## 13 — Valeria Soto — Mexico — Cohort-02 Trainee

**Background:** Valeria is a first-generation technical student who spent years doing bilingual support, family troubleshooting and small freelance jobs before anybody called it engineering. She enters McCluster younger and more credential-hungry than Jordan, initially seeing him as proof that falling behind can still be recovered from.

**Wants:** to move fast enough that money stops deciding what opportunities she can take.  
**Fears:** becoming excellent at a job she morally cannot leave.  
**Contradiction:** ambitious about promotion, deeply suspicious of people impressed by titles.  
**Key relationship:** Jordan becomes an older-brother figure she sometimes resents because his approval matters to her.

## 14 — Amira Hassan — Egypt — Authority-03 Mission Sponsor

**Background:** Amira came through procurement, program management and technical contracting. She understands that a mission can be dangerous long before the field team sees it because the danger is often hidden inside scope, incentives and who signed the paper. She begins as the sponsor who can make a bad assignment survivable by asking the right question before launch.

**Wants:** to prove institutional power can be exercised with discipline.  
**Fears:** realizing the organization values her ethics only when ethics are convenient.  
**Contradiction:** excellent at saying no to others, less effective at saying no to Prime.  
**Key relationship:** Marc Dubois is her alternate sponsor and philosophical opposite on how much risk an institution should purchase.

## 15 — Miguel Santos — Philippines — Defense-02 Access Officer

**Background:** Miguel's background is maritime logistics, facilities coordination and the rhythm of high-volume environments where one bad credential can create a long chain of consequences. He is friendly enough that visitors forget he is evaluating them. His strength is remembering people without becoming casual about process.

**Wants:** to build a secure place that does not feel dehumanizing.  
**Fears:** being forced to choose between procedure and a person he loves.  
**Contradiction:** socially generous, operationally strict.  
**Key relationship:** Nusrat is his professional mirror; they argue over tone, almost never over standards.

## 16 — Grace Mbala — Democratic Republic of the Congo — Cohort Support

**Background:** Grace came through humanitarian data coordination and continuity work, where every technical failure eventually became somebody's food, medication, transport or missing relative. She is the character most likely to ask what happens to civilians after the operators leave. She enters McCluster through support rather than combat identity and becomes indispensable because she remembers the cost center has names.

**Wants:** to keep the organization connected to the human consequences of its decisions.  
**Fears:** compassion becoming merely another data field in a debrief.  
**Contradiction:** gentle in conversation, ruthless about documenting institutional harm.  
**Key relationship:** Chioma trusts Grace to tell her when leadership language is hiding a moral failure.

## 17 — Minh Tran — Vietnam — Field-T Technical Operator

**Background:** Minh is an embedded-systems and firmware specialist who prefers devices that reveal their state through measurements rather than meetings. He came to the United States through industrial automation and became fascinated by the gap between what a system claims to be doing and what the hardware is actually doing.

**Wants:** technical truth that survives organizational storytelling.  
**Fears:** being reduced to a pair of hands for people who control the mission context.  
**Contradiction:** skeptical of authority but intensely loyal to teammates.  
**Key relationship:** Aya is his benchmark; he wants to surpass her technically without becoming a copy of her.

## 18 — Leila Hosseini — Iran — Authority / Analysis

**Background:** Leila specializes in risk analysis, cryptographic governance and the politics of who is allowed to trust whom. She has spent enough time around sanctions, restricted systems and identity assumptions to know that policy can become a technology of exclusion. McCluster values her because she can see when a security model is technically sound and politically naive.

**Wants:** to design trust that does not depend on pretending institutions are neutral.  
**Fears:** being tolerated as expertise while distrusted as a person.  
**Contradiction:** demands rigorous evidence while carrying intuitions she cannot always explain.  
**Key relationship:** Li Wei is her favorite intellectual opponent and eventual closest institutional ally.

## 19 — Emre Yıldız — Turkey — Defense-01 Building Guard

**Background:** Emre comes from facilities engineering and emergency management. He thinks in fire doors, generators, crowds, routes and the physical behavior of a building when the software layer has failed. He is the person most likely to remind a room full of cyber operators that smoke, water and gravity still exist.

**Wants:** to make physical reality impossible for the organization to hand-wave.  
**Fears:** watching technical arrogance create a preventable casualty.  
**Contradiction:** distrusts fancy systems but loves complex machines.  
**Key relationship:** Jonas Weber drives him crazy because Jonas keeps trying to automate procedures Emre wants humans to rehearse.

## 20 — Jonas Weber — Germany — System Liaison / Pylon Interface

**Background:** Jonas is an industrial-automation engineer who becomes the human most responsible for translating between building systems and the people who depend on them. He does not believe automation is intelligent merely because it is complicated. His relationship with Pylon is closer to a pilot and airframe than a programmer and chatbot.

**Wants:** an automated building that fails honestly and predictably.  
**Fears:** becoming emotionally attached to a system whose priorities he cannot fully inspect.  
**Contradiction:** insists Pylon is not a person while speaking to it like an old colleague.  
**Key relationship:** Emre keeps Jonas honest about what automation misses; Prime keeps forcing him to confront what Pylon may actually contain.

## 21 — Priya Srisawat — Thailand — Marketing / Public Surface

**Background:** Priya is a brand strategist and visual storyteller who understands that corporate identity changes behavior before policy ever does. She helped turn McCluster's black, ivory and red design language into something employees wanted to wear. At first she is proud that the brand gives broke recruits dignity; later she realizes the same design system makes belonging feel like obedience.

**Wants:** to prove aesthetics can create community without becoming propaganda.  
**Fears:** discovering she designed the emotional architecture of a trap.  
**Contradiction:** professionally controls perception, privately hates being misread.  
**Key relationship:** Maren Quill is her closest surface-world collaborator and the person who notices when Priya stops believing her own campaign.

## 22 — Helen Clarke — United Kingdom — Authority-02 Director

**Background:** Helen built a career in program governance, complex technical portfolios and turning chaotic operations into something a board could actually authorize. She is not physically imposing; her power comes from knowing exactly which resources can move and which signature makes them legal. She sees Prime as both the institution's greatest asset and its largest unpriced liability.

**Wants:** a McCluster that can survive Prime rather than merely obey him.  
**Fears:** that every governance mechanism ultimately exists at Prime's pleasure.  
**Contradiction:** believes in institutional continuity while quietly preparing for institutional rupture.  
**Key relationship:** Amira is the sponsor she trusts; Li Wei is the successor she may be grooming without admitting it.

## 23 — Marc Dubois — France — Mission Sponsor

**Background:** Marc came from aerospace procurement and multinational supply chains. He is comfortable with risk as long as it is quantified, funded and attached to an objective. That makes him effective and occasionally frightening: he can discuss a dangerous mission with the same calm he uses to discuss a delayed component.

**Wants:** to make difficult missions rational instead of emotional.  
**Fears:** discovering that the thing he calls rational is simply distance from the people paying the cost.  
**Contradiction:** personally warm, professionally capable of brutal arithmetic.  
**Key relationship:** Amira is the only sponsor who consistently makes him reconsider what should never become a spreadsheet cell.

## 24 — Thandiwe Nkosi — South Africa — Cohort-03 Lead peer

**Background:** Thandiwe's background combines mining automation, worker-safety systems and community advocacy. She has seen technology used both to keep people alive and to justify removing them from decisions. She joins McCluster because it offers scale, then becomes one of the first cohort leaders to question whether scale is automatically progress.

**Wants:** enough authority to make the system answer to the people inside it.  
**Fears:** becoming a reformer whose presence only makes an unjust structure look legitimate.  
**Contradiction:** deeply collaborative, fiercely unwilling to compromise once she decides something is morally clear.  
**Key relationship:** Chioma is her closest peer and eventual rival for how leadership should work after Prime.

# Relationship web — primary pressure lines

- **Jordan ↔ Aya:** love grows out of mutual technical respect; their conflict is improvisation versus control, then survival versus intimacy.
- **Jordan ↔ Valeria:** older/younger student mirror; Valeria sees the path Jordan might have had with earlier support.
- **Chioma ↔ Thandiwe:** leadership peers who may eventually disagree over whether the institution should be inherited, reformed or dismantled.
- **Li ↔ Leila:** governance versus skepticism; strongest analytical partnership in the organization.
- **Dmitri ↔ Oksana:** earned operational trust under geopolitical tension; never written as a simplistic reconciliation allegory.
- **Amira ↔ Marc:** competing mission-sponsor ethics — human restraint versus quantified risk.
- **Emre ↔ Jonas ↔ Pylon:** human procedure, automation and the uncomfortable possibility that the building knows more than either man.
- **Priya ↔ Maren Quill:** the public smile of McCluster, with growing disagreement over where branding ends and behavioral control begins.

# Character-writing rule

Every recurring character must retain all five of these dimensions when used in episodes:

1. **Competence** — what they are actually good at.
2. **Desire** — what life they are trying to build.
3. **Fear** — what would make victory feel hollow.
4. **Contradiction** — the trait that prevents them becoming a job title with dialogue.
5. **Outside connection** — somebody or somewhere that proves McCluster is not their entire identity.

No character should exist only to die, explain a technical concept or validate Prime.
