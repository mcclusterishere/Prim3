# Character Psychology & Decision Authority

## Purpose

PRIM3 characters must behave like people with accumulated histories, not scene-specific functions.

Every major recurring character should eventually have a writer-facing psychological/background dossier detailed enough that a writer, game designer or agent can answer:

- what this person notices first;
- what they fear;
- what they misread;
- what they protect;
- what kind of authority they respect;
- what kind of betrayal activates them;
- what they do under scarcity;
- what they do when they have too much power;
- what they conceal;
- what they are willing to sacrifice;
- and what earlier experience makes the present decision psychologically coherent.

The goal is not deterministic characters.

The goal is **earned behavior**.

---

# 1. Canonical causal chain

For consequential character behavior, use this causal chain:

```text
LIVED EVENT
    ↓
INTERPRETATION
    ↓
BELIEF / RULE LEARNED
    ↓
TRIGGER IN PRESENT SCENE
    ↓
DEFAULT REFLEX
    ↓
AVAILABLE ALTERNATIVES
    ↓
CHOICE
    ↓
CONSEQUENCE
    ↓
RELATIONSHIP / SELF-CONCEPT RESIDUE
    ↓
UPDATED FUTURE PRIOR
```

Do not skip directly from plot requirement to behavior.

If a writer cannot explain the chain, the scene needs more character work.

---

# 2. Required dossier fields

Every major recurring character dossier should eventually include:

## Identity
- name;
- aliases/callsigns;
- age by era;
- nationality/cultural context where relevant;
- visual identity by era;
- public identity versus hidden identity;
- what different groups believe they know about the person.

## Material history
- childhood/adolescent facts that matter;
- education;
- work;
- money/class experience;
- housing/stability history;
- criminal/operational history where story-relevant;
- major injuries/losses;
- formative relationships;
- major betrayals;
- promotions/succession events;
- moments of humiliation, rescue, abandonment or unexpected mercy.

## Psychological interpretation
For each major event, record:
- what objectively happened;
- what the character thinks happened;
- what lesson the character learned;
- whether that lesson is accurate, incomplete or maladaptive;
- what future triggers resemble the original event.

## Strengths
At minimum:
- professional/technical strength;
- interpersonal strength;
- survival strength;
- leadership strength;
- unusual perception pattern.

## Vulnerabilities
At minimum:
- blind spot;
- shame;
- fear;
- attachment vulnerability;
- anger/escalation trigger;
- authority conflict;
- scarcity behavior;
- overreach tendency;
- line they believe they will not cross.

## Power relationship
Record:
- whether they seek power, avoid it or distinguish capability from authority;
- what kind of power feels legitimate;
- what kind of power feels threatening;
- what they do when offered a chair;
- what they do when refusal would empower someone worse.

## Relationship map
For each core relationship:
- what this person wants from the other;
- what they refuse to ask directly;
- what they trust;
- what they hide;
- what could make them leave;
- what could make them stay;
- what past experience contaminates the relationship;
- what unique behavior the relationship unlocks.

# 3. Decision-tree representation

A character decision tree is a **behavioral prior**, not a hard script.

Use this pattern:

```text
TRIGGER
  ├─ character interprets as LOW THREAT
  │    └─ default response
  ├─ character interprets as PERSONAL THREAT
  │    └─ stronger response
  ├─ character interprets as BETRAYAL
  │    └─ historical reflex
  └─ character interprets as EXISTENTIAL THREAT
       ├─ old/self-destructive response
       └─ later/growth response
```

For important branches, annotate:

```text
HISTORICAL ANCHOR:
BELIEF ACTIVATED:
LIKELY FIRST MOVE:
ESCALATION RISK:
INHIBITORS:
WHO CAN INTERRUPT THIS REFLEX:
WHAT WOULD MAKE THEM CHANGE THEIR MIND:
RELATIONSHIP COST:
LONG-TERM RESIDUE:
```

---

# 4. Growth must modify priors, not erase history

Character development does not delete old reflexes.

A mature version of a character may:

1. notice the old reflex sooner;
2. delay acting on it;
3. ask another person for input;
4. distinguish current facts from past trauma;
5. choose a less destructive branch;
6. accept a cost that the younger version would have avoided;
7. or relapse under sufficient pressure.

Therefore later-season behavior should often be represented as competing branches:

```text
OLD REFLEX
vs.
NEWLY LEARNED RESPONSE
```

The dramatic question is which one wins **this time**.

---

# 5. Appearance and recognition continuity

Visual identity is part of character continuity.

For major appearance changes, record:

- before-state;
- after-state;
- why the character changes it;
- whether the change is deliberate reinvention, necessity, fashion, concealment or grief;
- who recognizes them immediately;
- who fails to recognize them;
- which photos/records become misleading;
- and whether fictional institutional systems gain or lose confidence in identity continuity.

Appearance changes should affect how the world reacts to the character when that change is narratively meaningful.

Jordan Vale's long-hair-to-waves transition is the first canonical example. See `story/JORDAN-VALE-PSYCHOLOGICAL-BACKGROUND.md`.

---

# 6. Background truth versus audience disclosure

Every dossier must distinguish:

```text
WRITER TRUTH
what actually happened / what writers know

CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE
what this character knows at this point in chronology

OTHER-CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE
what specific people know or believe

AUDIENCE KNOWLEDGE
what has been revealed in the current show/release order
```

These are not interchangeable.

A fact being canon does **not** mean a character can reference it.

A fact being in a psychological dossier does **not** mean PRIM3 should reveal it.

For reverse-reveal franchises especially, use:

> **Background truth is not disclosure permission.**

---

# 7. Scene-writing test

Before locking a consequential scene, answer:

1. Who wants what from whom?
2. What past event is the current moment unconsciously or consciously resembling?
3. What does the character believe is at risk?
4. What is their first reflex?
5. What would the younger version of this character do?
6. What has the character learned since then?
7. Who in the room can alter their decision?
8. What happens if nobody interrupts the reflex?
9. What choice is made?
10. What residue survives into the next episode?

If the scene could be given unchanged to another character, it is probably not psychologically specific enough.

---

# 8. Game / branching-dialogue use

For playable branches, behavior should not become arbitrary morality-menu writing.

Choice options should emerge from believable internal strategies such as:

- confront;
- verify;
- conceal;
- withdraw;
- negotiate;
- escalate;
- protect;
- delegate;
- retaliate;
- trust;
- compartmentalize;
- seek authority;
- bypass authority;
- sacrifice resource;
- sacrifice reputation;
- accept vulnerability.

Different characters should weight these strategies differently based on history.

Branching outcomes may change relationships and operational details without violating locked story spine.

---

# 9. Psychological continuity ledger

After every major episode, update at least the following when changed:

```text
TRUST:
FEAR:
SHAME:
GRIEF:
ANGER:
LOYALTY:
DEBT:
ROMANTIC ATTACHMENT:
INSTITUTIONAL TRUST:
SELF-CONCEPT:
POWER APPETITE / POWER AVERSION:
KNOWN SECRETS:
BELIEVED SECRETS:
ACTIVE THREATS:
NEWLY LEARNED RULE:
OLD RULE CHALLENGED:
```

Relationships do not reset because a new episode begins.

---

# 10. Jordan Vale as the reference implementation

`story/JORDAN-VALE-PSYCHOLOGICAL-BACKGROUND.md` is the first full implementation of this authority.

When building Aya, Chioma, Royce, Bigga Lex, Presiding Prime and the rest of the recurring cast, aim for comparable causal depth rather than copying Jordan's personality.

Jordan's dossier demonstrates the desired principle:

> **A decision becomes interesting when the audience can later discover that the character had twenty years of reasons for making it.**
