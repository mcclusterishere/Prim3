# PRIM3 Scene Standard

Every scene must perform human work as well as plot work.

## Required scene fields

- POV / focal character
- location and time
- immediate objective
- opposing objective
- relationship state entering scene
- information revealed or withheld
- technical concept, if any
- emotional turn
- relationship state leaving scene
- consequence that survives the scene

## Ordinary-life requirement

Not every scene is an operation. The series needs enough normal life that danger has something to destroy.

Recurring civilian and institutional spaces should include classrooms, labs, hallways, transit, cheap meals, dorm/apartment spaces, workstations, locker rooms, corporate elevators, break rooms, group chats, parking lots, stores, family calls and late-night study sessions.

## Dialogue

Dialogue should contain interruption, avoidance, subtext, misinterpretation and individual rhythm. Characters are allowed to be funny, petty, tired, horny, jealous, broke, proud, distracted, generous, defensive and wrong.

Do not make every character speak like an operations manual.

## Technical exposition

Technical language is allowed when the speaker would naturally use it. Another character may misunderstand it, challenge it, simplify it, mock it or weaponize it. Exposition should create status shifts inside the room.

## Mission aftermath

Every serious operation produces at least one non-technical aftermath scene. Show what the event did to schedules, bodies, relationships, confidence, finances or family life.
