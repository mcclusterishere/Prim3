# Relationship ecosystem (before the main story)

The series does not start with strangers in a logo.  
By High Alert / Jordan’s climb, the **24 mains** already carry an **ecosystem**: people who tried to get in, people who got in and died, people who got in and left, friendships that outlast badges, and the quiet math that **only about one in three** who reach the door ever stay company.

## Split of the 48 secondaries (locked)

| Bucket | Count | Status |
|--------|-------|--------|
| **Dead former members** | **24** | Were inside (building era or early cohort). Dead — op, raid approach, “accident,” dual-board tax, or leaving wrong. |
| **Living freelancers** | **24** | Were cohort / adjacent. **Alive.** No longer company. Freelance. May **pop into an episode** or help a mission **for friendship**, not payroll. |

Mains = the ~1/3 who held the seat.  
Dead + freelance = the other weight on every personality in the building.

---

## What this does for the 24 mains

Each main is not only a nation + job. They are:

- Someone who **survived selection** when two others on their line didn’t stay  
- Someone who still answers (or avoids) a **freelancer** who knew them before the M felt heavy  
- Someone who still sees a **dead name** when a corridor or a tool is used  

That pre-history is why Aya is precise, why Jordan hungers for paid-in-full, why Volkov and Bondarenko can share a floor without performing a news cycle, why Chioma leads like someone who buried a peer.

---

## Selection math (in-world)

When the organization was building:

- Many approached (smile floor, subsidiaries, field tryouts, Hitman-adjacent pipelines Prime also touched)  
- **~1/3** locked as lasting company  
- **~1/3** left alive — skills intact, loyalty personal not corporate → **freelancers**  
- **~1/3** did not survive the path → **dead former**  

Numbers are moral texture, not a spreadsheet episode. The point: **every main knows the ratio in their bones.**

## Dead former (24) — how they mark the living

Each dead secondary is **attached to one main’s line** (see CAST-NATIONS pairings). Types:

| Type | Meaning for the main |
|------|----------------------|
| **Door dead** | Died on access/entry — main is stricter at thresholds |
| **Outer-site dead** | RAID map is personal |
| **Quiet dead** | Dual-board or “accident” — main trusts less upward |
| **Peer dead** | Main’s humor goes cold in one specific corridor |

On screen: a name on a memorial strip under the M; a tool nobody else reassigns; one line of dialogue, not a eulogy episode every time.

## Building-era snapshot (short history)

1. **Smile floor opens** — marketing real; equity and land already heavy.  
2. **First cohort waves** — many try; ratio asserts itself.  
3. **Outer compounds staffed** — defense and field learn the multi-site religion.  
4. **Deaths and exits cluster** — some years thin the roster; freelancers scatter into the real economy.  
5. **Mains stabilize** — 24 seats that hold. Relationships already dense.  
6. **Jordan enters late** relative to founding — inherits the web, doesn’t invent it.  
7. **Main story** — High Alert / climb / RAID — lands on people who already lost people.

## Story rule

Before inventing a new stranger for an episode, check:

1. Is there a **freelancer** who could appear for friendship?  
2. Is there a **dead name** that explains a main’s refusal or ritual?  
3. Does this beat **use the ecosystem** instead of resetting it?  

The interpersonal tree is **prior art**. The main story walks into a room that was already full.
