# Hitman jobs

These four jobs are crew history. They are not songs. They are not any of the 21 episodes. They do not fill Song #21. McCluster is not in the room for any of them.

They happen across Royce's reign, after he has the chair and before RAID. A later Hitman-owned episode may show one of them. It may not replace the episode's song, and it may not move the succession.

The eight countries in the room are the major ones: Jamaica, Nigeria, Haiti, the Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico, Ghana, Trinidad, and Cuba. The people still claim the city. The jobs are where that stops being a biography and starts costing something.

Shared rules after you have read all four, not before:

- Nobody says a parent's country on a job. Dez breaks this every time.
- The Hitman flag can be in the room. The FBA flag stays in a house. Putting it on a job is Dez's mistake, not a custom.
- After the Atlanta job, Sable schedules Royce out of noon sun. He hates the schedule. It stands.
- The eight do not meet each other's parents in order to settle an argument. When it happens here, it is already too late.

## 1. The hall on King Drive

**Where:** Bronzeville, Chicago. Diane and Earl Moon's event space.
**When:** Early in Royce's reign. Keys is already the person who can open a room. Dez is new enough to think being FBA out loud is the same as being useful.
**On it:** Keys, Dez, Sable, Rack. Grant is on the phone and wants his name on the program. He is not in the building.

An FBA family from the block is having a real repast. The dead man was a bus driver. His sisters booked the hall the ordinary way. Hitman needs the office behind the kitchen for twenty minutes while the food is being walked out, because a payment has to change hands where Grant's club friends are not watching. The repast is not a costume. If the sisters feel a second event happening inside their goodnight, Keys has burned her parents' room.

Dez comes in with the flag talk already loaded. He sees Haitian gospel on a speaker in the office, which is Diane's, not the repast's, and he says the hall is a tether room that learned how to book Black American grief. He says it at a volume the dishwasher can hear. Diane is in the doorway with a stack of programs. She does not ask Kira to translate. She heard the word.

Keys can kill the twenty minutes by telling Sable the office is closed. She doesn't. She puts Dez at the sink with a tray and tells him the next thing out of his mouth is going to be a plate. Sable pays the hall as a customer, on an invoice, in the amount the repast was already going to cost the sisters. She takes Grant's name off the program before it is folded. Rack moves the payment and does not speak to the sisters at all, which is the most respectful thing he does all year.

**What it costs**

Diane knows the word now. She does not ban her daughter. She asks, once, whether the people Kira works for think the hall is a favor America did them. Keys says no. It is not a clean no.

Dez is off Keys's rooms. Not forever. Long enough that he has to ask Tone for a door and hear no from a second person.

Grant keeps the unprinted program because his name was on a thing and then wasn't. That is the kind of paper he thinks is power.

**Line that stays**

Keys, to Dez, at the sink: "My mother buried people in this room before you had a flag. Wash the tray."

## 2. Noon on the westside

**Where:** Collier Heights, Atlanta. July.
**When:** After the hall. Before the crew has a sun rule.
**On it:** Royce, Mina, Tone, Rack, Perch, Kiosk, Sable. Dez is told to stay in the car and does not.

A man Royce came up around tells a room that the chair does not stand on the block anymore. He does not say albino. He says "your boss can't take the day." In Royce's mouth that is the same sentence. He sets the answer for noon, outside, in front of his mother's street, because a car would look like he agreed.

Mina says no. A meet that exists to prove a body can stand in the sun is not a meet. Tone says put it in the car with the air on and let the man come to the window. Rack says the same thing in a complaint about the pavement. Royce hears all three and walks out at 12:10 in a hat and long sleeves like that fixes July.

Perch has already described the second man, the one who is not the argument. He is across the street by a cut-through, not part of the talking. Royce cannot see him. The light is too much and the distance is too far. Rack sees him because Rack is looking at the street instead of at the chair. Tone moves first. The talking half of the meet ends in the back seat with the air on. The second man leaves when he realizes nobody is performing for him. Nothing is won. A point is spent.

In the car, Kiosk says Royce is allergic to the same sun he claims. He says it because the quiet got thick and he is twenty-seven and bad at thick quiet. Sable tells him to sit down before Royce has to answer. Royce does not answer. He drinks water with his eyes shut and tells Mina to drive past his mother's house, not to it. Delores does not get this afternoon.

**What it costs**

Sable writes the next jobs in the morning or after four. Royce calls it babysitting one time. She does not argue. The schedule stands. He breaks it once, later, on a shorter door, and pays for it with a day he cannot read. The crew stops treating the hat as style.

Kiosk is embarrassed and also right. Those two facts sit in him and make him more careful with Rae and worse with everybody else for a month.

Mina stops believing Royce will choose the living people when the insult is about his body. She does not say this to him. Tone hears it in how she gives orders after.

**Line that stays**

Sable, to the car, not looking at him: "You proved you can stand there. You did not prove you can see. We are done."

## 3. The Bruckner hour

**Where:** Morrisania, the Bronx. Iris Rivera's building.
**When:** The first hard job after the sun rule. Dez is back on Mina's van because they are short, not because anybody forgave the hall.
**On it:** Tone, Perch, Mina, Dez. Keys is not in New York. Royce is not in New York. That is why Dez thinks he can talk.

A car has been sitting on Iris's block longer than a car sits there. Perch saw it from the El the day before and said he did not know yet, which is his way of saying it is real. Tone does not want the crew in his mother's stairwell. Mina comes anyway because she does not run a guess past a woman's door and then go home.

They are in the stairwell when Iris comes down with her cafeteria bag for a shift. Dez, who has been holding the joke since Chicago, says it to Tone where she can hear it. "Bronx-Bronx, or Ponce-Bronx?" He smiles like it is roster talk.

Iris stops. She looks at her son, not at Dez. Tone puts Dez out of the building. Not a speech. A hand on the jacket and the door. Dez stays on the sidewalk, which is the first adult thing he does, and also too late.

Perch was right about the car. The man is watching a different apartment, a cousin of a cousin who owes a number that is not Hitman's. He folds when Tone comes to the window as a son from the building and not as a show. Mina talks to him. The number gets paid out of Sable's quiet money later, because a debt on Iris's block is a debt on Tone, whether Tone claims it or not. The man leaves. The car does not come back.

**What it costs**

Dez is off the van until he apologizes to Iris. Not to Tone. Tone will not accept a sorry that skipped his mother. Dez calls her. He says the word was a joke. She tells him a joke has a room, and he picked hers. She tells him to go home to Cleveland and try the sentence on his own mother before he brings it back. He does not tell Shonda. The apology stays bad.

Perch and Tone are solid after this in a way they were not before. Perch said he did not know yet. Tone moved anyway. That is the trust.

Mina tells Royce what Dez said, because the chair let the habit live. Royce says Dez is young. Mina says Iris is not his mother to practice on. They do not resolve it.

**Line that stays**

Iris, on the phone, tired and exact: "You don't know me. Don't borrow my country to feel like a man."

## 4. Not your we

**Where:** The same night, in two rooms. Sable's fourplex in Third Ward. A donor breakfast in northwest D.C. that Sora is working. A Charlotte room Rae is already inside. None of these people can see each other.
**When:** Late enough that Grant thinks he has been useful long enough to say family words. Before anyone has said the name Sora to Rae, or the name Rae to Sora.
**On it:** Sable, Rack, Grant on a call. Sora, in D.C., hearing more than Grant thinks. Rae, in Charlotte, not hearing any of it. Priya is at the breakfast as herself, not as a handler. She does not know the call is happening.

Grant wants one introduction to look like one family. He has a man who will move money if the Houston side and the Los Angeles side sound like the same "we." He says Third Ward like he buried somebody there. Sable tells him he does not have a we over here and cuts his access to the number he thought was still open.

She is right and she is late. Rack already moved a case that morning because Grant's clock was ahead of Sable's yes. The case has to come back before it becomes a story in a room Sable does not control. Rack does it ugly and clean. No new people. No explanation to the man who was about to receive it. He eats the blame out loud, in the complaint voice, so nobody builds a myth about a shortage. Sable makes him whole out of money that was not Grant's. She tells him that in one sentence. He believes her, which is the only reason he is still there at the end of the year.

On the D.C. side of the same hour, Sora is standing where Grant put her, and she hears him say "Houston family" into his phone and then go cold when the phone goes dead. She writes it down later, in the notes she keeps because the briefing is always thin. She does not know the woman's name. She knows he lied about a family.

Rae is in Charlotte making a room feel chosen for a man who thinks Grant sent her. Grant did not. Keys did. The man mentions a girl in D.C. who "does the same thing cleaner." He does not have a name. Rae clocks it as competition and lets it go. She does not know she was described to herself.

**What it costs**

Grant cannot say Houston again. If he does, Sable does not take the call. He keeps the note anyway.

Rack trusts Sable more than he trusts the chair. That was already tending this way. This is the night it locks.

Sora's notes gain a lie with no name attached. That note is going to matter when she finally hears Third Ward from somebody who is allowed to say it.

Rae and Sora still have not met. The job's whole cruelty is that it was one night.

**Line that stays**

Sable, after the line is dead, to Rack: "He can be from Los Angeles all he wants. He does not get to be from here."

## What the crew believes after these four

Dez is loyal and untrained, and the four FBA flags do not make him the speaker.

Royce would rather go blind for an afternoon than let a man from Collier Heights say the chair hides indoors. The crew will not let him do it twice if they can stop it.

Keys will burn a personal door before she burns her mother's hall. She has not burned Dez. She has closed him.

Tone's building is not a site. Iris is not a prop. Perch understood that without a speech.

Grant is a client in his own head and a problem in Sable's book. He is still useful. He is no longer allowed to use the word we about a city he did not grow up in.

The eight still claim their cities. The jobs did not turn them into ambassadors for Kingston, Lagos, Ponce, Port-au-Prince, Port of Spain, Havana, Santo Domingo, or Accra. If anything they hold the cities harder, because the alternative was letting Dez say what they are.

McCluster hears none of this until somebody decides to tell him. Nobody in these four jobs decides that.
