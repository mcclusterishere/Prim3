# Character Personalization Standard

Use this standard to turn role labels into people who can carry scenes.

## Minimum personality depth

Every recurring character must answer the following in concrete terms:

### Life before the institution
- Where were they living?
- What did a normal Tuesday look like?
- What did they owe money for?
- Who called them when something went wrong?
- What possession would they hate to lose?
- What did they think success looked like before McCluster/Prim3?

### Human behavior
- What makes them laugh immediately?
- What embarrasses them?
- What makes them shut down?
- How do they behave when they are attracted to someone?
- How do they behave when they feel disrespected?
- What do they do when they are scared but cannot show it?
- What bad habit gets worse under stress?

### Voice
Give each character a distinct conversational rhythm. Define:
- sentence length,
- vocabulary level,
- profanity level,
- humor type,
- whether they answer questions directly,
- whether they talk more when nervous or less,
- what subjects make their voice change.

### Relationships
Every recurring character needs:
- one friendship with history,
- one professional dependency,
- one unresolved tension,
- one person they protect,
- one person they underestimate,
- one person who sees through them.

### Story pressure
Define:
- what they would lie about,
- what they would steal for,
- what they would fight for,
- what they would refuse to kill for,
- what could make them betray the institution,
- what could make them betray a friend,
- what they would regret surviving.

## Backstory rule

Backstory should create present behavior. Do not write biography that never affects a scene.

Bad: “She grew up in a strict family.”

Useful: “She learned that admitting uncertainty invited punishment, so on missions she hides confusion until the problem is already dangerous.”

## Character interaction rule

Two characters should never have only one relationship mode. Friends can compete. Rivals can protect each other. Lovers can disagree professionally. Mentors can envy students. Operators can dislike somebody they trust with their life.

## Death rule

A death is not valuable because it is surprising. It is valuable because it destroys routines, leaves unfinished conversations, creates logistical holes, changes how survivors behave, and forces somebody else to inherit responsibilities they did not want.

## Dialogue test

Remove the character names from a page of dialogue. If the reader cannot tell who is speaking from rhythm, priorities and attitude, the voices are not distinct enough.
