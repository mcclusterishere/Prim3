# Aya Shinoda — Supporting Character Relationship Web

## Purpose

This file records the people surrounding Aya whose histories materially shape her decisions. It is a relationship/knowledge authority, not a replacement for full individual dossiers.

The governing rule is:

> **No important relationship exists only to explain Aya. Every person in the web has their own incentives, limits and interpretation of her.**

# 2. Masato Shinoda — father

**Home:** Kariya, Aichi.

**Profession:** tool-and-die / production-equipment maintenance technician at a fictional automotive supplier.

**What he gives Aya:** the belief that care is anticipatory maintenance.

**Strength:** practical judgment; knows that maintainability matters as much as cleverness.

**Blind spot:** can communicate love almost entirely through service, which helps teach Aya to do the same.

**Relationship with Ren:** treats Ren's technical-school/manufacturing route as fully respectable and never frames Aya as the "successful" sibling and Ren as the ordinary one.

**Relationship with Aya after McCluster:** proud but wary. Masato understands what it means when an organization becomes dependent on one person who can always fix the machine.

**What he notices first about Aya:** fatigue disguised as preparation.

**What Aya hides from him:** how much danger her work actually contains.

---

# 3. Keiko Shinoda — mother

**Profession:** physical therapist in orthopedic rehabilitation.

**Core doctrine:** **The patient owns the goal.**

**What she gives Aya:** the language of autonomy, consent and human-centered success.

**Strength:** can distinguish assistance from control before Aya can.

**Blind spot:** because she is professionally calm around injury, Aya sometimes mistakes Keiko's steadiness for emotional distance.

**Relationship with Ren:** Keiko is the family member most determined that rehabilitation not turn Ren into a family project.

**Relationship with Aya:** she loves Aya fiercely but worries that Aya confuses being needed with being loved.

**What she notices first about Aya:** when Aya stops asking what somebody wants and starts telling them what is safest.

---

# 4. Tariq Al-Farsi — Saudi Arabia

**Home background:** Dhahran / Eastern Province, Saudi Arabia.

**Age:** close to Aya's age; old Yale-era engineering friend.

**Family context:** raised in a family where engineering, energy infrastructure, international education and technical careers are ordinary reference points rather than exotic ambitions. His extended family spans Saudi Arabia and Oman.

**Career:** electrical/control-systems engineer who later specializes in aviation systems, reliability and integration.

**Personality:** socially warmer than Aya, emotionally observant, technically serious without needing to dominate a technical conversation.

**Strength:** translates between people without pretending disagreement is dysfunction.

**Blind spot:** sometimes stays neutral too long because he values preserving relationships.

**Relationship with Aya:** one of the few friendships that survives disagreement without Aya being able to "fix" the disagreement.

**Relationship with Zayd:** maternal first cousins; close enough that Tariq hears both sides of Aya and Zayd's relationship.

**What Tariq knows:** Aya's tendency to turn care into project management; Hana's importance to her; Aya's original suspicion of McCluster; that Aya's later defense of McCluster is a dramatic reversal.

**What Tariq does not know:** Hana's NIS role, the Living Root, Prime's involvement in GLASSHOUSE.

**Tariq's function in Aya's life:** he preserves a version of Aya that existed before McCluster became her identity.

**Canonical challenge line:**

> **"Are Jordan's decisions bad, or are they decisions you didn't make?"**

# 5. Zayd Al-Harthi — Oman

**Home:** Muscat, Oman.

**Age:** approximately Aya's age.

**Education:** Yale undergraduate architecture / built-environment track.

**Family relationship:** Tariq's maternal first cousin. Tariq's mother and Zayd's mother are sisters from an Omani Al-Harthi family.

**Background:** Zayd grows up around Muscat's mixture of contemporary development, family continuity and the practical politics of building in a hot, infrastructure-dependent environment. He is interested in how buildings organize movement, privacy, climate and social life.

**Personality:** thoughtful, aesthetically sensitive, less systemizing than Aya and more comfortable allowing unfinished personal questions to remain unfinished.

**Strength:** sees environments from the user's experience outward rather than from technical architecture inward.

**Blind spot:** can avoid direct confrontation until resentment has accumulated.

**Relationship with Aya:** approximately eighteen months. Genuine affection, no betrayal, no villain turn.

**Why it fails:** Aya responds to Zayd's uncertainty, stress and ambition by structuring more and more of his life. Zayd increasingly experiences love as supervision.

**Canonical wound:**

> **"I don't know if you love me or the version of me you're trying to build."**

**What Aya learns:** good intentions do not make control consensual.

**What Zayd learns:** being cared for by an extraordinary person can still make you smaller if you stop making your own choices.

**Status by PRIM3 opening:** alive, professionally independent, not pining for Aya, not hostile toward her. His ordinary continuing life is important because not every formative relationship ends in death or betrayal.

# 7. Helen Clarke — institutional gateway

**Role in this web:** Helen is the senior governance figure who recognizes that Aya's reconstruction of Hana's death is technically serious rather than merely grief.

Helen brings Aya into controlled audit/reconstruction work and becomes one of the first senior McCluster people to treat Aya's skepticism as useful.

**Why Aya initially trusts Helen:** Helen does not tell her "Hana's death was an accident, move on." She gives Aya access to enough legitimate material to test her own theory.

**Why Helen is dangerous without intending to be:** by giving Aya real agency, Helen makes McCluster feel more governable than its hidden architecture actually is.

**What Helen knows:** more than most about structural governance weaknesses, less than Prime about the actual Living Root / Hana intelligence story.

**What Helen does not know at first:** the extent to which Prime has intentionally shaped Aya's evidence environment.

**Relationship engine:** Aya sees Helen as evidence that responsible authority can exist inside a dangerous institution. Helen sees Aya as someone who might eventually make technical veto power institutionally real.

---

# 8. Amira Hassan — the one who says stop

**Role in GLASSHOUSE:** Amira opposes continuation while the safety state remains unverifiable.

**Aya's initial interpretation:** Amira becomes evidence that not everyone inside McCluster ignored Hana.

**Amira's guilt:** she was right and still failed to stop the operation.

That distinction shapes her future leadership:

> **Being the person who objected is not the same as having built a system where the objection could stop the action.**

**Relationship with Aya:** Aya respects Amira more than many sponsors because Amira was willing to say no before Hana died. Aya can also become angry with her because moral correctness without sufficient authority did not save Hana.

**Hidden irony:** neither Aya nor Amira initially understands that Prime possessed information that would have materially changed the decision.

---

# 9. Marc Dubois — the one who says continue

**Role in GLASSHOUSE:** authorizes tightly bounded continuation under competing human risks and incomplete information.

**Aya's relationship with Marc:** difficult from the beginning.

Aya does not think Marc wanted Hana dead.

That almost makes it worse.

Marc represents a worldview Aya finds frightening:

> **A decent person can make a lethal decision while believing the arithmetic justifies it.**

**Marc's guilt:** Hana becomes the one mission loss he cannot comfortably reduce to risk language.

**Post-GLASSHOUSE behavior:** he becomes more careful about technical uncertainty but does not abandon quantitative decision-making. His growth must not turn him into Aya.

**Relationship engine:** Aya forces Marc to name assumptions his models hide; Marc forces Aya to acknowledge that stopping a system can also hurt people.

---

# 10. Prime-00 — hidden architect of the web

Prime knows more about this cluster than anybody realizes.

He knows:

- Hana is NIS-linked;
- Hana is approaching the Living Root;
- Aya is emotionally central to Hana;
- Aya will probably investigate after Hana's death;
- Marc is likely to authorize bounded continuation given the evidence he sees;
- Amira is likely to object;
- Aya is likely to respond to institutional guilt by trying to improve the system rather than simply abandon it.

Prime does **not** manufacture every emotion or relationship.

He exploits existing traits and controls information boundaries.

His deepest manipulation of Aya is allowing her to experience enough genuine institutional good that her own epistemology gradually talks her out of her original suspicion.

# 12. Trigger map for Aya

**Weak/intermittent warning dismissed** → Ren accident memory.

**Safety state cannot be verified** → Ren + Hana.

**Somebody asks Aya to "trust the process" without exposing state** → immediate resistance.

**Person she loves makes a choice Aya considers unsafe** → Ren/Zayd control reflex.

**Institution says she is indispensable** → pride + exploitation fear.

**Jordan notices undocumented authority seam** → Hana memory, initially suppressed by two years of failed investigation.

**Marc uses quantified risk language** → GLASSHOUSE anger.

**Amira objects but lacks stopping authority** → respect mixed with frustration.

**Helen gives Aya real authority** → institutional trust rises rapidly.

**Tariq questions whether Aya is controlling** → defensiveness followed by delayed reflection.

**Ren says no to help** → old reflex to argue; mature reflex to ask what he wants instead.

# 13. Real-world grounding notes

The cultural/professional texture for Tariq is grounded in Dhahran/Eastern Province's real engineering and international-education ecosystem; Saudi Aramco has operated education/training programs in Dhahran for decades and continues to sponsor students in engineering and technical fields.

Zayd's architectural background is fictional but consistent with Oman having established civil/architectural engineering institutions and a strong built-environment profession.

These real contexts are grounding only. Tariq, Zayd and their family are fictional and are not representations of any real family, Aramco employee or university student.
