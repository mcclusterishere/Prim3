# Hitman — where they are actually from

The twelve contemporary Hitman people each come from a different state. No repeats. No other states.

The list is the twelve states with the largest **Black alone** populations in the U.S. Census Bureau Vintage 2023 estimates. That is headcount, not percentage. Mississippi has the highest share of Black residents and is not on this list. Texas is, because more Black people live there than in any other state.

```text
1  Texas
2  Florida
3  Georgia
4  New York
5  California
6  North Carolina
7  Maryland
8  Illinois
9  Virginia
10 Pennsylvania
11 Ohio
12 Louisiana
```

Hitman is a national crew, not a hometown gang. They did not grow up on the same block. They know each other's states the way coworkers know each other's people.

## Lineage split

Four of the twelve are Foundational Black Americans. Eight are second-generation diaspora. That is the third / two-thirds split. Nobody in this crew is fresh off a plane. The eight were born in the cities they name. Their parents are the ones who migrated. The eight claim the city and the state. They do not lead with the country. The four do not care. They call the eight **tethers** anyway.

In the real argument this crew is copying, a tether is the insult FBA people use for African and Caribbean immigrants, and for their American-born kids, when they think those people attached themselves to Black American culture, neighborhoods, and struggle while keeping another country in the house. Some voices use it only for somebody who cashes the culture and then talks down on Black Americans. Inside Hitman, Dez uses the wide version. Anybody whose parents had a different flag can catch it. Sable uses the narrow version, and she does not say it often. Mina will shut Dez up if he says it in her van. Royce lets it ride.

The whole twelve fly the Hitman flag. That loyalty is real on both sides of the split. The four also fly an FBA flag, and they mean it.

McCluster's own earlier geography stays what it already is: upstate New York for the first college, Bridgeport for the Vibes lot. Bridgeport is not one of these twelve homes. Bigga Lex is not one of these twelve seats. His home is still tied to growing up with McCluster and is not assigned here.

Ages below are at the opening of PRIM3, when McCluster is 33 and Royce is 26.

"Bringing people home," for Mina, means the crew she takes out comes back alive. It does not mean they live with her.

## Royce — Georgia — Atlanta

**Age 26. Home: Collier Heights, then wherever the chair is.**

He grew up in Collier Heights, in a house his mother still will not leave. The neighborhood was built so Black Atlanta could own something with a lawn. By the time Royce was sixteen the lawn was still there and the money had moved. His mother, **Delores**, works admitting at Grady and tells people her son is in "promotions." She is not stupid. She prefers the version where he is busy.

Royce is albino. Oculocutaneous. Pale skin, light hair, a hard squint in daylight, and vision bad enough that he does not drive at night and does not pretend he can read a block from a distance. Atlanta sun is not a mood. It is a problem with his skin. Delores has put him in long sleeves and a hat since he was small. He still does it. People who do not know him misread the face until he talks. The crew does not. He is Black, he is American, and he will not do a speech about either. He is also the only one. A second albino does not get added later to make a point.

The FBA flag in his spot sits next to the Hitman flag. His mother's is older than his. He does not joke with the tether word. He lets Dez do it, and he does not correct him in front of the eight. Mina notices that and does not forgive it just because he is the chair.

His father was not a mystery and not a lesson. **Calvin Royce** drove for a linen company, came home, and died of a stroke when Royce was fourteen. What Royce actually inherited was an uncle, **Marcus**, who ran a small Atlanta lane for Hitman and liked being a neighborhood man more than a king. Marcus let his nephew stand around too young. Royce learned the rooms, the debts, and the fact that Marcus would rather be loved on a block than feared in a city.

The rivalry with Benji was not a childhood war. It got serious at the end, when Royce was 18 and 19 and already willing to make a succession bloody. McCluster saw that and still put the chair in his reach rather than make Lex's kids grow up without a father. Royce has never decided whether that was respect or the worst insult of his life. Both feel true on different days.

He hates Collier Heights gossip that the crown was given to him. He hates Buckhead rooms that think Atlanta power looks like their party. He bought his mother a car she did not want and got angry when she kept the old one as the daily. The new one sits under a cover. She drives it to church.

What he wants is for somebody who knew him at 19 to say he would have taken the chair even if McCluster had stayed. Nobody who was there can say that honestly. Sable will not lie to him about it.

## Sable Quinn — Texas — Houston

**Age 41. Home: Third Ward. The books live wherever the chair lives.**

Sable's father, **Ellis Quinn**, kept a one-room tax office on Almeda. Neighbors came to him when a letter from the state looked like a threat. She learned two things at that desk before she learned anything about Hitman: people lie hardest when they think the numbers are boring, and a correct number can keep a family in a house.

Her mother died when Sable was nineteen. Ellis lived another eleven years and never quite trusted a job he could not explain to a client. Sable's sister **Nia** is in Pearland with two kids and believes Sable does finance for a medical supplier. The tuition Sable pays is real. The supplier is not.

She was already on Hitman's books in Benji's last years. She is the reason a crew got paid when a charismatic man's story ran out of cash. One old ledger still has a payout that is not in Royce's name. She has not shown it to him. She has not destroyed it. It is not the whole of McCluster's life. It is enough to prove the chair did not start where Royce says it did.

She rents, on purpose, in a brick fourplex near her father's old corner. Owning a house would make her feel like she had bet her sister's kids on Royce's appetite. She eats too little when the week is bad and can recite a payroll from memory, which scares people who thought they were only behind by a little.

Ellis was American Black, Third Ward, and so is she. Houston is full of Nigerian families who have been in the city longer than Dez has been alive. Sable does not need a video to know the difference, and she does not say tether every time she hears a last name. She says it when somebody uses "we" about a block their parents did not bury anyone on. There is a small FBA flag inside the fourplex. She will not discuss it. The Hitman flag is the one people are allowed to see.

## Mina "Halo" Brooks — Louisiana — New Orleans

**Age 36. Home: New Orleans East.**

Mina is the oldest daughter of **Ruth Brooks**, a night-shift nurse, and **Louis Brooks**, who ran a refrigeration route until his back quit. There were four kids. Mina raised the younger two in the practical sense while Ruth slept and Louis was on the road. That is the actual origin of her leadership. Not a manual. A house where somebody had to notice who had eaten.

Hitman found her because she was already the person a shift would listen to when a supervisor was useless. She was working security at a hotel that flooded its own basement and blamed the staff. She got six people out of a bad order and lost one. His name was **Andre Polk**. He was twenty-four. He stayed below because a manager told him the equipment mattered and Mina had already started up the stairs. She went back. The water did not care that she was right.

"Halo" started as a joke in that hotel, because she would circle back for a headcount like she was taking attendance at a school. After Andre, younger operators started saying it like a prayer. She hates that. A prayer does not check the back of the van.

Her cell is small on purpose. **Dez** is the one she is trying not to turn into another Andre. **Tone** is the adult in the room who will tell her when her certainty is a performance. She sends money to her youngest brother, **Cedric**, who is trying to finish a refrigeration certificate their father never got to use. She does not tell Cedric what the money is from. Ruth suspects enough to stop asking direct questions and not enough to sleep.

When a file says Mina "brings people home," it means they come back alive. Her failure is the one who did not.

She is FBA, New Orleans East, and she flies both flags. Ruth does not love the FBA one in the window. It stays. Mina will call a man a tether if he spends a job explaining Black Americans to Black Americans. She will also end Dez's whole week if he says it about Perch while Perch is the reason they saw the car. Loyalty to the flag is not permission to get people killed.

## Tone Rivera — New York — the Bronx

**Age 34. Home: Morrisania.**

Tone's mother, **Iris Rivera**, was born in Ponce and came to the Bronx as a teenager. His father, **Luis**, was from Mayagüez. Luis died in a crash on the Bruckner when Tone was eleven. Iris works in a school cafeteria. Tone was born in Morrisania. If you ask him where he is from, he says the Bronx, and he is not starting a heritage conversation. The apartment is loud in a way Tone finds relaxing and other people find like an argument.

He got big early and the block decided what that meant. At sixteen he hurt a boy at a cookout because everybody was looking at him to be the one who would. The boy lived. Tone's uncle had to talk the family down. Tone still knows the boy's name, **Mikey Peña**, and still will not go to that family's building. The lesson he took is not a slogan. He learned that people will hand you a fight so they do not have to have it, and then they will call you violent for taking it.

He works on a 1984 Honda he keeps in a cousin's garage in Soundview. It runs. He is in no hurry to finish it, because finishing it would mean driving it somewhere, and somewhere is how men in his family leave. He sends Iris money and shows up for her appointments. She tells the church he does private security. That one is closer to true than Sable's sister's version.

He mentors Dez without calling it that. He is the person who can hear Perch's soft warning and move before Royce has turned it into a loyalty test. His fear is not dying. It is finding out the room only loved him when it was afraid.

## Kira "Keys" Moon — Illinois — Chicago

**Age 31. Home: Bronzeville. The rooms she works are usually somewhere else.**

Kira's parents, **Diane** and **Earl Moon**, came from Haiti through an older cousin's apartment in Flatbush, then Chicago. They own a tired event space on King Drive that has hosted repasts, baby showers, and two political fundraisers that did not pay the balance. Kira was born in Bronzeville. She says Chicago. She does not say Haiti unless she is on the phone with her mother. She grew up moving chairs and learning who was allowed to come in the front. The city taught her the rest: a list, a cousin at the door, a ride, a number that still works after midnight.

She was not recruited with a speech. She kept being the person who could get a Hitman face into a room that had already said no. At some point they started paying her for what she had been doing to keep her parents' lights on.

She knows everybody and lets very few of them know her. **Rae** is the exception she pretends is only professional. She saw Rae's talent before Rae had a name for it, and she is afraid of handing Rae a room the way she was handed rooms, with the cost left off the invitation. She keeps an exit list Royce has never seen: apartments, a cousin in Joliet, a cash amount that is not on Sable's official books because Sable would ask whose exit it is. It is Kira's. It is also, if it comes to it, Rae's.

Her fear is specific. If the list and the rides go away, she does not know whether Diane would still think she is clever or just a woman who used to know people.

## Omar "Rack" Dillon — Virginia — Portsmouth

**Age 38. Home: Portsmouth, in and out of the shipyard gates.**

Rack's father, **Samuel Dillon**, came from Trinidad, did twenty years at the yard, and came home missing the tip of two fingers and still on time. His mother, **Connie**, came with Samuel and drives a city bus. Rack was born in Portsmouth. He says Portsmouth. He does not do a Trinidad speech. The house smells like coffee and the kind of cleanliness that is a moral position. Rack was supposed to take the apprenticeship. He did, for three years. He was good at knowing what was in a cage, what was missing, and which supervisor would sign for a part that was not there yet.

Hitman did not need him to be brave. It needed him to be the man who could tell you, without romance, that the job did not have what the job required. Sable trusts him because his count matches the shelf. He trusts her because she does not dress a shortage up as a story.

He lives with **Alicia**, his partner, and her son **Malik**, who is nine and calls him by his name, not Dad, which Rack pretends does not bother him. Alicia is tired of "five more minutes." She thinks he manages warehouses for a freight company out of Norfolk. The freight company exists. It is not why he is late.

What he wants is his name on something that cannot be taken back into Royce's pocket. A yard. A cage. A building with a lock he paid for. He has not told Alicia the number he has saved, because the number would start a conversation about where it came from.

## Jules "Kiosk" Mendez — Florida — Orlando

**Age 27. Home: Pine Hills. The bench is in a strip-mall repair counter.**

Jules's mother, **Yolanda Mendez**, came from Cuba before he was born and has told him since he was small that he is too smart for the counter and too stubborn to prove it the way schools wanted. His father is not in the picture and Jules does not do a speech about it. Yolanda works front desk at a clinic. Jules was born in Pine Hills. He says Orlando. She still asks when he is going to get a real tech job. He has a folder of legitimate repair and small-contract work that would answer her. He does not show it, because showing it would mean he was ready to leave, and he is not sure the folder is a life or a costume.

He got into Hitman through Rack, who needed somebody that could make a dead handheld live without calling a company. Jules liked being underestimated in the store. The danger is that he also likes proving a loud man wrong, and that is how a careful technician becomes a person who says yes to a bad idea.

Rae is the only person he talks to without performing the genius. She knows he wants a shop with his name on the glass and no back room. He knows she wants a life where nobody is in love with a version of her she built for them. Neither of them has said the quiet part, which is that Hitman pays them to be exactly the thing they want to stop being.

## Rae "Twin" Colton — North Carolina — Charlotte

**Age 29. Home: the west side, and whatever room she is working.**

Rae's aunt **Pam Colton** raised her after her mother, **Tasha**, went to prison on a fraud charge Rae still will not summarize for other people. Tasha and Pam are from Kingston. They came over before Rae was born. Rae was born in Charlotte. She says the west side. Pam works at a beauty supply and at a church that thinks Rae does brand ambassador work. That is not entirely a lie. Rae can make a room feel chosen. She learned it in Charlotte, watching women get talked into things by men who were not as smart as the women, and then learning she could do it cleaner.

Kira brought her in. The first jobs were introductions, not cruelty. The talent turned out to be uglier than the invitations. Rae can feel the second a person wants to be seen as good, or dangerous, or desired, and she can hand them that picture. She is good enough that she does not know which of her friendships started as that. Jules is the test she gave herself. If she is plain with him and he stays, then at least one thing is real.

She does not know Sora exists as her pair. She thinks her opposite is just "those girls at the donor things," a class of woman Charlotte's new money produces. If she met Sora tomorrow she would clock the polish and miss the job.

Pam's youngest, **Deon**, is sixteen and thinks Rae is the successful one. Rae will not let a job touch him. That boundary is the most serious promise she has.

## Chris "Perch" Alvarez — Pennsylvania — Philadelphia

**Age 33. Home: Norris Square. He watches from roofs and from the El when the roofs are wrong.**

Perch's mother, **Lena Alvarez**, and his father, **Mateo**, are both from the Dominican Republic, Santiago and La Vega. They raised him in Norris Square. Mateo left when Perch was eight in the ordinary way: another city, occasional money, then not even that. Lena is a home health aide. Perch was born in that neighborhood. He says Philadelphia. He is quiet in a family that is not. They decided early that quiet meant he was judging them. He was mostly just watching.

He had a job with a small property photographer, shooting roofs and alley grades for insurance. He liked the part where you could see a block change before the block admitted it. Hitman used him first as a lookout and found out he would rather be wrong out loud than right too late. Once he warned too softly, a car sat on a corner an hour longer than it should have, and a man named **Daryl Witt** got hurt bad enough to leave the crew. Daryl lived. Perch still hears the version of the warning he should have given, which was shorter and ruder.

Tone is the person who does not make him perform certainty. Mina will hear him say "I don't know yet" without treating it as cowardice. He has a daughter, **Nia**, who is six and lives with her mother **Keisha** in Frankford. He is present. He is also gone enough that Keisha has stopped expecting him to become a different kind of reliable. He sends money and shows up to the doctor's appointments. He does not promise more than that.

He and Rafael have the same terror and have never met.

## Dez "Switch" Calhoun — Ohio — Cleveland

**Age 22. Home: Glenville, when he is home.**

Dez was fifteen when Benji left Hitman. He does not remember a man. He remembers older cousins talking about a wild predecessor the way a high school talks about a class that graduated before you got there. He has no idea that predecessor is a student somewhere, with his hair cut off.

His mother, **Shonda Calhoun**, works two front-desk jobs and is proud of him in a way that makes him feel like a fraud, because what she is proud of is that he is "into everything." Music, a little boxing, somebody's moving truck, a cousin's studio, a weekend of electrical help he learned off videos. Cleveland taught him that being useful in six directions is how you eat. It did not teach him which direction is his.

Tone treats him like a nephew and will embarrass him in front of Royce if Dez is about to do something only because the room is watching. Mina treats him like somebody she can still get home. Royce treats him like proof that loyalty is a resource. Dez says yes to all three and then cannot sleep, because yes is the only personality he is sure he has.

He has an LLC name written in his phone for a studio-and-repair idea. He has not filed it. Filing it would mean picking a city and a job. He is afraid that if he picks, Hitman will be disappointed, and he does not know who he is when Hitman is not asking.

Dez is FBA from Glenville and he is the one who says tether like it is punctuation. He learned the wide version online and uses it on anybody whose parents kept another flag in the house. He has not earned the lecture. Shonda is proud of him and does not know he is starting fights with people who are going to be the ones standing next to him when something goes wrong. His phone wallpaper is the FBA flag. He also has a real one. He is too proud of both.

## Grant Voss — California — Los Angeles

**Age 52. Home: Baldwin Hills. The office is wherever the respectable meeting is.**

Grant's parents, **Harold and Cecile Voss**, came from Lagos and bought in Baldwin Hills when they could finally afford the version of Los Angeles they had been promised. Harold became a dentist here. Cecile taught. Grant was born in Los Angeles. He says Baldwin Hills. He does not say Nigeria in a meeting, and he does not have the accent. He inherited the house, the posture, and a horror of looking like anybody's idea of a newcomer, which is its own kind of tell.

He made real money in commercial real estate and "strategic introductions." He did not start as a criminal. He started as a man who could get a meeting. Hitman, and later the thing above Hitman he does not understand, learned that a meeting is a weapon if the man holding it needs to stay clean. Grant still thinks he is the client. He uses words like exposure and special handling. He has never said the plain word for what he pays for.

He is divorced. His daughter **Lauren** is twenty-seven, works at a foundation, and loves him in the careful way of a child who has decided not to inventory the money. His humiliation fear is not abstract. He cannot stand the idea of Lauren reading his name next to a fact he had someone else "handle."

He keeps records on people he thinks he owns. Sable is not one of them, which annoys him. Sora is, in his mind, a gifted girl he can place in a room. He does not know she keeps notes because men like him withhold the reason. He does not know Rae exists as the other half of that placement.

## Sora "Gymnast" Okada — Maryland — Prince George's County

**Age 28. Home: Bowie. The rooms she is paid to enter are usually in D.C. or somewhere that wants a Maryland girl to look like she has always been there.**

Sora's mother, **Naomi Okada**, is Japanese American, from a family that had already been in the D.C. suburbs for a generation. Her father, **Curtis Brooks** — no relation to Mina — came from Accra as a young man. He coaches club gymnastics because he was a college walk-on who never got the roster and refused to let that be the end of it. Sora was born in Bowie. She says Bowie. She was in the gym before she cared what the gym was for. She was excellent. She was also, by sixteen, tired of being told she was "so articulate" in one room and "not a real Japanese girl" in another, depending on who needed her to be simpler. Dez adding tether on top of that is the fastest way to make her stop being polite.

She went to a state school on a partial athletic scholarship, finished, and started getting asked to stand in rooms: donor receptions, brand things, a political breakfast where nobody wanted her opinion and everybody wanted her posture. Priya met her that way and liked her for real. That friendship is not a con. The job underneath it is.

PRIM2's architecture uses her as the elite door and Rae as the street door. Sora does not know Rae. She knows Grant as a man who smiles too long and says "we'll find a place for you" as if she were a chair. She keeps private notes on what she was actually asked to notice, because the briefing is always thinner than the room. Her fear is that the gymnastics, the degree, and the manners were scouted, not respected.

She calls her father after hard weeks and lets him talk about a junior meet. It is the only conversation where nobody is using how she stands.

## How the joke actually goes

The eight claim the city. The four answer with the parents. That is the whole bit, and it is also the fight.

Dez, to Tone: "Bronx-Bronx, or Ponce-Bronx?"
Tone: "Keep talking and you can ask the Bruckner."

Dez, in the van, about Perch: "Tether in the back still clocking cars for us."
Mina: "He saw the car. You saw a video. Shut up."

Sable, when Grant says "we" about a Houston block: "You don't got a we over here."
Grant, later, not in front of her: "Born on La Brea. She can check the certificate."

Rae, when Dez will not drop it: "Charlotte is on my birth certificate. Kingston is on my mother's. You want to read both, or you want to be useful?"

Keys, if somebody says she only got in because Haiti sticks together: "I can get you in the room. I can also leave you on King Drive."

Kiosk, once, about the hat and the long sleeves in July, because he talks when he should not: "You allergic to the same sun you claim."
Sable, before Royce has to answer: "And you still work for him. Sit down."

What the eight will not do is fly the FBA flag to end the argument. What the four will not do is take the Hitman flag down to win it. Both of those are loyalty. Neither one fixes the other.

## What this file does not do

It does not move McCluster's childhood, the upstate college, or the Bridgeport lot.

It does not make Bigga Lex a thirteenth state.

It does not add a second albino. Royce is the only one.

It does not make the eight new arrivals. They were born in the cities they claim.

It does not make Pylon, Maren, or anyone at Equity Uprise part of this twelve. Maren still distrusts Grant's kind of money. Priya still knows Sora as a contractor and a friend.

It does not tell anybody how to commit a crime. The lives above are the reason these people stay in the room when the job is over.
