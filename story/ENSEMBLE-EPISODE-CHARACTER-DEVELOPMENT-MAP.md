# PRIM3 — Ensemble Episode Character Development Map

## Purpose

This file assigns the 21 canonical episodes to the people whose lives make the technical lesson matter.

It does **not** mean only the named owner appears. It means every episode knows:
- whose internal rule is challenged;
- which relationships change;
- who the player actually controls;
- what Jordan is doing or not doing;
- what PRIM2 learns/reveals through institutional gravity;
- what Hitman-side people gain or lose;
- and what residue must survive into later episodes.

Canonical law:

> **The episode belongs to the person who changes. The mission belongs to the people who were sent.**

Jordan remains the succession spine, but Episodes 02–18 are designed so the audience learns the world primarily by living inside other people.

# Season One — RECOGNITION

## 01 — HIGH ALERT / The Blip

**Owners:** PRIM2 + Jordan Vale + Royce  
**Jordan state:** scripted future principal  
**Playable state:** tightly authored future fragment; not normal campaign deployment  
**Character write:** the audience first sees Jordan as a man dangerous enough to reach Site 0, Royce as a parallel apex threat, and PRIM2 as the person who somehow knows both boards. None of the relationships are explained yet.  
**Ensemble function:** the 17 memory fragments promise that the story belongs to far more people than the three men in the room.  
**PRIM2 write:** competence, contempt, pressure and the habit of turning other people's histories into strategic information.  
**Royce write:** he is not a random end boss; his body language around Jordan carries personal history the audience cannot decode yet.  
**Residue:** question planted — *How the hell did these people get here?*

## 02 — WHITE GREY BLACK HAT / Permission Slip

**Primary owner:** Nusrat Rahman  
**Secondary owners:** Miguel Santos, Jordan Vale, Aya Shinoda  
**Jordan narrative state:** C — supporting  
**Base playable roster:** Nusrat + Miguel + Aya  
**Jordan PLAY state:** locked out; Jordan is part of the human problem, not the default pawn  
**Wildcard:** unavailable  
**Apex:** unavailable  
**Royce:** unavailable

**Nusrat development:** her belief that rules must apply equally meets the first real test: a promising new recruit whom powerful people clearly want admitted. She must decide whether Jordan receives normal scrutiny even when senior personnel are quietly interested in him. She does.  
**Miguel development:** shows how he can enforce the same rule without humiliating the person being stopped; establishes his human-security philosophy.  
**Jordan development:** realizes competence does not equal permission. His respect for Nusrat grows because she will tell him no without treating him like a criminal.  
**Aya development:** notices Jordan declaring ambiguity rather than exploiting it; first positive trust write.  
**PRIM2 trace:** an unexplained approval arrives only after the ordinary process is complete. First lesson: the hidden authority prefers observing choices before intervening.

**Residue:** Nusrat earns the audience's respect before Jordan does.

**Primary owners:** Oksana Bondarenko + Rafael Costa  
**Secondary owners:** Valeria Soto, Jordan Vale, Chris “Perch” Alvarez  
**Jordan narrative state:** D — edge/cameo until crisis  
**Base playable roster:** Oksana + Rafael + Valeria

**Wildcard:** eligible; this is the first clean Wildcard tutorial opportunity  
**Apex:** unavailable  
**Royce:** not eligible

# Season Seven — PRIME

## 19 — RAID (HOT SITE) / Hot Site

**Owners:** Jordan Vale + Royce + PRIM2  
**Secondary owners:** every member of the two assault elements through accumulated relationship debt  
**Jordan state:** scripted principal  
**Royce state:** scripted principal  
**PRIM2 state:** active defender

**Development:** all three men's theories of power become physical. Jordan's “another path,” Royce's tempo/force and PRIM2's information/authority architecture collide. Jordan and Royce nearly kill one another, then cooperate because continuing the rivalry protects PRIM2. Both assault teams are lost; the two old rivals become sole survivors and jointly defeat PRIM2 physically.

**Residue:** victory does not resolve authority.

## 20 — EVIL TWIN / Mirror Room

**Owners:** PRIM2 + Jordan + Royce  
**Secondary owners:** Aya/Hana, Helen, Li, Leila, Amira, Marc through the truths being reinterpreted

**PRIM2 development:** finally speaks from a position of physical defeat. Some admissions are true, some self-serving, some strategically timed.  
**Jordan development:** has to use information from a man he hates without surrendering judgment to him.  
**Royce development:** learns that even the Hitman sovereignty he inherited sat beneath another man's larger information advantage. The insult is existential.  
**Ensemble payoff:** prior character episodes become evidence rather than exposition; Hana's hidden investigation, GLASSHOUSE, Pylon and cross-board anomalies acquire new meaning.

**Residue:** Jordan/Royce keep PRIM2 alive because the living root is still needed for clean succession/revocation.

## 21 — GHOST IN THE WIRES / Last Process

**Primary owners:** Jordan Vale + PRIM2  
**Secondary owner:** Royce; Aya/Helen/Leila/Jonas/Pylon may participate through remote/technical authority where canon permits

**Jordan development:** must accept capability while refusing PRIM2's theory that seeing enough gives him the right to decide enough. Jordan becomes PRIM3 only after validated transfer.  
**PRIM2 development:** experiences the first true loss of sovereign agency: he may still observe/analyze/advise but cannot make the institution obey.  
**Royce development:** watches the man who once refused a smaller chair accept a far larger one and has every reason to distrust what comes next.  
**Aya development:** if present remotely, gets the first chance to design a system where even the new person she loves cannot simply become another hidden root without challenge.  
**Helen/Leila development:** governance stops being theory; they help define how power can be contested after succession.  
**Jonas/Pylon development:** validate that root-aware systems now resolve to PRIM3 rather than former PRIM2.

**Final thematic write:**

> **The world is not saved because Jordan gets the crown. The world gets another chance because the people around him have learned enough to argue with the crown.**

# Intervention-development rule

Calling Jordan or PRIM2 is also a character write.

Every use records:

```text
WHO REQUESTED HELP?
WHO RESENTED THE REQUEST?
WHO WAS SAVED?
WHOSE PLAN WAS PRESERVED?
WHO FELT REPLACED?
WHAT DID JORDAN LEARN FROM THE SPECIALIST?
WHAT DID PRIM2 LEARN ABOUT THE TEAM?
WHAT NEW DEBT / TRUST / SHAME / LOYALTY EXISTS AFTERWARD?
```

A Wildcard call is never narratively neutral.

A PRIM2 Apex request is even less neutral: every use should make the eventual revelation that he was personally observing the teams feel more intimate and disturbing.
