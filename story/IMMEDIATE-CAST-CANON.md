# PRIM3 Immediate Cast Canon

## Authority and scope

This file governs the people who recur in the immediate age-33-to-40 PRIM3 story.

Root `CANON.md` remains highest authority. Character-specific psychological dossiers override short summaries here where they are more specific and non-conflicting.

Core cast law:

> **PRIM3 is a recurring workplace/community ensemble, not an anthology that invents new support people whenever an episode needs texture.**

The same people work in the same institutional ecosystem for years. They should repeatedly cross paths in hallways, classes, briefings, meals, shifts, command rooms, missions, training, debriefs, arguments, parties, messages and crises.

Nationality is not a costume or substitute for characterization.

---

# 1. Jordan Vale — canonical Wildcard

Jordan Vale is the PRIM-side **Wildcard / Flex**.

He can cross operational seams because he repeatedly takes unowned work, learns beside specialists and combines R/E/T/Defense/logistics reasoning when the original division of labor fails.

Wildcard does **not** mean:
- magical omniskill;
- automatic authorization;
- being better than every specialist;
- owning every episode;
- being a default selectable unit in every mission.

Gameplay law:

> **Ordinary PLAY withholds Jordan from the normal selectable roster.**

Jordan enters only when:
- a specific authored mission scripts him from launch;
- an eligible CRITICAL mission receives a limited Wildcard call-up;
- or the story explicitly changes his availability.

Writer shorthand:

> **If a task falls between chairs, Jordan picks it up.**

Character risk:

> **Being able to cover everybody's job can turn into believing he is entitled to make everybody's decisions.**

# 5. Immediate recurring roster total

The default recurring immediate-story roster is therefore:

```text
24 PRIM / McCluster mains
+ 3 recurring public/system/apex presences
+ 12 contemporary Hitman figures
= 39 recurring immediate-story character slots
```

That is already more than enough population for a dense 21-episode series.

Canonical production rule:

> **Use the 39 before inventing anybody else.**

An episode needing seven meaningful presences should normally select seven people from this recurring ecosystem, not import five new guest characters.

# 7. Recurrence law

The same characters must repeatedly overlap across episodes.

For Episodes 02–18, default authoring should create three forms of recurrence:

### Workplace recurrence
People physically coexist because they work/study/train in the same institutional ecosystem.

Examples:
- the person from last episode appears in the next briefing;
- a previous mission owner is now support staff;
- two characters argue in the cafeteria about fallout from a mission they both witnessed;
- somebody covers another character's shift;
- a director walks through a technical lab and has a thirty-second scene with people whose episode this is not.

### Relationship recurrence
Established relationships continue even when they are not the episode's main subject.

Examples:
- Chioma and Thandiwe continue disagreeing after their command episode;
- Li and Leila keep correcting one another's reasoning;
- Dmitri and Oksana continue building trust in inches;
- Sari and Yonas keep competing;
- Aya and Minh's technical rivalry continues;
- Priya and Maren keep seeing the public version of internal decisions.

### Consequence recurrence
Earlier outcomes visibly affect later people.

Examples:
- a new access rule created by Nusrat appears three episodes later;
- Sari complains about a process added after Got Wifi;
- Helen cites an incident that the audience actually lived through;
- Pylon behavior changes because Jonas altered a control policy earlier;
- a Royce incursion leaves injuries, fear or procedure changes that persist.

# 8. Episode-density and micro-development law

Every ordinary episode should normally contain **7–12 meaningful recurring presences across WATCH + PLAY** when mission scale permits.

The default source order is:

```text
1–2 episode owners
4–8 additional people from the 39-person recurring roster
0–2 established historical/outside people only when specifically relevant
0–2 hidden/opposing/apex presences where story warrants
```

Do **not** interpret this as a requirement to invent guests.

A recurring character counts only if the episode gives them a persistent development beat.

Canonical test:

> **If a named recurring character appears, what is newly true about them when the episode ends?**

Valid small writes include:
- relationship movement;
- changed habit;
- competence gained/lost/revealed;
- status or workload change;
- secret learned/hidden;
- moral-line movement;
- material-life pressure;
- grief progression;
- future decision seed.

Seven silent bodies in a room do not count.

Full lifecycle authority: `story/CAST-LIFECYCLE-ATTRITION-PROMOTION-AUTHORITY.md`.

# 10. Historical-character restraint

Historical characters are strongest when recurrence makes them emotionally recognizable.

Hana works because Aya remembers her repeatedly.
Bigga Lex works because Jordan/Royce history points back to him repeatedly.
Ren works because he is Aya's living brother, not a one-scene prosthetic anecdote.
Tariq works because he remains Aya's real friend over years.

Therefore:

> **Prefer recurring historical relationships over one-use historical names.**

The audience should be able to remember who a dead mentor or outside friend is without consulting a wiki.

# 11. Immediate cast hierarchy is dynamic

### Tier A — multi-season spine
At minimum:
- Jordan;
- Aya;
- PRIM2;
- Royce;
- Chioma;
- plus whichever relationships become season-critical.

### Tier B — recurring operational ensemble
The remaining immediate roster.

Several Tier B characters should own episodes outright, and nearly all should recur outside the episode they own.

### Tier C — established outside/history web
Only people promoted by specific relationship/history authority or deliberately promoted later.

### Development pool
Unpromoted names in `CAST-NATIONS.md` remain available for future use but are not presumed to exist on-screen.

These tiers are **not permanent contracts**.

A support character can become a main through repeated usefulness, relationships, responsibility and audience attachment.
A main can drift outward, transfer, lose authority, become injured, leave, turn antagonistic or die.

Do not preserve screen prominence merely because an early spreadsheet once labeled somebody `main`.

# 12. Attrition, vacancies and replacements

The recurring roster is not guaranteed to remain at 39 living/active people.

Missions are dangerous.
Named characters may die or permanently leave when the story earns it.

A death is not followed automatically by a one-for-one replacement.

The vacancy may create:
- workload redistribution;
- understaffed missions;
- delayed operations;
- reluctant promotions;
- more dependence on Jordan's Wildcard function;
- conflict over whether/when to hire;
- a support character gradually absorbing the work.

If a replacement eventually arrives, they inherit the **vacancy**, not the dead character's personality or relationships.

A newcomer should already know some people and be unknown to others. Equal-distance introductions are unrealistic.

See `story/CAST-LIFECYCLE-ATTRITION-PROMOTION-AUTHORITY.md`.

# 13. Ensemble gameplay law

Normal missions launch with named recurring people assigned to the operation, **not Jordan by default**.

Recurring gameplay creates attachment:
- the player remembers Aya because they have controlled her more than once;
- Chioma's command choices matter because the player has worked under her before;
- losing Hassan would hurt because he has guarded several missions, not because a dossier says he is nice;
- calling Jordan matters because he is being called to save people the player already knows.

PRIM2 is the rarer second-stage intervention.
Royce is the opposing elite escalation.

Named characters should not feel like disposable tactical units. Loss matters precisely because the player has watched their ordinary lives and evolving relationships.
