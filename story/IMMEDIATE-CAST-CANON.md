# PRIM3 Immediate Cast Canon

## Authority and scope

This file governs the people who recur in the immediate age-33-to-40 PRIM3 story.

Root `CANON.md` remains highest authority. Character-specific psychological dossiers override short summaries here where they are more specific and non-conflicting.

Core cast law:

> **PRIM3 is a recurring workplace/community ensemble, not an anthology that invents new support people whenever an episode needs texture.**

The same people work in the same institutional ecosystem for years. They repeatedly cross paths in hallways, classes, briefings, meals, shifts, command rooms, missions, training, debriefs, arguments, parties, messages and crises.

Nationality is not a costume or substitute for characterization.

---

# 1. Jordan Vale — canonical Wildcard

Jordan Vale is the PRIM-side **Wildcard / Flex**.

He crosses operational seams because he repeatedly takes unowned work, learns beside specialists and combines R/E/T/Defense/logistics reasoning when the original division of labor fails.

Wildcard does **not** mean:
- magical omniskill;
- automatic authorization;
- being better than every specialist;
- owning every episode;
- being a default selectable unit in every mission.

Ordinary PLAY withholds Jordan from the normal selectable roster.

Jordan enters only when:
- a specifically authored mission scripts him from launch;
- an eligible CRITICAL mission receives a limited Wildcard call-up;
- or the story explicitly changes his availability.

> **If a task falls between chairs, Jordan picks it up.**

Character risk:

> **Being able to cover everybody's job can turn into believing he is entitled to make everybody's decisions.**

# 7. Recurrence law

The same characters repeatedly overlap through:

### Workplace recurrence
briefings, labs, halls, classes, food, transport, shifts, recovery, parties, paperwork, equipment handoff and post-mission cleanup.

### Relationship recurrence
established friendships, rivalries, flirtations, debts, mentorships and resentments keep moving even when neither person owns the episode.

### Consequence recurrence
policies, injuries, staffing gaps, missing people, workload, equipment, reputation and grief visibly carry forward.

Examples:
- Nusrat's access decision becomes somebody else's later process;
- Sari carries a backup habit after `Got Wifi`;
- Pylon's behavior changes because Jonas changed policy earlier;
- a Royce incursion leaves fear/injury/procedure changes;
- after a death, somebody covers the shift and starts becoming more important.

# 8. Episode-density / micro-development law

Every ordinary episode should normally contain:

> **at least 6–7 meaningful named presences, with 7–10 preferred**

when story scale permits.

Default source order:

```text
1–2 owners
3–6 additional recurring people from the immediate roster
0–2 recurring opposing-board people when relevant
0–2 established historical/outside people when specifically earned
```

Do not invent guests to hit the count.

A recurring character counts only if the episode changes or reveals something persistent about them.

Canonical test:

> **If a named recurring character appears, what is newly true about them when the episode ends?**

Valid micro-writes include relationship movement, changed habit, competence, status, workload, secrets, moral-line movement, material-life pressure, grief and future seeds.

Silent bodies do not count.

Full population authority: `story/EPISODE-CAST-DENSITY-SUPPORT-HISTORY-AUTHORITY.md`.

# 10. Cast hierarchy is dynamic

Tier labels describe current narrative prominence, not permanent contracts.

A support character can move:

```text
BACKGROUND RECURRING
-> SUPPORT
-> RELIABLE RECURRING
-> SECONDARY MAIN
-> EPISODE OWNER / MAIN
```

A main can move outward through transfer, loss of clearance, injury, burnout, political drift, resignation, antagonism or death.

Do not preserve prominence because an early planning file once called somebody `main`.

---

# 11. Attrition / vacancy / replacement law

Missions are dangerous. Named recurring people may die or permanently leave when earned.

Death is not followed automatically by one-for-one replacement.

Vacancy may create:
- workload redistribution;
- understaffing;
- delayed missions;
- reluctant promotions;
- cross-training;
- greater Jordan/Wildcard dependence;
- support-character rise;
- permanent capability loss.

If a replacement eventually arrives:

> **They inherit the vacancy, not the dead person's personality or relationships.**

They should already know some people and be unknown to others. Equal-distance introductions are unrealistic.

Full authority: `story/CAST-LIFECYCLE-ATTRITION-PROMOTION-AUTHORITY.md`.

---

# 12. Ensemble gameplay law

Normal missions launch with named recurring people assigned to the operation, not Jordan by default.

Recurring gameplay creates attachment. Losing someone hurts because the player has worked with them, watched them eat, argue, improve and become useful—not because a dossier says they matter.

PRIM2 is the rarer second-stage intervention. Royce is the opposing elite escalation.
