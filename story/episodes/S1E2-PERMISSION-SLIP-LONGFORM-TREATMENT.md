# S1E2 — PERMISSION SLIP

## What this episode must feel like

Episode 2 is the first time the audience lives inside PRIM/McCluster long enough to understand the place as a workplace rather than a mystery box.

It starts mundane.
It becomes inconvenient.
Then it becomes dangerous.

The episode's dramatic progression is:

```text
ordinary boundary
-> personal inconvenience
-> organizational pressure
-> field deployment
-> tactical uncertainty
-> hostile pressure
-> authorization crisis
-> defensible choice
-> aftermath
```

The technical concept and human concept are the same:

> **Knowing how to do something does not automatically give you the right to do it.**

---

# Scene 1 — “NOT YOUR FLOOR”
**Approx. 0:00–2:30**

Morning inside the McCluster/PRIM building.

The audience sees ordinary operation for the first time:
- people coming through access;
- Pylon calling elevators and access states;
- Priya moving between two unrelated onboarding preparations;
- Miguel already joking with familiar staff;
- Nusrat doing the least glamorous version of security work imaginable.

Priya tries to take equipment through a controlled door because the room she was scheduled to use for a photo setup changed at the last minute.

Her badge does not cover the new route.

She knows everybody involved.
The equipment is obviously hers.
The reason is legitimate.
She is late.

Nusrat still says no.

Priya:
> “You know where I'm going.”

Nusrat:
> “Yes.”

Priya:
> “You know why.”

Nusrat:
> “Yes.”

Priya:
> “Then what are we doing?”

Nusrat:
> “Waiting for somebody whose badge says yes.”

Miguel quietly enjoys this far too much.

Priya is irritated, but she waits.

This establishes that Nusrat's behavior with Jordan later is not selective suspicion.

**Character writes:**
- Nusrat = consistency before status.
- Priya = first taste of operational friction beneath public polish.
- Miguel = human ease around rigid process.
- Pylon = boring baseline.

# Scene 2 — “THE NEW GUY”
**Approx. 2:30–5:30**

Jordan arrives for formal onboarding.

This should be one of the first extended looks at age-33 Jordan after the future shock of Episode 1.

He does not enter like a superhero.

He is carrying too much stuff, asking practical questions, trying to understand where to put equipment, where food is, where he can charge things, what rooms stay open, and which rules are real versus ceremonial.

Chioma sees him before she speaks to him.

Aya is checking technical issue kits nearby.

Miguel greets Jordan casually.
Nusrat checks the manifest.

One diagnostic/security appliance is present physically but absent from Jordan's issuance record.

Jordan notices Nusrat stop on the line item.

He says before she accuses him:

> “Yeah, I don't think that one's supposed to be mine.”

That matters.

Aya examines it and confirms the device class, not ownership.

Jordan can explain what it is useful for.

Nusrat:
> “That's not the question.”

Jordan:
> “I know.”

Aya looks at him because she expected more resistance.

# Scene 3 — “CAN'T YOU JUST…”
**Approx. 5:30–8:00**

The small problem starts inconveniencing larger people.

Priya's onboarding schedule is now slipping.
Maren needs to know whether Jordan's delay is routine or something she has to explain.

Somebody proposes the obvious solution:

> Helen can authorize it.

Helen appears remotely or in person depending production blocking.

Nusrat explains:
- device class known;
- provenance unresolved;
- Jordan disclosed discrepancy;
- no evidence of misconduct;
- no authority to issue/use the appliance yet.

Helen:
> “Would you authorize him if I wasn't asking?”

Nusrat:
> “No.”

Helen:
> “Then I'm not asking.”

That exchange should land quietly.

Jordan hears it.

This is one of his first pieces of evidence that authority here can actually submit to procedure.

Aya offers to retain the device in technical custody until provenance is corrected.

Jordan gives Aya the most useful information he knows about what the device was likely intended for.

Then he stops.

Aya:
> “You're not going to argue?”

Jordan:
> “About what? She said I'm not cleared.”

Aya:
> “You seem like somebody who argues.”

Jordan:
> “I argue when I think I'm right.”

Nusrat, without looking up:
> “That's reassuring.”

Miguel laughs.

# Scene 4 — THE CALL
**Approx. 8:00–10:30**

The episode widens.

Chioma receives/joins an urgent mission call.

A regional cybercrime/task-force partner is executing a lawful seizure/search at **Harborline Edge Exchange**, a fictional multi-tenant edge/data facility.

The task force has physical authority and specified target assets.

The problem:
- the site is technically complex;
- unrelated tenant systems are nearby;
- the task force wants technical support that can identify and preserve the right assets without knocking unrelated customers offline;
- site conditions are changing faster than their warrant packet anticipated.

Chioma looks at Nusrat.

Nusrat thinks she is being asked to support.

Chioma:
> “You're leading.”

Nusrat:
> “Why?”

Chioma:
> “Because you just told an executive no.”

Nusrat:
> “That's the qualification?”

Chioma:
> “Today.”

Team:
- Nusrat;
- Miguel;
- Aya.

Jordan immediately asks if he is going.

Chioma:
> “No.”

Jordan:
> “Because of the device?”

Chioma:
> “Because you aren't field-cleared.”

Jordan absorbs it.

No tantrum.
No secret ride-along.

That reinforces the episode's rule.

# Scene 5 — WHITE / GREY / BLACK
**Approx. 10:30–14:00**

Music-film / mission-brief sequence.

`White Grey Black Hat` plays across preparation, transport and visualization of the mission model.

The song's categories are explicitly separated:

```text
HAT = intent / authorization relationship
BOX = prior system knowledge
```

The team is:

```text
WHITE HAT
BLACK BOX
```

They are authorized.
They do not know enough yet.

Visuals can show:
- task-force legal packet;
- specific asset identifiers;
- facility map with unknown technical layers;
- blank/uncertain nodes;
- scope boundaries;
- Aya receiving incomplete architecture information;
- Nusrat marking what the team may and may not do;
- Miguel reviewing physical routes/civilians/extraction.

During transport Miguel notices Nusrat silently rereading the same authority page.

Miguel:
> “You already know what it says.”

Nusrat:
> “I'm checking.”

Miguel:
> “No. You're checking if you deserve to be leading.”

She looks at him.

Miguel:
> “Different document.”

This is his first real friend/partner beat with her.

# Scene 6 — HARBORLINE
**Approx. 14:00–16:00**

They arrive.

The task force has gained partial control, not absolute control.

Facility staff are scared and annoyed.
Some customers are still active.
People want answers immediately.

The mission lead on the task-force side wants speed.

Nusrat wants exact boundaries.

He says some version of:
> “We have the paper.”

Nusrat:
> “For which room?”

He gestures toward the whole facility.

Nusrat waits.

He finally answers correctly.

That exchange establishes what kind of mission this is.

# Scene 7 — FIRST ENTRY
**Approx. 16:00–18:30**

PLAY begins in earnest.

Nusrat, Miguel and Aya move through the service corridor toward the target cage.

The player learns:
- physical Control;
- scope overlay;
- evidence identity;
- authorized vs out-of-scope zones;
- civilian/facility-staff movement.

Nothing is shooting yet.

This is tension through uncertainty.

Aya sees how much infrastructure is shared.

She does not like the confidence level in the task-force packet.

Miguel notices a facility employee trying to return to a work area for medication/phone/personal effects.

The player can handle them like an obstacle or like a person.

Miguel's better path is slower but stabilizes cooperation.

---

# Scene 8 — THE TARGET CAGE
**Approx. 18:30–20:30**

The team reaches the authorized tenant cage.

Several visually similar systems exist.

The task-force packet gives specific identifiers, but the physical rack state has changed since documentation.

The player must **verify identity before custody**.

Aya finds enough discrepancy to refuse immediate removal.

Task-force pressure rises.

Nusrat backs Aya.

This is the first time Aya experiences Nusrat's process not as bureaucratic friction but as protection from being forced to pretend uncertainty is confidence.

---

# Scene 9 — HOSTILE RECOVERY
**Approx. 20:30–23:00**

The environment changes.

A hostile recovery element tied to the target tenant enters/activates through another service path and attempts to recover/destroy specified evidence.

Now the XCOM grammar fully arrives:
- contested corridors;
- cover;
- evidence under threat;
- facility staff moving unpredictably;
- Miguel unable to defend every lane;
- Aya requiring protected technical time;
- Nusrat forced to lead rather than simply enforce policy.

The task-force side wants the team to grab the equipment and leave.

Aya warns that one target appears connected to something beyond the cage.

---

# Scene 10 — “DON'T PULL IT”
**Approx. 23:00–25:00**

The easy answer is now physically obvious:

Remove/isolate the target asset immediately before hostile recovery reaches it.

Aya:
> “Don't.”

Nusrat:
> “Why?”

Aya:
> “Because I don't know what else dies with it.”

Task-force pressure:
> “It's the target.”

Aya:
> “That isn't what I said.”

This is the episode's central field argument.

The target is authorized.
The action may be authorized.
The consequence is unknown.

The player has to protect the evidence while earning more information.

---

# Scene 11 — THE PERMISSION SLIP
**Approx. 25:00–27:30**

Aya determines that the shared cross-connect/service room can resolve the dependency question.

But that room is only `OBSERVE-ONLY` under the initial mission authority.

The player chooses:
- request a bounded extension;
- use a slower authorized alternative;
- invoke emergency authority only if life/safety has genuinely degraded;
- or knowingly violate scope.

During the request window, hostile pressure continues.

That matters mechanically.

Permission costs time.

The point is not that bureaucracy is good because it is slow.

The point is that **authority has to remain meaningful precisely when speed makes violating it tempting**.

Nusrat has to articulate exactly:
- what they know;
- what they do not know;
- what exact new observation they need;
- why they need it;
- what they still are not asking permission to do.

This is her command climax.

---

# Scene 12 — PROVE, THEN MOVE
**Approx. 27:30–29:30**

The team earns enough knowledge to take the correct bounded action.

The correct action is intentionally not the first obvious action.

Aya performs the change.

Nusrat verifies authority remained bounded.

Miguel keeps the technical space secure long enough for Aya to finish.

Then:

> `VERIFY`.

Only after validation does the evidence move.

Hostile recovery makes one last attempt during extraction.

The player has to choose between chasing a hostile and protecting the custody lane.

The game should reward understanding that the mission objective is not “win every fight.”

---

# Scene 13 — PARKING-LOT DEBRIEF
**Approx. 29:30–31:30**

Outside the facility.

No heroic applause.

People are tired.

A task-force person is irritated about Nusrat's delay.

Another points out that the delayed action prevented collateral service interruption and gave them a cleaner evidence package.

Nusrat does not look relieved.

Helen joins review remotely.

Helen:
> “Can you show why you stopped?”

Nusrat does.

Helen:
> “Can you show why you restarted?”

Nusrat does.

Helen:
> “Good.”

That is all.

It means more to Nusrat than praise would.

A facility employee thanks Miguel for actually explaining what was happening.

Miguel acts like it was nothing.

Aya watches him.

---

# Scene 14 — THE ONBOARDING THAT WASN'T
**Approx. 31:30–33:00**

Back at McCluster.

Priya's polished onboarding schedule is wrecked.

Jordan spent half the day waiting, learning the building and talking to whoever was around instead of receiving the planned introduction.

Priya thinks the day looks sloppy.

Maren disagrees with rewriting it into something cleaner than it was.

Priya:
> “Nobody needs the whole mess.”

Maren:
> “No. But we also don't need to pretend nothing ever takes longer than the slide deck.”

They find a middle ground.

This seeds a future communications conflict without making either woman wrong.

---

# Scene 15 — “NOW YOU'RE AUTHORIZED”
**Approx. 33:00–34:30**

The device mismatch is resolved.

It was an ordinary internal issuance error, not a conspiracy.

That is important: not every irregularity points to PRIM2.

Jordan receives the correct access state.

Nusrat hands/activates his credential.

Jordan:
> “So now you trust me?”

Nusrat:
> “No.”

Beat.

> “Now you're authorized.”

Miguel laughs.
Aya tries not to.

Jordan:
> “That's cold.”

Nusrat:
> “That's access control.”

Pylon announces the successful access state in exactly the same boring cadence used in Scene 1.

Jordan walks through.

---

# Scene 16 — ROOT VIEW
**Approx. 34:30–35:00**

Very short hidden coda.

Do not overexplain PRIM2.

The audience receives only enough to know somebody above the visible structure is evaluating more than mission grades.

PRIM2 already knows Jordan's historical identity.

The useful observation is behavioral:

```text
SUBJECT: JORDAN VALE
CAPABILITY: CONFIRMED / PREVIOUSLY KNOWN
BOUNDARY RESPONSE: RESTRAINT
UNAUTHORIZED OPPORTUNITY: DECLINED
```

Then another line — not Jordan:

```text
RAHMAN, NUSRAT
HIERARCHY PRESSURE: RESISTED
SCOPE DISCIPLINE: HIGH
```

PRIM2 is not just selecting Jordan.

He is studying the whole institution.

Cut.

# Episode-wide relationship writes

### Nusrat ↔ Miguel
Moves from competent coworkers toward genuine trust. Miguel becomes one of the few people who can tell when Nusrat is turning preparation into self-doubt.

### Nusrat ↔ Aya
Aya realizes access/governance discipline can protect technical truth rather than merely restrict it.

### Nusrat ↔ Jordan
Jordan respects her because she tells him no consistently rather than suspiciously.

### Aya ↔ Jordan
Aya's first positive trust marker: Jordan does not convert expertise into entitlement.

### Helen ↔ Nusrat
Helen signals that legitimate executive authority can strengthen subordinate authority rather than automatically override it.

### Priya ↔ Maren
First real philosophical difference about whether communications should reduce friction or reveal it honestly.

### Chioma ↔ Jordan
Chioma's first meaningful judgment of Jordan is about discipline, not talent.

# What Episode 3 inherits

Episode 3 should not reset the office.

Carry forward:
- Nusrat has now led a mission successfully;
- Miguel has a stronger internal reputation;
- Aya has a specific reason to trust Nusrat and a tiny reason to trust Jordan;
- Jordan remains not-yet-field-cleared;
- Priya/Maren's communications philosophy has begun diverging;
- Chioma has begun building behavioral models of the cohort;
- Pylon's normal behavior has been established;
- PRIM2 has started evaluating Nusrat as well as Jordan.

# Governing line

> **The episode starts with a badge reader and ends with the same question under fire: who has the right to do what, and what happens when the fastest answer is outside the line?**
