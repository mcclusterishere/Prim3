# Aya Shinoda — Real-World Research Grounding

## Purpose

This file records the external real-world research used to construct `story/AYA-SHINODA-PSYCHOLOGICAL-BACKGROUND.md`.

It is **not itself character canon where it describes real institutions, industries or technologies**. It exists so future writers can tell which parts of Aya's biography were grounded in real-world facts and which parts are fictional narrative choices.

Never treat a real company, school, city, laboratory or university named below as responsible for fictional events involving Aya, Ren, Hana, McCluster or PRIM3.

---

# 1. Why Kariya / Aichi works

Aichi Prefecture describes itself as a leading manufacturing center. Manufacturing accounts for a large share of prefectural GDP, and Aichi contains Japan's largest automotive cluster as well as a major concentration of robotics manufacturing and research.

This makes Kariya/Nishi-Mikawa a credible home for a family in which machinery, suppliers, maintenance, production engineering and technical career paths are part of ordinary life.

Source:
- Aichi Prefectural Government, **Industrial Capital of Japan** — https://www.pref.aichi.jp/ricchitsusho/en/industrial_capital/

Fiction derived from this grounding:
- the Shinoda family lives in Kariya;
- Masato works for a fictional automotive supplier;
- Aya works for fictional systems integrator Mikawa Motion Automation;
- Ren later works for fictional Tier-2 supplier Mikawa Motion Components.

No unsafe conduct is attributed to Toyota, Denso, Aisin or any other real Aichi manufacturer.

# 4. Why Ren's accident is physically plausible

OSHA materials on mechanical power presses document serious amputation hazards associated with point-of-operation exposure, safeguarding failures, control-system failures and unintended/double cycling. OSHA specifically notes that a control-reliable system should prevent a further stroke after certain component failures.

This supports the **hazard class** used in Ren's fictional accident: a system with unresolved/intermittent safeguarding or control-reliability concerns is kept in operation, then moves/cycles unexpectedly during a stoppage/line-clear event.

Sources:
- OSHA, **Hazards Associated with the Unintended (Double) Cycling of Mechanical Power Presses** — https://obis.osha.gov/dts/shib/shib020210.html
- OSHA, **Mechanical Power Presses** standard — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.217
- OSHA, **Amputations: Safeguarding Equipment and Protecting Employees from Amputations** — https://www.osha.gov/publications/bytopic/amputation

Important fiction/safety boundary:
- the exact machine, fault, plant and sequence are invented;
- the story should not provide a procedural recipe for defeating guards/interlocks;
- the narrative focus is the human/governance choice to tolerate unresolved safety uncertainty.

# 5. Why a powered transfemoral REN prosthesis is plausible

Georgia Tech's EPIC Lab describes powered knee-and-ankle prosthesis research using two motors, joint encoders, an inertial measurement unit, a six-degree-of-freedom load cell, sensor fusion and intent-recognition methods across walking modes including overground walking, ramps and stairs.

This establishes that Aya's fictional REN architecture uses real research categories rather than science-fiction vocabulary.

Source:
- Georgia Tech EPIC Lab, **Powered Knee & Ankle Prosthesis** — https://www.epic.gatech.edu/powered-knee-ankle-prosthesis/

Canon deliberately keeps clinical responsibilities separate:
- Aya designs/builds the mechatronics, sensing, actuation and embedded-control system;
- licensed prosthetic/rehabilitation clinicians handle medical fitting, socket/interface issues and supervised human testing;
- REN remains a research prototype, not instant commercial medical certification.

Yale Undergraduate Admissions explicitly accepts a **STEM Research Supplement** through which applicants can describe their own contribution and attach a paper, abstract or poster, with an option to invite a research mentor recommendation.

Yale also states that international applicants are evaluated through the same whole-person process, are considered without regard to ability to pay, and that Yale meets demonstrated financial need for admitted students.

This supports REN serving as Aya's defining application artifact without treating it as an automatic admissions guarantee.

Sources:
- Yale Undergraduate Admissions, **Supplementary Materials** — https://admissions.yale.edu/supplementary
- Yale Undergraduate Admissions, **International Students** — https://admissions.yale.edu/international
- Yale Undergraduate Admissions, **Affordable. For Everyone.** — https://admissions.yale.edu/affordability

Canon inference:
- Aya's combination of academics, industrial work, research documentation, REN and mentor recommendations forms the coherent application.
- Yale need-based aid makes attendance plausible for a skilled but non-wealthy international family.

# 10. Why Operation GLASSHOUSE uses fail-safe / graceful-degradation doctrine

NIST's Cyber-Physical Systems framework describes CPS resilience in terms including fault tolerance, graceful degradation and predefined fail-safe states. It also emphasizes that cyber components controlling physical systems can create safety consequences and that safety, reliability and cybersecurity must be considered together.

That research supports the fictional GLASSHOUSE conflict: contradictory cyber/physical state is not merely an IT problem when automated equipment can physically move around humans.

Source:
- NIST, **Framework for Cyber-Physical Systems: Volume 2, Working Group Reports (SP 1500-202)** — https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1500-202.pdf

Canon inference:
- Hana's stop recommendation and Aya's later doctrine are grounded in the principle that a safety-critical cyber-physical system may need to transition to a known fail-safe state when trustworthy resilience cannot be maintained.
- The exact McCluster incident, warehouse system and death are fictional.
