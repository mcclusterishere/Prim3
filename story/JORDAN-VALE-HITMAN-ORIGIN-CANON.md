# Jordan Vale — Hitman Origin Canon

## Authority

This file locks Jordan Vale's first two adult seven-year arcs and the identity relationship among **Jordan Vale**, **Benji**, **Bigga Lex the Jet**, and **Royce**.

Where older PRIM3 material treats Benji as a separate contemporary character, this file supersedes that interpretation.

**Benji is Jordan Vale's Hitman identity from age 19 through age 26.**

Where an older draft needs a separate present-day Hitman-side operator after Jordan has left Hitman, that structural role belongs to **Royce** unless later owner canon deliberately changes it.

The purpose of this history is character and continuity. Criminal events may be dramatized at a story level, but PRIM3 does not need to teach operational methods for committing crimes, evading law enforcement, or defeating real-world tracking systems.

---

# 1. Age 19 — first college attempt

Jordan enters college for the first time at **19 years old**, at a school in **upstate New York**.

He did **not** grow up as a drug dealer and does not arrive at college with an established drug operation. College is the first environment in which he begins selling drugs.

Jordan begins primarily with **marijuana**. His supply is connected to legitimate Native/tribal communities living on nearby reservations. The exact nation/reservation remains open until deliberately researched and selected so the story does not invent or misidentify a real tribal community.

Jordan also sells some of his own **prescribed Adderall**.

The operation grows quickly. Jordan becomes widely known on campus as one of the largest distributors and uses the money in part to pay his tuition and other expenses.

This is an important character distinction:

- Jordan is not following a childhood profession;
- he discovers an opportunity inside a system;
- he scales it rapidly;
- he learns that he is unusually good at finding demand, supply, customers and margins;
- and he begins using an illegal solution to solve a legitimate financial problem.

The experience teaches nineteen-year-old Jordan a dangerous lesson: **hustling works for him.**

---

# 2. Ricky and the dorm near-catch

Jordan's roommate is **Ricky**, a white student.

The defining near-catch of Jordan's first college period occurs after Ricky opens several pounds of marijuana inside their dorm room, becomes heavily intoxicated on promethazine, falls asleep and leaves the marijuana exposed.

The smell spreads through the entire dormitory building strongly enough to be noticeable from outside.

Police are called and begin moving through the building room by room.

Jordan receives a warning/call, rushes back to the dorm and finds Ricky still unconscious.

Jordan pours water on Ricky in an attempt to wake him. Ricky eventually wakes.

Jordan tells Ricky to put the marijuana into a backpack and leave the building while they clean the immediate room enough to reduce the obvious signs of what happened.

Ricky leaves carrying the backpack.

Police reach the room shortly afterward.

The officers allow Ricky to leave while directing their search and suspicion toward Jordan.

Jordan understands the racial asymmetry immediately: the white roommate carrying the contraband is treated as less suspicious while Jordan is treated as the likely offender.

**Racism and biased suspicion inadvertently help Jordan survive the incident.**

This event becomes one of Jordan's earliest lessons that institutions can be simultaneously unfair, irrational and exploitable without ever becoming trustworthy.

Crucially, Jordan does **not** stop selling afterward.

He recognizes how close he came to serious consequences and continues anyway.

That decision is owner-locked. Jordan's later removal from school cannot be written as one innocent mistake or one unlucky misunderstanding. He sees the danger, believes he can continue managing it and keeps going.

---

# 3. The dean's private investigation and exit

Jordan's spending and campus reputation increasingly attract the attention of the college dean.

Jordan is a broke nineteen-year-old student with no ordinary job that plausibly explains the amount of cash he is using to pay tuition and other expenses.

The dean becomes suspicious and begins conducting her own informal/background inquiry into what Jordan is doing.

She eventually obtains information from another student who informs on Jordan. The exact arrangement—whether the student is formally paid, rewarded, recruited, pressured or otherwise incentivized—remains open for later scene-level writing, but **a student informant is part of how the dean finally confirms what Jordan is doing.**

The dean confronts Jordan privately.

Instead of immediately turning the matter into a criminal case, she gives him an exit: **leave the college rather than force the situation onto a path that could put him in jail.**

Jordan accepts.

He does not create a public confrontation, force the school to humiliate him or gamble on continuing after the dean has given him a way out.

He **gracefully bows out**.

The exact college, dean identity, money totals and formal administrative paperwork remain open until deliberately written.

---

# 4. The lie Jordan tells his parents

Jordan does not tell his parents the real reason he left school.

He tells them that he **flunked out**.

To Jordan, academic failure is the easier shame to carry home than admitting:

- he sold marijuana and some prescribed Adderall at school;
- he became one of the campus's largest known distributors;
- the school discovered it;
- and he was privately given an opportunity to leave before the situation became criminal.

This becomes one of the earliest durable secrets in Jordan's adult life.

Later, when Jordan returns to college at 33, the audience should understand that this is not simply an older student attempting higher education for the first time. He is returning to a part of his life that ended fourteen years earlier under a lie his family may still not fully understand.

Jordan did not simply fail school the first time.

**He succeeded at the wrong enterprise while he was there.**

---

# 5. Why Jordan enters Hitman

Jordan does **not** leave college at 19 and immediately sign up to become a Hitman operator.

He enters Hitman through **culture, friendship and music**.

Jordan grew up with **Bigga Lex the Jet**, one of the Hitman operators who holds major authority inside the organization and Jordan's closest personal bridge into that world.

Jordan's initial reasons for being around Hitman are ordinary compared with what the organization later becomes for him:

- his best friend is there;
- his social circle overlaps with the crew;
- he wants to make music with people inside Hitman;
- the culture gives him belonging after leaving college;
- and Hitman initially feels like a crew/community before it feels like an operational institution.

Jordan does not join because he dreams of becoming an operator.

**Friendships turn him into one.**

---

# 6. The studio incident — friendship becomes operational loyalty

The first decisive bridge from music/culture into real Hitman danger happens at a studio.

Jordan is with Bigga Lex when another person comes into the studio and seriously challenges/threatens Lex.

Jordan is present when the situation becomes violent.

Jordan and Lex defend themselves together.

The event is a bonding point because Lex learns that Jordan does not disappear when a social situation becomes physically dangerous, and Jordan learns that loyalty to Lex can carry real consequences.

The studio incident is not a formal initiation ceremony.

It is more important than that.

It is the moment when **shared friendship becomes shared danger**.

Afterward, Lex knows Jordan is looking for legitimate work/money and also knows what Jordan is capable of when pressure becomes real.

---

# 7. The Vibes concert hustle

After leaving college, Jordan continues looking for ways to make money.

At a **Vibes concert in Bridgeport**, Jordan is selling parking spaces.

Bigga Lex approaches him with marijuana to sell as well.

Jordan recognizes the obvious economic opportunity: he can make money from the parking hustle and from marijuana sales during the same event.

The moment reinforces Jordan's belief that he is **born to hustle**—not in the sense that crime is his destiny, but in the sense that he instinctively sees overlapping revenue opportunities and knows how to move quickly when an opening appears.

This is an early version of the same systems/entrepreneurial instinct that later appears in Whip Equipped and PRIM3.

The underlying trait does not change.

The legitimacy of the system Jordan applies it to does.

---

# 8. Benji — the Hitman identity

Inside Hitman, Jordan becomes known as **Benji**.

Benji is not a separate person, clone, brother, rival or parallel protagonist.

**Benji = Jordan Vale during his Hitman years.**

The Hitman-era songs previously associated with Hitman Benji are therefore treated as Jordan's own earlier-life music/story material where their content remains compatible with later detailed canon.

The tonal difference between young Benji and 33-year-old Jordan is intentional. Jordan has lived another seven years after leaving Hitman before PRIM3 begins.

The Hitman arc covers approximately:

**AGE 19 -> AGE 26**

This is Jordan's first seven-year adult identity arc.

---

# 9. How Benji hardens

Benji does not begin the Hitman period believing himself to be a monster.

His moral deterioration develops through repeated retaliation.

His working logic becomes increasingly simple:

> I do not start with innocent people. But if somebody crosses me, what happens next is on them.

That philosophy lets Benji retain a sense that he is reacting rather than initiating while allowing his retaliation to become increasingly disproportionate.

One major hardening event involves a group of Nigerian scammers who attempt to scam Benji and misuse/steal his identity.

Benji responds with an elaborate retaliatory scheme involving deception, travel to **New Mexico**, his own associates, and the robbery of members of the opposing family/network. He later returns home and deliberately flaunts the proceeds in front of the people who originally targeted him.

The exact operational mechanics of the fraud/robbery are not required for PRIM3 canon and should not be developed as actionable instructions. The character fact is what matters:

**Benji escalates a fraud against him into a deliberately humiliating and materially destructive counterattack.**

By this point in the Hitman years, Benji can be savage, ruthless and apparently conscience-free toward people he has classified as enemies.

His internal justification is retaliatory rather than indiscriminate: he believes the world is turning him into the person required to survive it, and he repeatedly answers harm with greater harm.

That distinction does not excuse what he does. It explains how Jordan is able to become Benji without seeing himself as evil.

Over time, his greatest moral vulnerability becomes obvious:

**Benji himself decides who deserves mercy and who has forfeited it.**

That worldview later becomes an important mirror for Presiding Prime.

---

# 10. Bigga Lex the Jet

**Bigga Lex the Jet** is central to why Jordan stays in Hitman as long as he does.

Jordan grew up with him.

Jordan's loyalty to Hitman is therefore never purely loyalty to an organization. A major part of it is loyalty to his best friend.

Bigga Lex is already an operator and major authority figure within Hitman when Jordan enters the culture around it.

Over the seven-year period, Jordan/Benji becomes valuable enough inside the organization that Bigga Lex eventually views him as a legitimate successor.

Lex is also the kind of friend Jordan understands would come to his aid if Benji were ever in serious trouble.

That loyalty becomes important when Lex becomes a father.

---

# 11. Age 26 — Lex becomes a father and decides to leave

At approximately **26 years old**, Jordan reaches the end of his first seven-year adult arc.

The same year, **Bigga Lex's children are born**.

Fatherhood changes the risk calculation for Lex.

Lex decides to retire from Hitman.

He attempts to give **Benji/Jordan his title and position**.

This is the first major succession opportunity of Jordan's life.

Jordan refuses it.

He does not fight for the chair, negotiate for a larger share or accept leadership out of ego or obligation.

Two reasons make the answer immediately clear to him.

### 11.1 Jordan will not make Lex's children collateral to their friendship

Jordan knows that if he inherits Lex's role and later gets into serious trouble, Lex is the kind of man who would come back to help him.

Before Lex had children, Benji might have accepted that loyalty as part of their bond.

Once children exist, Jordan no longer considers that an acceptable risk.

He understands that a father should not be repeatedly pulled back into danger because his best friend chose to inherit the life he was trying to leave.

Jordan therefore refuses to become the reason Lex's children could lose their father.

This becomes an early version of a recurring Jordan problem: **when does protecting someone become making a life decision for them?**

At 26 the choice reads as mature restraint. Later relationships, especially Aya, will force Jordan to confront the limits of that instinct.

### 11.2 Royce would make the succession bloody anyway

Royce is one of Benji's biggest Hitman rivals.

Royce is **seven years younger than Benji**.

When Jordan is 26, Royce is approximately **19**—the same age Jordan was when his own first adult arc began.

Royce is nastier, more openly power-hungry and more willing to make the succession violent.

Benji understands that if he accepts Lex's position, Royce is likely to challenge him and may attempt to kill him for it.

Jordan does not want the organization badly enough to risk restarting a war over a chair he never truly wanted.

Between Lex's children and Royce's ambition, accepting the title is doubly not worth it.

Jordan **gracefully bows out of Hitman**.

---

# 12. Royce succession

Jordan does not leave Bigga Lex's position empty.

Instead, Benji/Jordan gives the opportunity to **Royce**.

This is simultaneously:

- pragmatic;
- self-preserving;
- protective of Lex's exit;
- a recognition that Royce wants the position more than Jordan does;
- and potentially one of the most consequential mistakes Jordan ever makes.

Royce therefore enters the long-form saga as much more than a late-game enemy.

He is:

- a genuine rival from Jordan's Hitman period;
- seven years younger than Jordan;
- the person who receives the position Jordan refused;
- the operator whose later trajectory can show what Jordan's life might have become if he had remained;
- and the natural present-day Hitman-side counterpart once the PRIM3 story reaches the dual-board conflict.

The succession creates a durable moral question for Jordan:

> Is refusing corrupt power enough if you knowingly hand that power to someone worse?

The exact early Jordan/Royce rivalry, Royce's family/background, whether Royce interprets Jordan's transfer as respect or insult, and how their relationship evolves after age 26 remain open for character-by-character development.

---

# 13. Why Jordan creates Whip Equipped

Jordan leaves Hitman at approximately 26 with a major realization.

He does **not** conclude that making money, hustling, scaling, buying, selling or building networks are themselves the problem.

He concludes that he wants **legitimacy**.

Jordan looks at the used-car business and recognizes a legal economic analogue to the part of drug dealing that had always appealed to him:

- find an undervalued or available asset;
- understand demand;
- buy correctly;
- move inventory;
- negotiate;
- create margin;
- repeat.

To Jordan, **selling used cars can feel like drug money without the drug case**: meaningful cash flow, deal-making, motion and independence, but inside a legitimate business structure.

That realization becomes the seed of **Whip Equipped**.

Whip Equipped is therefore not a random company Jordan happens to start after Hitman.

It is his first deliberate attempt to answer the question:

> Can I use the same hustle legally and build something I can actually own in public?

---

# 14. Age 26–33 — the Ghost Years

Whip Equipped does **not** immediately turn Jordan into a conventionally stable businessman.

The second seven-year arc is defined by a paradox:

**Jordan becomes more legitimate while becoming harder for the people from his old life to pin down.**

He knows two categories of people may still have reasons to look for him:

- **Royce**, who inherited the position Benji refused and may still view Jordan as a rival, threat or unfinished succession problem;
- people and networks Benji made enemies of during the Hitman years.

Jordan therefore has no desire to plant himself permanently in one obvious place.

For roughly seven years, mobility becomes both his business model and his survival posture.

He moves among states, lives for periods in **cars, trucks, RVs and buses**, buys and flips vehicles, and uses legitimate dealer/auction access as a recurring way to create cash without depending on a conventional long-term job.

The story-level point is not that Jordan possesses a magical technique for becoming untraceable. The point is that he does not build the kind of fixed, predictable civilian life that would make him easy for old enemies to locate through routine habits.

He is **off-grid in the social sense**:

- no durable home identity;
- no single long-term residence that defines him;
- no stable daily geography;
- income repeatedly generated through deals and vehicle turnover;
- possessions kept mobile;
- identity increasingly separated from the Benji world he left behind.

Jordan is not necessarily invisible to governments, financial institutions or modern technical systems. Canon should not exaggerate the concept into supernatural invisibility.

But to people searching through ordinary street knowledge, old routines, neighborhoods, mutual contacts and predictable physical presence, **Jordan becomes extraordinarily difficult to catch in one place.**

This is why the age-26-to-33 period is known structurally as **the Ghost Years**.

---

# 15. The vehicle as shelter, inventory and identity

During the Ghost Years, vehicles become much more than merchandise.

At different times the same class of asset can function as:

- transportation;
- temporary housing;
- business inventory;
- office/storage space;
- emergency liquidity;
- a symbol of independence;
- and the next thing Jordan intends to sell.

Jordan can therefore appear materially successful while remaining structurally unstable.

He may have access to valuable vehicles while lacking a conventional bedroom.

He may generate meaningful cash while lacking a predictable paycheck.

He may understand assets while possessing very little permanence.

This contradiction becomes one of the defining images of the Whip Equipped era:

> **Jordan owns motion before he owns stability.**

---

# 16. Infrastructure enters Jordan's life

The Ghost Years are not seven years spent only flipping vehicles.

Jordan also begins taking work around **data centers and infrastructure**.

He learns through practical exposure to environments organized around:

- power;
- backup power;
- cooling;
- racks and physical systems;
- connectivity;
- access control;
- monitoring;
- redundancy;
- maintenance;
- controlled environments;
- uptime and failover.

This creates one of the central ironies of Jordan's life.

He may leave a facility deliberately engineered for continuity and redundancy and then sleep in a vehicle because his own life has no fixed home.

Jordan becomes fascinated by infrastructure partly because it represents everything his personal life does not yet possess:

**continuity, predictability, redundancy and designed stability.**

This practical infrastructure exposure is part of why 33-year-old Jordan enters PRIM3 with knowledge and instincts that exceed what other people expect from a returning college student.

---

# 17. Why Jordan returns to college at 33

By 33, Jordan understands something uncomfortable:

**he could keep surviving this way forever.**

He is good enough at hustling, buying, selling, moving, improvising and finding another route that instability itself has become sustainable.

That is no longer what he wants.

Returning to college becomes Jordan's deliberate attempt to **re-enter the grid**.

For the first time since leaving school at 19, he intentionally chooses:

- a fixed institution;
- a recurring schedule;
- conventional housing;
- formal credentials;
- professors and prerequisites;
- a path toward traditional employment;
- and a life whose next step does not always depend on another flip or another workaround.

College is therefore not merely an educational decision.

It is Jordan's attempt to normalize his life.

He wants to cool down the **hustle-for-a-living mentality** that has defined fourteen years of adulthood.

He is trying to become easier to understand, easier to employ, easier to house and more conventionally rooted.

That makes the beginning of PRIM3 deeply ironic:

> **Jordan finally reappears in a fixed place because he is trying to live normally—and that is exactly when the deepest system in his life finds him.**

Age 33 is therefore a genuine reemergence.

The world that knew Benji could not reliably say where Jordan had settled during the Ghost Years.

Now Jordan is enrolled, housed, scheduled and visible again.

PRIM3 begins when the ghost voluntarily steps back onto the grid.

---

# 18. Three seven-year identities

The long-form Jordan chronology is now structurally:

```text
AGE 19–26 — HITMAN / BENJI
Question: What will Jordan do to survive and retaliate?
Power language: force / loyalty / street organization

AGE 26–33 — WHIP EQUIPPED / THE GHOST YEARS
Question: Can Jordan turn hustle into legitimate ownership while staying alive long enough to disappear from his old life?
Power language: capital / mobility / vehicles / business / infrastructure

AGE 33–40 — PRIM3 / JORDAN
Question: What will Jordan do when knowledge becomes authority?
Power language: infrastructure / information / command

AGE 40+ — PRIME
Question: Can Jordan hold force, capital and information without becoming the man he defeated?
```

These eras are not personality resets.

Hitman teaches Jordan operational instinct, loyalty, retaliation and the danger of self-authorized morality.

Whip Equipped teaches him ownership, mobility, business, logistics, infrastructure, legal systems, improvisation and the limits of a life built entirely around motion.

PRIM3 adds formal systems knowledge, technical discipline, evidence, authorization, governance and eventually succession.

Prime inherits all three men.

# 20. Continuity migration rule

Effective with this canon:

1. **Benji is Jordan Vale's former Hitman identity.**
2. There is no separate contemporary Benji who independently climbs Hitman alongside Jordan.
3. **Royce inherits the structural role of the post-Benji Hitman-side rival/successor** in later dual-board material unless deliberately revised.
4. Older references to `Jordan + Benji` as two different men in RAID/Evil Twin are superseded at the canon level and must be rewritten as the finale is refined.
5. References to `Benji` that clearly describe Jordan's age-19-to-26 Hitman period remain valid and should not be renamed to Royce.
6. Bigga Lex's retirement is tied to the birth of his children and Jordan's refusal to endanger their father through inherited loyalty.
7. Royce is seven years younger than Jordan/Benji and receives Lex's former position at approximately age 19.
8. Jordan's age-26 exit flows directly into Whip Equipped as a legitimacy/ownership strategy.
9. The age-26-to-33 Whip Equipped period is also **the Ghost Years**: Jordan stays geographically mobile, lives in vehicles, flips vehicle inventory, works around infrastructure and avoids rebuilding the predictable routines of his Hitman life.
10. Jordan's return to college at 33 is a deliberate **re-entry into conventional visibility, housing, education and employment**, not merely a degree decision.
11. Prime's relationship to Jordan must eventually account for Jordan's hidden Hitman and Ghost Years history, but **whether Prime knew about Benji before Jordan's college reemergence remains open until explicitly locked**.
