# PRIM3 — Ensemble POV & Episode Ownership Authority

## Core correction

PRIM3 is **not** a single-protagonist show in which every episode exists primarily to develop McCluster.

McCluster is one of the central spine characters and the eventual PRIM3 successor, but PRIM3 is an **ensemble techno-thriller** whose world becomes deep because other characters own complete episodes, relationships, mistakes, victories and transformations independent of McCluster.

Canonical law:

> **McCluster is the succession spine. He is not the owner of every story.**

The show may follow McCluster's seven academic years as its chronological frame while allowing episode POV, emotional ownership and mission leadership to rotate among the multinational cast.

---

# 1. Episode owner

Every episode must identify an **EPISODE OWNER**: the character or small character cluster whose internal problem changes most because of the episode.

The episode owner is not automatically:
- McCluster;
- the mission commander;
- the person with the most screen time;
- the performer of the song;
- or the character who performs the decisive technical action.

The owner is the person whose belief, relationship, obligation or identity receives the deepest story write.

Examples:
- an Aya episode can center her unresolved Hana/McCluster suspicion while McCluster appears only briefly;
- a Chioma episode can be about command responsibility while McCluster is one difficult subordinate among several;
- an Amira/Marc episode can center GLASSHOUSE guilt and risk authority without McCluster participating in the mission at all;
- a Jonas/Pylon episode can function primarily as a human-machine relationship story;
- a Hitman-side episode can develop Royce or another Hitman figure while McCluster exists only as an old name, rumor or unexplained reference.

---

# 2. McCluster presence is optional

There is **no rule requiring McCluster to appear in every episode**.

Allowed narrative states include:

```text
A — MCCLUSTER-OWNED
McCluster is the primary emotional/mission POV.

B — SHARED
McCluster is one of several co-equal episode owners.

C — SUPPORTING
McCluster appears materially but the episode belongs to somebody else.

D — CAMEO / EDGE
McCluster appears briefly, calls, texts, is seen in class, handles one task, or affects a decision at the edge.

E — MENTIONED ONLY
McCluster is discussed, referenced in a report, remembered, or has an off-screen consequence.

F — ABSENT
McCluster does not appear and is not required to be mentioned.
```

A healthy season should use several of these states.

Do **not** force McCluster into an episode merely to remind the audience who the eventual successor is.

---

# 3. The opening exception — High Alert

Global Episode 01 / S1E1 `High Alert` is structurally unusual.

Its chronological event belongs to the endgame and is centered on:
- **PRIM2** — presiding Mantle holder;
- **McCluster** — eventual challenger / PRIM3;
- **Royce** — Hitman-side surviving challenger/counterpart.

The opening therefore foregrounds the succession triangle because it is showing the future conflict before the story rewinds.

This does **not** establish a rule that McCluster must dominate Episodes 02–18.

Instead, the rewind reveals the people and systems whose lives ultimately make the RAID meaningful.

---

# 4. Majority-ensemble principle

Across Episodes 02–18, the default writing assumption should be:

> **Most episodes primarily deepen characters other than McCluster, even when McCluster is present.**

McCluster's college progression remains a chronological connective tissue:
- classes;
- academic years;
- changing competence;
- friendships;
- relationships;
- summers away;
- institutional standing;
- and gradual approach toward succession.

But that timeline is the **clock**, not the ownership rule.

The audience should be able to describe substantial arcs for Aya, Chioma, Royce, Amira, Marc, Helen, Thandiwe, Li, Leila, Jonas, Pylon and others without reducing those arcs to "how they affected McCluster."

---

# 5. McCluster's past appears as residue, not monopoly

PRIM3 preserves the reverse-reveal structure for McCluster's Whip Equipped and Hitman/Benji history.

Because McCluster is not always the episode owner, past hints should often enter through other people's experience of him:

- a character notices knowledge McCluster should not obviously possess;
- McCluster recognizes a person/place/object and refuses to explain why;
- somebody finds an old photograph that visually conflicts with present McCluster;
- an institutional record has an unexplained age/date/alias inconsistency;
- Royce reacts to McCluster in a way the present cast cannot interpret;
- Aya notices a compartment in McCluster's life she cannot access;
- somebody mentions an old name without the audience receiving the full answer;
- McCluster is absent from an episode but an old consequence of his life intersects another character.

The rule is:

> **McCluster's mystery should deepen the ensemble's stories; the ensemble should not exist merely to excavate McCluster.**

---

# 6. Every major character must own consequences

Tier A and recurring Tier B characters require their own:
- material wants;
- family/external relationships;
- professional ambitions;
- wounds;
- secrets;
- moral contradictions;
- episode-scale decisions;
- and continuity residue.

A character is not considered developed merely because McCluster has a meaningful conversation with them.

The strongest test is:

> **Could this character carry an episode in which McCluster never enters the room?**

If the answer is no for a supposedly major character, the character needs more development.

---

# 7. Mission POV can differ from emotional POV

PRIM3 can separate:

```text
MISSION LEAD
TACTICAL PLAYER FOCUS
EMOTIONAL POV
EPISODE OWNER
```

They may be the same person, but they do not have to be.

Example:
- Chioma commands the mission;
- PLAY spends several turns controlling Aya/Arjun/Rafael;
- Marc owns the authorization decision;
- Amira owns the emotional consequence.

This prevents the game layer from flattening ensemble storytelling into "McCluster does everything."

McCluster's Wildcard status remains canon: he can cross operational lanes when present. It does **not** grant him automatic narrative ownership.

---

# 8. Gameplay ownership — McCluster is deliberately withheld

The story's ensemble principle must be experienced mechanically.

During **ordinary PLAY**, McCluster is not part of the normal selectable deployment roster.

The player should spend the majority of campaign turns learning, risking and caring about the rest of the cast.

McCluster can enter a playable mission in only three states:

```text
SCRIPTED
The story specifically requires McCluster from launch.

WILDCARD
McCluster begins off-board and is requested after an eligible mission reaches CRITICAL.

LOCKED OUT
McCluster cannot participate because of story, geography, injury, secrecy, authorization or communications state.
```

A McCluster Wildcard arrival never changes the episode owner by itself.

If Aya owns the episode before the call-up, Aya still owns the episode afterward. McCluster can save her plan, save her life, create an alternate route or help the team extract without taking her emotional climax away from her.

The specialist gameplay authority is:

`game/WILDCARD-INTERVENTION-AUTHORITY.md`

Canonical escalation:

```text
ENSEMBLE TEAM
    -> CRITICAL
MCCLUSTER / WILDCARD
    -> eligible high-value mission remains CRITICAL
PRIM2 / APEX INTERVENTION
```

The hostile board can answer with a **Royce Incursion**.

The purpose is not to create a hero button. It is to turn attachment to the ensemble into a strategic resource decision.

> **Everybody gets to be the hero. McCluster is who they call when heroism is not enough.**

---

# 9. WATCH / PLAY ensemble rule

WATCH may shift POV cinematically between characters.

PLAY may temporarily center whichever role/person makes the mission mechanically legible.

The player is **not literally becoming PRIM3/McCluster in every mission**.

Instead, PLAY represents the viewer entering the operational perspective of **the PRIM program / mission team**.

This distinction is mandatory now that `PRIM3` specifically names McCluster's eventual Mantle succession index.

Use language such as:
- player enters PRIM operational view;
- player assumes mission control perspective;
- player controls the current mission element;
- player operates through assigned R/E/T/Defense/support viewpoints;
- player requests the Wildcard when eligible.

Do not say "the player becomes PRIM3" during ordinary pre-succession missions.

---

# 10. Character-advancement ledger

For every episode, record:

```text
EPISODE OWNER:
SECONDARY OWNER(S):
MCCLUSTER PRESENCE: A/B/C/D/E/F
MISSION LEAD:
BASE PLAYABLE ROSTER:
PRIMARY PLAYABLE POV(S):
MCCLUSTER PLAY STATE: scripted / wildcard / locked out
WILDCARD ELIGIBLE: yes/no + reason
APEX ELIGIBLE: yes/no + reason
ROYCE INCURSION ELIGIBLE: yes/no + reason
WHO CHANGES MOST:
RELATIONSHIP WRITE:
SECRET LEARNED / MISLEARNED:
PAST-HINT WRITE:
WHO IS ABSENT BUT CONSEQUENTIAL:
NEXT CHARACTER WHO INHERITS THE RESIDUE:
```

This ledger prevents the ensemble from silently collapsing back into a McCluster-only structure in either WATCH or PLAY.

---

# 11. Distribution target

Do not lock an exact episode quota until detailed episode ownership is reassigned, but use the following authoring bias:

- McCluster should own a **minority** of Episodes 02–18 outright;
- ordinary PLAY should default to a base roster that does **not** include McCluster;
- several episodes should place him in supporting/cameo/mentioned-only status;
- at least one ordinary-season episode should prove the series can work with McCluster entirely absent and unavailable as Wildcard;
- no major recurring character should require McCluster's presence to justify their development;
- final succession episodes naturally increase McCluster/PRIM2/Royce ownership again.

This is a bias, not a mechanical quota.

---

# 12. Title and succession interaction

The ensemble structure strengthens the title reveal.

The audience may initially believe `PRIM3` means the operational program, particularly because lower-clearance personnel know the cover expansion:

> **Program for Resilient Infrastructure Management**

At root clearance it means:

> **Primary Root Identity Mantle**

The show can therefore spend years acting like it is about the **program and its people** before revealing that the number in the title also names one specific eventual human holder:

> **PRIM3 — McCluster.**

Both readings are true.

---

# 13. Governing thematic statement

PRIM3 is not "the chosen man meets interesting side characters."

It is a story about a multinational network of adults whose technical work, loyalties, failures and private histories intersect a hidden succession architecture.

McCluster ultimately inherits the Mantle because he emerges from that world.

The world does not exist because he inherits it.

Canonical shorthand:

> **The succession belongs to McCluster. The world belongs to everybody.**
