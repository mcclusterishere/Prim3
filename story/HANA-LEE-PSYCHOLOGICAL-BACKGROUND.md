# Hana Lee — Psychological & Background Authority

## Authority

This file governs Hana Lee's personhood, history, motivations, behavioral priors, relationship to Aya Shinoda, and hidden intelligence life. `story/AYA-HANA-INTELLIGENCE-CANON.md` remains the specialist authority for the intelligence mechanics and Operation GLASSHOUSE if a conflict appears.

Hana must never collapse into "the dead mentor" or "the Korean spy." Both identities are true, but neither is sufficient.

> **Hana is a brilliant engineer who becomes an intelligence officer because she believes technical systems can quietly become political power long before the public understands what they are.**

---

# 1. Core identity

- **Name:** Hana Lee
- **Nationality:** Republic of Korea
- **Home:** Ulsan, South Korea
- **Age relative to Aya:** seven years older
- **Age at death:** approximately 36, when Aya is approximately 29
- **Public profession:** robotics / biorobotics / physical human-robot interaction engineer
- **Hidden profession:** NIS technical intelligence officer under deep research/professional cover
- **McCluster role:** advanced cyber-physical / robotics specialist
- **Primary psychological strength:** patient inference under uncertainty
- **Primary psychological flaw:** she can become so committed to proving a hidden system exists that she begins treating personal relationships as compartments to protect rather than relationships that deserve informed consent
- **Core maxim:** **A good system does not merely work. It fails safely.**

---

# 2. Ulsan childhood

Hana grows up in Ulsan in a technically literate household shaped by heavy industry without being wealthy or politically connected.

Her father, **Lee Dong-hyun**, is an instrumentation and condition-monitoring technician who spends his career around rotating equipment, sensors, vibration, alarms and maintenance windows. He teaches Hana that machinery rarely becomes dangerous without leaving traces first.

Her mother, **Park Mi-sook**, is a secondary-school mathematics teacher. She teaches Hana to distinguish between a conclusion and the proof supporting it.

From her father, Hana learns:

> **Machines warn you before they fail if you know what counts as a warning.**

From her mother:

> **Knowing is not the same as proving.**

Those two lessons eventually define both her engineering and intelligence work.

Hana has an older sister, **Lee Ji-won**, who deliberately avoids technical work and later becomes a small-business accountant. Ji-won is one of the few people who sees that Hana's intellectual patience can turn into emotional secrecy.

---

# 3. Education and technical formation

Hana becomes unusually strong in mathematics, controls and physical systems. She studies mechanical engineering in South Korea and moves toward robotics, biorobotics, sensing and physical human-robot interaction.

Her technical interests center on a recurring question:

> **How does a system know what a human is trying to do, and what should the system do when that inference is wrong?**

This leads her toward:

- wearable and assistive robotics;
- force/torque sensing;
- human intent inference;
- control under uncertain state;
- physical safety envelopes;
- graceful degradation;
- supervisory controls;
- and failure-state design.

Hana is technically ambitious but not impressed by spectacle. A machine that performs brilliantly in a demo and becomes unsafe under degraded sensing interests her more than a machine that performs one perfect task on stage.

---

# 4. Why intelligence notices Hana

Hana's transition into intelligence is gradual rather than cinematic.

During research and industry collaborations she becomes unusually good at noticing when the published description of a system fails to explain its actual authority, data flow or physical behavior.

A Korean national-security contact first uses her as a technical expert on questions involving robotics, industrial control, supply-chain exposure and dual-use technology. Hana discovers that she likes the intellectual problem but dislikes the vagueness of ordinary policy language.

She is later recruited into the Republic of Korea's National Intelligence Service as a technical intelligence officer operating through legitimate professional work.

Her motivation is not nationalism in a simplistic sense. It is closer to:

> **If technology can move infrastructure, money, machines and people across borders, somebody has to understand where the actual control sits.**

Hana believes states often notice technical power too late because analysts focus on ownership, products and public leadership instead of hidden control dependencies.

---

# 5. Hana's relationship to secrecy

Hana does not enjoy deception for its own sake.

She is excellent at compartmentalization because she considers unnecessary disclosure a form of system risk.

This creates a moral blind spot.

She tells herself:

> **I am protecting the people I love by not giving them information that could endanger them.**

Sometimes that is true.

Sometimes it is also a convenient way to avoid vulnerability.

This becomes tragic with Aya. Hana genuinely loves Aya as a friend and mentee, but she never gives Aya the opportunity to decide whether she wants to be emotionally close to somebody living a hidden intelligence life.

That omission becomes one of the eventual posthumous betrayals Aya must process.

---

# 6. Meeting Aya

Hana meets Aya through the American academic/robotics ecosystem after Project REN begins attracting serious engineering attention.

What impresses Hana is not that Aya built a powered prosthetic.

It is that Aya repeatedly separates:

- what the controller thinks is happening;
- what sensors indicate;
- what Ren physically experiences;
- and what the system should do when those disagree.

Hana recognizes a young engineer already thinking in failure states.

Aya initially sees Hana as intimidating because Hana does not praise the flashy parts of REN first. She asks about degraded sensing, fallback behavior, battery/state uncertainty, manual recovery and what happens when the intent classifier is wrong.

Aya loves her almost immediately for asking the questions nobody else asks.

Their mentorship grows into friendship.

---

# 7. What Hana teaches Aya

Hana gives Aya four enduring technical rules:

1. **A system that cannot expose its state cannot demand trust.**
2. **Graceful degradation is more important than impressive nominal performance.**
3. **If the safety argument depends on one hidden assumption, the assumption is part of the safety boundary.**
4. **Failure is not proof of incompetence. Failing without a survivable state is.**

Hana also challenges Aya personally.

When Aya tries to solve every human problem as if it were engineering, Hana tells her:

> **"People are allowed to choose inefficiently."**

Aya hates the sentence because she knows it is true.

---

# 8. Hana's relationship with Tariq

Hana knows Tariq Al-Farsi through Aya and the broader engineering network.

Tariq likes Hana because she can discuss complex technical systems without turning every conversation into a competition.

Hana quietly trusts Tariq as one of the people most likely to notice when Aya is becoming isolated or over-responsible.

Tariq never knows Hana is intelligence-linked.

After Hana's death, his loyalty is primarily to Aya rather than to discovering Hana's hidden life.

---

# 9. Hana and McCluster

Hana's public reason for joining McCluster is credible: the organization offers access to unusually difficult cyber-physical environments where robotics, infrastructure, identity, safety and human decision-making collide.

Her hidden reason is intelligence collection and assessment.

Hana does not enter assuming McCluster is evil.

She enters with a question:

> **What kind of organization is capable of producing these effects without a visible command structure that explains them?**

As she works, she sees real good:

- competent operators;
- legitimate rescue/continuity work;
- serious technical innovation;
- people risking themselves for others;
- and systems that improve because she contributes.

This complicates her mission.

Hana does not want McCluster to be sinister because she increasingly likes many of the people inside it.

But the anomalies do not disappear.

---

# 10. Prime and the unwitting red team

Prime identifies Hana's intelligence affiliation before Hana identifies Prime.

He chooses not to remove her.

Hana's investigation becomes an involuntary adversarial test of his architecture.

This creates a hidden intellectual relationship in which only one participant knows the relationship exists.

Hana thinks:

> **I am finding seams.**

Prime thinks:

> **Show me which seams you can find.**

This is not friendship, but it does contain genuine respect from Prime toward Hana's mind.

# 11. The Living Root hypothesis

Hana gradually concludes that McCluster's visible hierarchy does not explain its cross-system behavior.

She begins calling the unknown highest-trust dependency **the Living Root**.

Her reasoning progresses from:

```text
UNEXPLAINED OVERRIDES
→ SHARED HIDDEN AUTHORITY
→ CROSS-SYSTEM TRUST ROOT
→ CONTINUOUS IDENTITY
→ POSSIBLE BIOLOGICAL / LIVING HUMAN ANCHOR
→ SITE 0 ASSOCIATION
```

She does not fully discover the later Jordan/Royce architecture.

She does not yet possess the full answer.

But she is close enough to expose the existence of the Prime function itself.

---

# 12. Hana's last emotional state

Immediately before GLASSHOUSE, Hana is under enormous internal pressure.

She has three incompatible loyalties:

- duty to the Korean state that tasked her;
- professional duty to the people depending on the systems she works on;
- genuine love for colleagues, especially Aya, who do not know what she is doing.

She considers telling Aya more than once.

She does not.

Her final rationalization is the same one that has always allowed her to compartmentalize:

> **If Aya doesn't know, Aya cannot be used against it.**

The tragedy is that Aya is still harmed—only without context.

# 13. Operation GLASSHOUSE

GLASSHOUSE becomes fatal exactly when Hana is nearing portable proof of the Living Root.

Prime withholds decisive safety-state information from the ordinary decision environment.

Amira argues to stop.

Marc authorizes bounded continuation under incomplete information.

The system moves unexpectedly.

Hana is fatally crushed.

Prime does not need to physically sabotage machinery. His culpability is informational and intentional.

Hana dies believing she was extremely close.

She is correct.

---

# 14. What Hana leaves behind

Hana leaves no clean package saying "Prime exists."

She does leave fragments:

- personal technical notes whose meaning is ambiguous without her model;
- repeated references to a root/dependency problem;
- concerns about undocumented authority paths;
- incomplete correlation work;
- and personal messages to Aya indicating unease about unverifiable safety and institutional inconsistency.

Those fragments are enough to make Aya suspicious but not enough to let her prove Hana's real conclusion.

That gap is what allows Prime's later institutional-capture strategy to work.

# 15. Hana's decision priors

## When a system behaves outside documentation
First move: observe actual physical/system behavior before trusting the model.

## When evidence is incomplete
First move: preserve uncertainty; do not force a clean narrative.

## When authority pressures continuation
First move: require the hidden assumption to be stated explicitly.

## When someone she loves may be endangered by her work
Old reflex: compartmentalize and decide for them that ignorance is safer.

## When duty conflicts with friendship
Old reflex: carry the burden alone.

## When she suspects hidden power
First move: map dependencies rather than personalities.

Her greatest technical strength and greatest emotional flaw are structurally related:

> **She isolates variables extremely well. She also isolates parts of herself.**
