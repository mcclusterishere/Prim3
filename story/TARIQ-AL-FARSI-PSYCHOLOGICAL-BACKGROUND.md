# Tariq Al-Farsi — Psychological & Background Authority

## Core identity

- **Nationality:** Saudi Arabian
- **Home:** Dhahran / Eastern Province
- **Age at PRIM3 opening:** approximately 31–32
- **Education:** engineering path overlapping Aya's Yale years
- **Career:** electrical/control-systems engineer specializing in aviation systems, reliability and integration
- **Relationship to Aya:** long-term friend from Yale-era engineering circles
- **Relationship to Zayd:** maternal first cousin
- **Primary strength:** relational translation—he can preserve trust through disagreement
- **Primary flaw:** neutrality can become avoidance when he delays taking a side to keep relationships intact
- **Core belief:** **A system can remain connected without every node agreeing. People should be able to do the same.**

## Dhahran background

Tariq grows up in the Eastern Province in a family where engineering, energy infrastructure, technical education and international study are familiar parts of adult life. His family is comfortable but not dynastic or politically elite.

His father, **Faisal Al-Farsi**, is an electrical protection/reliability engineer whose work around power systems teaches Tariq to think in redundancy, fault isolation and restoration rather than dramatic failure.

His mother, **Maha Al-Harthi Al-Farsi**, is Omani-born and raised partly between Muscat and the Eastern Province. Her extended family gives Tariq deep personal ties to Oman, including Zayd.

Tariq grows up in a home where family visitors, technical coworkers and relatives from different Gulf cities overlap constantly. He becomes good at reading the social temperature of a room without needing to dominate it.

## Engineering formation

Tariq is drawn toward electrical/control engineering because he likes systems whose failure consequences travel far beyond the failed component.

Aviation systems later appeal to him because aircraft force disciplines he respects:

- redundancy;
- fault tolerance;
- human-machine coordination;
- formal maintenance;
- configuration control;
- and the refusal to treat "probably fine" as an adequate safety argument.

He is technically rigorous but less obsessive than Aya. Where Aya keeps digging until the state is known, Tariq is more comfortable making bounded decisions under uncertainty if responsibilities and fallback states are explicit.

## Aya

Tariq meets Aya through Yale engineering/prototyping circles and eventually through Zayd.

He is one of the few people who understands that Aya's controlling behavior is not primarily arrogance. It is fear translated into competence.

He also refuses to reward it.

When Aya and Zayd struggle, Tariq does not act as spy for either side. He hears both, challenges both and refuses to let family loyalty turn Zayd into automatically right or friendship turn Aya into automatically right.

That fairness is why Aya keeps him in her life after the relationship ends.

## Zayd

Tariq and Zayd are close maternal first cousins.

Tariq admires Zayd's comfort with ambiguity and aesthetics but gets frustrated by his tendency to postpone confrontation.

After Aya/Zayd ends, Tariq's conclusion is not that Aya was bad for Zayd or Zayd was weak. His conclusion is that both waited too long to name the real problem:

> **Aya thought helping more would fix the relationship; Zayd thought tolerating more would preserve it.**

## Hana

Tariq knows Hana as a brilliant older engineer and Aya's mentor.

He respects her immediately because she can disagree without performing superiority.

He does not know her NIS identity.

After Hana dies, Tariq becomes one of Aya's external stabilizers. He believes Aya has the right to investigate McCluster but worries that grief can make any unexplained pattern feel intentional.

This caution later contributes to Aya doubting her own original suspicion. Tariq will eventually feel guilt when the truth emerges—not because he betrayed Aya, but because some of his most reasonable advice helped her remain inside the institution.

## McCluster

Tariq never becomes a core McCluster operator.

He remains valuable precisely because he is outside.

He consults occasionally on aviation/reliability questions and therefore understands enough technical context to speak intelligently without being institutionally absorbed.

Prime considers him lower threat than Hana because Tariq does not obsessively pursue hidden authority. Prime may nonetheless monitor him because he remains one of Aya's strongest independent emotional reference points.

## Behavioral priors

**Conflict between two people he loves:** mediate, translate, seek the hidden disagreement; risk = staying neutral after a moral line is clear.

**Technical uncertainty:** identify the failure envelope and fallback state rather than demand impossible certainty.

**Aya over-controls:** challenge her with a question instead of direct accusation.

**Family pressure:** respectful but resistant; he does not confuse family closeness with automatic obedience.

**Institutional secrecy:** skeptical, but unlike Hana he does not assume secrecy itself proves malign intent.

## Canonical role in Aya's arc

Tariq preserves continuity with **Aya-before-McCluster**.

He knows her as a student, engineer, girlfriend, sister, grieving mentee and later Field-T operator.

His narrative function is not to rescue Aya from Prime.

It is to remain a person whose relationship with Aya does not depend on her being useful to an institution.
