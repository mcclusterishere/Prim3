# Zayd Al-Harthi — Psychological & Background Authority

## Core identity

- **Nationality:** Omani
- **Home:** Muscat, Oman
- **Age at PRIM3 opening:** approximately 31
- **Education:** Yale undergraduate architecture / built-environment path
- **Relationship to Aya:** serious former partner of approximately eighteen months
- **Relationship to Tariq:** maternal first cousin
- **Primary strength:** understands how environments shape people without reducing people to systems
- **Primary flaw:** can delay confrontation until accommodation becomes resentment
- **Core belief:** **A life cannot be designed entirely from the outside. The person living in it has to participate in the design.**

## Muscat background

Zayd grows up in Muscat in a family that values education, hospitality, family continuity and professional responsibility without treating tradition and modernity as opposites.

His father, **Salim Al-Harthi**, works in municipal/infrastructure project management and teaches Zayd that every elegant plan eventually meets budgets, heat, water, maintenance, traffic and people who use spaces differently than the designer imagined.

His mother, **Nawal Al-Harthi**, is the sister of Tariq's mother Maha. She works in education administration and is the person in the family most likely to ask whether a prestigious opportunity actually makes somebody's life better.

Zayd grows up noticing architecture less as iconic buildings and more as daily choreography:

- where shade falls;
- where families gather;
- how privacy is created;
- how heat changes behavior;
- how people move between public and private space;
- and how a building can respect or frustrate the people inside it.

## Yale

Zayd attends Yale and studies architecture / built environment.

He is not a tortured-genius stereotype. He is disciplined, observant and talented, but his best work comes from interviewing users and understanding lived routines before drawing solutions.

That makes him philosophically compatible with Aya at first.

Both hate bad design.

They simply disagree on who owns the correction.

Aya thinks superior diagnosis creates responsibility to intervene.

Zayd thinks diagnosis creates responsibility to **invite the person into the redesign**.

## Aya relationship

Aya and Zayd date for approximately eighteen months.

The relationship is genuine and initially excellent.

Aya respects that Zayd notices human experience she sometimes treats as secondary to system state.

Zayd admires Aya's ability to make impossible technical problems concrete.

The fracture begins gradually.

When Zayd is overwhelmed, Aya helps.

When he misses a deadline, Aya helps more.

When he has career uncertainty, Aya builds options.

When he expresses anxiety, Aya translates it into tasks.

Each individual act is loving.

Collectively, Zayd begins to feel that Aya has turned his uncertainty into a project she is managing.

His own flaw makes the problem worse: instead of confronting her early, he accepts the help, appreciates the results and quietly resents losing authorship of his own life.

Eventually he tells her:

> **"I don't know if you love me or the version of me you're trying to build."**

The line devastates Aya because she believes improvement is one of the clearest forms of love.

## Breakup

There is no infidelity, abuse, intelligence plot or betrayal.

The relationship simply reaches a point where Zayd feels smaller inside it and Aya feels unappreciated for the amount of care she is providing.

They end it painfully but without hatred.

This is important to canon:

> **Not every deep wound comes from somebody being evil. Sometimes two decent people create a damaging pattern together.**

Zayd does not spend PRIM3 waiting for Aya.

He continues his career and eventually works on climate-responsive, infrastructure-aware design projects in the Gulf and internationally.

## Tariq

Tariq is both cousin and referee.

Zayd trusts him because Tariq can tell him when his own conflict avoidance contributed to the relationship failure.

Tariq refuses to let Zayd reduce the breakup to "Aya is controlling." He makes Zayd admit that Zayd repeatedly accepted interventions he did not want because they worked.

This becomes Zayd's growth lesson:

> **Silence is not consent, but silence also does not communicate a boundary.**

## Hana

Zayd knows Hana only socially through Aya's technical circle.

He recognizes immediately that Hana is one of the few people Aya treats as intellectually senior without resentment.

Hana's death affects Zayd mostly through what it does to Aya.

He is already out of Aya's romantic life by the time she becomes deeply entangled with McCluster, but he can see from a distance that grief has given Aya another system she believes she must understand and fix.

## Jordan

Zayd and Jordan do not need to become rivals.

If they eventually meet, Zayd's value is that he can identify Aya's old relationship pattern without jealousy.

He may notice that Jordan is different because Jordan pushes back much earlier than Zayd did.

Zayd's presence can therefore make Aya realize that the Jordan relationship is not merely repeating the past; both people are changing the pattern in real time.

## Behavioral priors

**Someone offers help:** accept readily if the help preserves authorship; become uneasy when help silently becomes governance.

**Conflict with family/friend:** delay confrontation too long, then become unexpectedly firm.

**Bad design:** ask how the user actually lives before redesigning the system.

**Aya starts fixing him:** old response = accept help and resent it; mature response = state the boundary immediately.

**Authority claims expertise:** asks who bears the consequences if the expert is wrong.

## Canonical role in Aya's arc

Zayd is the first person Aya seriously loves who teaches her that **effective help can still be unwanted help**.

He also proves that Aya can have a major relationship end without treachery, death or conspiracy.

That contrast becomes crucial later: when Jordan and Aya struggle, Aya cannot simply classify relationship pain as evidence that the other person is dangerous.
