# Equity Uprise Workplace Canon

## Governing decision

The canonical recurring workplace for PRIM3 is the **current Equity Uprise building**.

This building is where the ensemble studies, works, trains, argues, eats, briefs, debriefs, repairs systems, produces media, conducts research, receives partners and experiences ordinary life across the seven-season series.

It is one authoritative location across WATCH, PLAY, songs, character workspaces, archetype/skin populations, future animation, Equity Uprise training and later assault/defense gameplay.

> **Same building. Same people. Seven years of accumulated history.**

## This is not Site 0

Equity Uprise HQ is not the root McCluster Corp headquarters attacked in the final RAID.

| Location | Story function |
|---|---|
| Equity Uprise HQ | recurring workplace, training campus, visible institution, community, day-to-day headquarters |
| Site 0 / Root HQ | hidden PRIM / McCluster Corp Corp Corp Corp root facility, succession site, final-arc assault target |

The final RAID therefore does not erase the audience's familiar workplace.

## Institutional stack

Equity Uprise is the visible training/work/community institution.

It participates in the McCluster Corp ecosystem.

McCluster Corp is governed through the PRIM architecture.

PRIM2 presides during most of the series.

Validated succession transfers the Mantle to McCluster / PRIM3.

Equity Uprise is not merely a fake shell. Its educational and community mission is real.

## Building-life rule

Every floor must contain named recurring staff workspaces, role/archetype populations, ordinary background work, technical systems, recurring social routines, episode-capable locations, after-hours states and visible consequences when people leave, die, transfer or get promoted.

Do not populate the building with anonymous mannequins when an established recurring character or role family already fits the space.

## B1 is open workplace canon

B1 is no longer a secret owner-only floor.

Canonical B1 function:

**Technical Operations + Infrastructure + Security + Hands-On Lab**

B1 still uses role-based access for safety, life-safety systems, electrical rooms, rack work and tunnel operations, but it is a known staffed workplace floor.

It appears in ordinary staff wayfinding and can be used by learners/instructors when the activity and authorization are appropriate.

The tunnel/logistics infrastructure is known operational infrastructure, not a secret passage.

Restricted compartments can exist inside B1. The floor itself is not hidden.

# Workspace persistence

A named workspace is a continuity anchor, not a prison.

Characters can work anywhere their job requires, but their assigned desk/bench/office gives visual storytelling a home.

Workspaces accumulate personal objects, wear, changed equipment, photographs, jokes, notes, evidence, relationship residue and vacancies.

If a character dies or leaves, do not instantly reset the workspace.

> **If you die, the building changes.**

# Songs live in the building

The one-song / one-episode law now has a physical home.

Examples:

- **Got WiFi?** — F2/F3 wireless problem, B1/F3 diagnosis, F4 reinforcement/recording.
- **OSINT** — F5 research/source spaces.
- **Red Blue Purple White Team** — F2 instruction and F3 operational planning.
- **Data Breach** — F3 technical response and F5 evidence/accountability.
- **Cap Ex Vs. Op Ex** — F5/F6 institutional decision pressure.
- **Per Diem** — F7 mobility/logistics and temporary labor.
- **RAID / Evil Twin / Ghost In The Wires** — may begin/end at Equity Uprise, but the final root confrontation occurs at Site 0.

Songs are no longer floating technical interludes. They arise from work, people and systems the audience recognizes.

# Assault / defense continuity

When Equity Uprise becomes assaultable/defensible in PRIM3 PLAY, game code reads the canonical building and workplace state rather than inventing a separate HQ map.

Attacks can affect people at their actual workspaces, camera coverage, access routes, power/network systems, evacuation, command, media/public response, research/evidence and mobility.

The everyday familiarity of the building is what makes an assault emotionally matter.

# Episode-writing requirement

Future episode writing answers:

1. What floor(s) does this episode use?
2. Which named workspaces are active?
3. Which recurring coworkers are naturally present?
4. What ordinary work was happening before the problem?
5. What changes physically or socially in the building afterward?
