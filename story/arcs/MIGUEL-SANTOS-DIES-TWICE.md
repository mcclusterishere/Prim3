# Miguel Santos dies twice

> Owner, 2026-11-03: "You kill somebody. We're gonna revive them later in the story … because it was a fake death and then it was really a death that they predicted … they came back at the last minute, but then they actually die when they come back. So they die twice … Do a crazy reveal … last five episodes or something like that … she's the one who points him back to it … because Hannah or whoever got led to the new situation through the word game … Make the word game really clever … put some dialogue in there and build the story situationally … Elongate the arc."

**One-line arc.** Miguel Santos is certified dead at Harborline in S1E2. He was not dead: he saw the one thing in the building that should not exist, knew what happens to people who see it, and failed safely before the system could fail him. For fifteen episodes the building carries his empty chair. In S6E2 Aya, finally rescuing Hana's last dead end in Kkeutori, rereads Miguel's last radio word and understands that it was not a loss. She points McCluster to him. Miguel comes back at the last minute for RAID, and dies there for real, the death the system had already written for him, the second time by his own choice.

This file adds to canon only where it says so, and every addition is listed in §9 for change control.

## 2. The word game, extended

Kkeutori's canon rules (`story/AYA-HANA-KKEUTORI-BREADCRUMB-CANON.md`) are unchanged. This arc adds one house variant and one reading.

**The third-language variant (new, DRAFT).** When Aya plays with someone outside the pair she lets them use their own language, and keeps exactly one rule: *you lose if your word ends on n*. Miguel plays in Tagalog. He is bad at it on purpose, because losing makes Aya laugh on an open channel and a laughing operator is a calm operator.

**Hana's rule, applied late.** Canon gives Aya the sentence she will need: *"Dead end in one language. Continue in the other."* and its deeper reading, *"A dead end may only mean you are using the wrong system."* For fourteen episodes Aya uses it only on Hana. In S6E2 she finally uses it on Miguel.

**The word.** Miguel's last move in S1E2 is **taguan**. In Aya's rules it is a loss: it ends on n. In Tagalog it is the game children play when someone has to disappear and everyone else has to count: *hide-and-seek*, and also *a hiding place*. Aya heard a man losing a word game seconds before a fire. He was telling her where he was going and that someone would have to come looking.

His next words, the ones the audience hears as a joke and Nusrat later hears every night, were: **"Then rescue me."**

Under the N-rescue rule a dead end is rescued by an n-word. The rescue Aya finally plays in S6E2 is **North River**.

## 3. S1E2 — Permission Slip: the first death

Planned mission, solo: Aya inside under concealment, Nusrat on comms, Miguel holding the exterior (per the E02 solo draft). The partner's lawful seizure (treatment Scene 4) is under way in the utility yard. It has been raining since the shoot started.

### 3.1 The device and the signature (placed in the treatment's Scene 14–15)

The recruit's onboarding kit is open on the access desk. The device inside answers to the same class as the unmanifested unit on Harborline's network.

> **NUSRAT:** Issuance batch. Same class, wrong envelope. Somebody in onboarding grabbed from the wrong drawer.
>
> **MIGUEL:** *(turning the approval slip toward the lamp)* Then who signed the drawer?
>
> **NUSRAT:** The local office.
>
> **MIGUEL:** The local office signs in blue and dates it like a human. This one resolved. *(He taps the field twice.)* It came down from somewhere that doesn't have a desk.
>
> **NUSRAT:** Miguel.
>
> **MIGUEL:** I know. Ordinary error. *(beat)* I'm just going to remember it.
>
> **NUSRAT:** *(to the recruit, flat)* No. *(She stamps the kit.)* Now you're authorized.

The audience gets the canon resolution and a relieved laugh. Miguel does not laugh. He photographs nothing. He *remembers* it, which in his job is worse.

### 3.2 The radio round (planned downtime, mid-shoot)

The seizure stalls. Aya is holding position behind the shoot's light rig. Nusrat is logging. Miguel is under the loading canopy in the rain.

> **AYA:** You're breathing like a man who's bored.
>
> **MIGUEL:** I'm breathing like a man who's wet. Play your game.
>
> **AYA:** Kkeutori. Your language, my rule. End on n, you lose. *Tora.*
>
> **MIGUEL:** *Radyo.*
>
> **AYA:** *Yoru.*
>
> **MIGUEL:** *Ruta.*
>
> **AYA:** You're stealing Spanish.
>
> **MIGUEL:** Everybody stole Spanish. *Ta.* Give me a *ta*.
>
> **AYA:** *Tamago.*
>
> **MIGUEL:** *Gabi.*
>
> **AYA:** *Biru.* *(beat)* Building. Like the one you're leaning on.
>
> **MIGUEL:** I'm not leaning, I'm load-bearing.
>
> **NUSRAT:** *(on channel)* Utility yard, the partner's moving the transformer cage. Miguel, eyes on the cage.
>
> **MIGUEL:** Eyes on the cage. *(beat)* Aya, last word, I have to work.
>
> **AYA:** *Kata.*
>
> **MIGUEL:** *(no hesitation)* *Taguan.*
>
> **AYA:** *(delighted)* N! That's n. You lose. You always lose.
>
> **MIGUEL:** Then rescue me.

Two taps on the channel. Then the yard lights go white, then orange, then out.

### 3.3 The death the paperwork certifies

There is no body to look at, and the episode never shows one. The partner's incident report records a transformer fault and fire in the utility yard. The death is certified the way everything in this episode is certified:

- the issued badge's last reader event at the yard gate;
- the issued device's last heartbeat, flat at 21:14;
- the partner's report;
- and an approval for release of personnel records that resolves, again, above the local office.

The episode's theme closes like a trap: **the building's permission system says he is dead, so he is dead.** Aya, who just spent forty minutes learning that permission is not knowledge, does not think to apply the lesson to the paperwork. Grief does not audit.

> **NUSRAT:** *(after the channel has been quiet too long)* Miguel. Report. *(nothing)* Miguel. Two taps. *(nothing)* … Exterior is lost. Aya, hold. Hold. Do not go outside.
>
> **AYA:** He lost the game. He *lost the game*, Nusrat, that was the last thing —
>
> **NUSRAT:** Hold.

**Debrief (PLAY).** The mission scores as a success. The Defense-02 roster card for Miguel greys. His slot on the access desk is not refilled (CANON §4: the building inherits the vacancy, not the person).

### 3.4 What actually happened (shown only in S6E3)

Miguel knew what the approval field meant because he had seen the pattern once before, at a port, years ago: when someone asked about a signature that came from nowhere, the systems around them got very clean, and then the person did. He did not know the word PRIM. He knew the shape.

So he failed safely before anyone could fail him. The seizure's transformer fault was real: he did not cause it. He *used* it. He dropped his badge and device into the cage yard where the fire would read them last, walked out through the partner's own cordon in a partner's rain shell (the one person on site who remembers every face also knows how faces are not checked), and went somewhere nobody looks for a living man: a reopened building organized around a dead woman.

He did not tell Nusrat. Telling her would have made her the next clean system.

## 5. S6E2 — Patch Work / Trust Boundary: Aya rescues the dead end

This is Aya's episode (CANON §21) and the canon place where Hana's decisive `ん` dead end lands as "your turn". The arc folds into it rather than competing with it.

Aya has just reconstructed Hana's rescue clusters (MAP → PERMISSION → ROOT → PULSE → ZERO → INHERIT → TWO; their meaning is canon and is not changed here). She reaches the final unfinished chain and understands it: *Hana left the last dead end for Aya to rescue.*

Then, alone at 3 a.m., she does what grief has stopped her doing for five seasons: she plays every dead end she remembers through Hana's rule. Including the one on the S1E2 channel log.

> **AYA:** *(to the log, playing it back)* *"Taguan."* *(beat)* N. You lose. *(She almost closes the file. Hana's sentence comes back in Hana's voice.)* "Dead end in one language." *(She types the word into a dictionary she has never once opened for Tagalog.)*
>
> *taguan* — hide-and-seek. A hiding place.
>
> **AYA:** … Continue in the other.

She replays the next line. *"Then rescue me."* Two taps.

She knows the N-rescue rule better than anyone alive. A dead end on n is rescued by an n-word. She does not need a dictionary for that one. Every grief file she has kept for five seasons is open on the second monitor, and three of them have the same two words in them: Rafael's heat signature, Amira's argument, Marc's certainty.

> **AYA:** *North River.*

She does not go alone. That is the growth. She takes it to the one person she trusts least and believes most.

> **AYA:** *(McCluster's office, no knock)* I need you to look at something and not decide anything for one minute.
>
> **MCCLUSTER:** You've never asked me for a minute.
>
> **AYA:** Hana lost on purpose. Miguel lost on purpose. I thought I was the only one who knew the rules. *(She plays the channel. "Taguan." "Then rescue me.")* That's not a loss. That's a location and an instruction.
>
> **MCCLUSTER:** He was certified.
>
> **AYA:** By a badge, a heartbeat, a partner's report and a signature that comes from above the desk. You taught me what permission is worth. *(beat)* It's an n. You rescue an n with an n.
>
> **MCCLUSTER:** *(long beat)* … North River.
>
> **AYA:** You already knew.
>
> **MCCLUSTER:** I know Marc swears it's empty. I've never once believed a man who swears.

She points him back to it. He goes.

## 6. S6E3 — the hiding place (Song #21 stays open)

The song for this slot remains PENDING and is not filled here. Its locked job (CANON §21) is unchanged: a shared root becomes verifiable, allies split, McCluster decides to challenge PRIM2. This arc supplies one route into that job and nothing else.

McCluster finds Miguel on the sealed service level at North River, thinner, living off the reopening's contractor supply, with a notebook of every approval field he has seen resolve from nowhere since S1E2. He has spent five seasons doing the one thing an access officer can do from the dark: *remembering*.

> **MIGUEL:** You took your time.
>
> **MCCLUSTER:** You took a fire.
>
> **MIGUEL:** I borrowed a fire. It was already burning. *(beat)* Is Nusrat okay?
>
> **MCCLUSTER:** She kept your call sign. She packs your kit.
>
> **MIGUEL:** *(breaking, very quietly)* Then she's not okay.
>
> **MCCLUSTER:** Why didn't you tell her?
>
> **MIGUEL:** Because the system was going to clean the next person who knew. I wasn't hiding from you. I was hiding *her*.

**The verifiable root (proposal for E18's locked job).** Miguel's notebook is not testimony; it is a reader-by-reader list of approval events that resolve above every local office, including the S1E2 slip, the S4E2 reader Grace bagged, and North River's own reopening permits. Leila and Helen can verify it against records they control. That is what makes the root verifiable without PRIM2's confession (CANON: a confession alone never counts). *Proposed, not canon.*

**Allies split.** Helen wants Miguel reported and protected. Leila wants him kept dead because his death is the only thing protecting the notebook. Nusrat finds out last, which is the point: the access officer who logs every entry is the one person nobody told. Her scene is one line.

> **NUSRAT:** *(seeing him)* … Your badge is still in my drawer. *(beat)* You're not authorized.
>
> **MIGUEL:** I know.
>
> **NUSRAT:** *(she stamps the back of his hand with the desk stamp she has carried for five seasons)* Now you are.

It is the S1E2 line, reversed, and it is the first time she has cried on screen.

## 7. S7E1 — RAID (Hot Site): the second death

CANON §18 is unchanged: in RAID every other member of both assault elements dies; McCluster and Benji are the only survivors of their sides; the roster is locked only after Episodes 02–18 are fully developed. This draft **proposes Miguel for the PRIM-side roster** (it is the one RAID death the audience has already been forced to grieve once, which is the opposite of a redshirt).

Miguel is not on the plan. He comes back at the last minute because the plan's access path runs through a reader he is the only living person to have seen fail.

> **MCCLUSTER:** You're dead. Stay dead. It's the only thing protecting the notebook.
>
> **MIGUEL:** The notebook's verified. Leila has it, Helen has it. It doesn't need me anymore. *(beat)* You need someone who knows which door signs in blue.
>
> **MCCLUSTER:** If you go in, the system gets the death it already wrote for you.
>
> **MIGUEL:** It wrote it in my handwriting. *(beat)* My whole life I was afraid of choosing between procedure and someone I love. This isn't that. This is both.

He holds the access point long enough for McCluster to get through. He does not get out. The death is not hidden and not certified by paperwork: Nusrat hears it live, on the same channel as S1E2.

> **MIGUEL:** *(low, steady)* Nusrat. Last word. *Tahanan.*
>
> *(tahanan: home. It ends on n.)*
>
> **NUSRAT:** *(Bengali, without hesitation, the rescue)* *Nodi.*
>
> *(nodi: river.)*

Two taps. Then nothing, and this time nothing is the truth.

He died twice: once by the building's permission, once by his own.
