# PRIM3 — Episode Cast Density & Recurring Roster Authority

## Core law

PRIM3 is not an anthology that invents support people whenever an episode needs texture.

The immediate world already contains approximately **39 recurring character slots** who work, study, train, brief, debrief, eat, argue, recover and operate inside overlapping institutional environments for years.

Default solution:

> **Reuse the people who already live here.**

Canonical density:

> **Every ordinary episode should normally contain at least 6–7 meaningful named presences, with 7–10 preferred.**

Larger convergent episodes may exceed ten naturally.

Canonical micro-development:

> **Every recurring character who appears must receive at least one meaningful development beat.**

Historical/outside characters appear only when an established relationship makes them relevant.

---

# 1. Support means recurring coworker first

A support character is usually another recurring cast member whose episode this simply is not.

The same person can be:

```text
OWNER in one episode
ACTIVE SUPPORT in the next
REMOTE SUPPORT later
AMBIENT WORKPLACE PRESENCE elsewhere
OWNER again seasons later
```

That recurrence is the point.

Do not confuse `support` with `new guest`.

---

# 2. Default composition

```text
1–2  primary owners — deep development
3–6  recurring coworkers / command / technical / support — micro/mid development
0–2  recurring Hitman-side parallels when relevant
0–2  established outside/history relationships when specifically earned
----------------------------------------------
6–10 meaningful named presences normally
```

Before inventing anybody, ask:

> **Which existing person would realistically already be here?**

Then ask of every named recurring person who appears:

> **What is newly true about this character by the end?**

If the answer is nothing, give them a beat or remove the appearance.

---

# 3. Valid micro-development

A support beat can be 5–30 seconds if it persists.

Useful categories:
- belief;
- relationship;
- competence;
- status;
- habit;
- secret;
- material life;
- moral line;
- grief;
- future seed.

Examples:
- Miguel stops joking with somebody after a disagreement;
- Sari automatically brings a backup after `Got Wifi`;
- Priya starts removing a phrase from press drafts because she no longer trusts what it implies;
- Emre checks a physical control twice after a prior failure;
- Valeria begins correcting Jordan instead of only absorbing his advice;
- Oksana notices Dmitri saving her seat;
- Jonas says `we` when talking about Pylon and catches himself.

Tiny does not mean disposable. The beat must survive.

---

# 4. Presence types

A recurring character can matter through:

### WORKPLACE
briefing room, lab, access desk, cafeteria, hallway, classroom, transport, command floor, debrief, recovery, office, campus crossover.

### MISSION
playable operator, remote analyst, sponsor, command lead, access officer, technical support, logistics, extraction, medical/safety support.

### RELATIONSHIP
friendship, rivalry, flirtation, resentment, mentorship, humor, debt, competition.

### CONSEQUENCE
policy, injury, reputation, staffing problem, resource shortage, grief, workload or emotional fallout created earlier.

### OPPOSING BOARD
Hitman people recur because Hitman is also an organization, not because every episode needs a fresh villain.

### INSTITUTIONAL
Maren, Pylon, Helen, Amira, Marc, Li, Leila, Chioma and PRIM2 recur through approvals, decisions, policy, system behavior and consequence.

# 6. Same-building realism

Low-intensity recurrence is required.

Examples:
- Miguel hands Aya a badge while continuing an earlier conversation;
- Sari steals Yonas's coffee, but later Yonas already bought her one;
- Dmitri/Oksana enter an elevator mid-argument;
- Chioma asks Grace for a debrief note during Valeria's scene;
- Priya passes through a lab shooting approved photos and chooses not to photograph something she once would have;
- Jonas complains Emre manually disabled automation, then asks why before re-enabling it;
- Leila corrects Li and he silently updates the document;
- Maren waits outside command with two prepared statements because she knows leadership will disagree;
- Pylon behaves mundanely repeatedly before unusual behavior becomes meaningful.

The beat can be tiny. It cannot be inert.

# 7. Whole-roster continuity

Every episode tracks the current recurring roster through:

```text
A — ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT
B — AMBIENT CONTINUITY
C — OFFSCREEN CONSEQUENCE
D — UNAFFECTED / OUT OF FRAME
```

Do not force everybody on camera.
Do not accidentally forget major people exist for half a season either.

Episode chapters should record:

```text
CORE OWNERS:
ACTIVE ROSTER:
WORKPLACE RECURRENCE:
RELATIONSHIP RECURRENCE:
OPPOSING-BOARD RECURRENCE:
ESTABLISHED HISTORY/OUTSIDE PRESENCE: optional
WHO CARRIES PRIOR-EPISODE RESIDUE:
WHO GETS A FUTURE-PAYOFF SEED:
```

# 8. Historical restraint

Do not require a historical/outside character per episode.

Use established relationships when they actually affect present behavior:
- Hana / Ren / Tariq / Zayd with Aya where appropriate;
- Bigga Lex with Jordan/Royce history;
- other outside people only after deliberate promotion.

Prefer recurring outside relationships over one-use exposition names.

---

# 9. Promotion / attrition / vacancy

An ownership episode makes a character more reusable; it does not retire them.

A support character can become a main through repeated competence, relationships, responsibility, secrets, vacancies and audience attachment.

A main can drift, transfer, lose authority, become injured/disabled, resign or die.

Death/exit must alter:
- workload;
- briefing combinations;
- deployments;
- routines;
- relationships;
- equipment/secrets;
- promotion pressure;
- need for cross-training;
- Wildcard reliance.

Do not auto-replace a dead person one-for-one.

If a newcomer arrives later, they inherit the **vacancy**, not the predecessor's social role.

Full authority: `story/CAST-LIFECYCLE-ATTRITION-PROMOTION-AUTHORITY.md`.

# 10. No single-episode boxes

After somebody owns an episode, later supporting appearances should become richer because the audience now understands them.

Examples:

```text
NUSRAT owns E02 -> richer support later
OKSANA owns E03 -> E09/E11/E18 carry that investment
CHIOMA owns E05 -> command recurrence through the series
SARI owns E06 -> E13/E15/E16 deepen her rather than restart her
LEILA owns E07 -> E13/E17/E18/E21 inherit the same person
AYA owns E08 -> E12/E17/E18/E20/E21 continue her psychology
JONAS owns E12 -> Pylon thread persists into E18/E20/E21
AMIRA/MARC own E14 -> GLASSHOUSE/risk history matters later
```
