# PRIM3 — Episode Cast Density, Support & Historical Web Authority

## Core correction

A PRIM3 episode cannot feel like two named people standing inside a curriculum lesson.

The series is a multinational institutional world. Every canonical episode must therefore contain enough overlapping human presence that the audience feels coworkers, friends, rivals, mentors, families, dead predecessors, outside professionals and opposing-board people continue existing when they are not the episode owner.

Canonical density law:

> **Every episode should contain at least 6–7 meaningful named character presences across WATCH + PLAY, with 7–10 preferred for most episodes.**

A “presence” does not require every person to stand in the same room or receive a monologue.

Valid presence modes:

```text
OWNER      — receives the deepest psychological write
ACTIVE     — physically participates in present-day scenes/mission
REMOTE     — call, command, technical support, debrief, message, live feed
OUTSIDE    — friend/family/contractor connection outside McCluster
LEGACY     — deceased/lost person whose old note, memory, recording or lesson affects a present choice
OPPOSING   — Hitman or another adversarial/parallel character whose action changes the episode
TRACE      — PRIM2/Pylon/institutional presence that produces a character consequence
```

The minimum is about **story function**, not crowd size.

Do not solve density by putting eight silent people around a table.

---

# 1. Episode population formula

Default episode composition:

```text
1–2  primary owners
2–4  active/remote recurring mains
1–2  legacy/outside/historical relationships
1–2  institutional or opposing-board presences
----------------------------------------------
7–10 meaningful named characters
```

Every non-owner presence must earn its inclusion through at least one of:
- changing an owner's decision;
- exposing a contradiction;
- carrying residue from an earlier episode;
- planting residue for a later episode;
- supplying a different professional/cultural assumption;
- creating/solving a relationship problem;
- revealing history;
- being the person who must live with the outcome.

---

# 2. Overlap law

Every episode from 02–18 should normally include:

1. at least **one character who materially carried the previous episode**;
2. at least **one character whose larger payoff occurs later**;
3. at least **one outside/Legacy relationship**;
4. at least **one non-owner main receiving a mini-development write**;
5. an opposing-board or PRIM2/Pylon/institutional pressure where relevant.

This produces continuity rather than anthology reset.

Historical people must not exist only as dead-body motivation. A Legacy appearance should reveal a habit, argument, joke, professional doctrine, unresolved guilt, old document, or remembered mistake that changes present behavior.

Outside characters must not exist merely to answer the phone and agree. They should preserve an alternate worldview outside McCluster.

---

# 3. Runtime law

The approximately nine-minute WATCH target does not mean all seven-to-ten characters need equal screen time.

Use layers:
- cold-open / ordinary life;
- music-film imagery;
- mission brief;
- active mission;
- comms chatter;
- short flash memory;
- post-mission call/debrief;
- background continuity action.

PLAY can carry additional dialogue and relationship beats that WATCH compresses.

A character can receive a meaningful write in 10–20 seconds if the beat is specific and carries forward.

CHARACTER:
MODE: owner / active / remote / outside / legacy / opposing / trace
WHAT THEY WANT IN THIS EPISODE:
WHAT THEY DO:
WHAT CHANGES OR IS REVEALED:
WHAT CARRIES FORWARD:

A character can have a small write. They cannot have **no** write.

Bad:
> “Hassan is also in the room.”

Good:
> “Hassan initially assumes the unexplained access anomaly is human malice; Dilshad's outside challenge makes him revise that assumption, weakening his long-term slide toward generalized suspicion.”
