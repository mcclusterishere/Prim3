# FO-E — Entry Field Operator

## Function
The physical access and close-security specialist. Responsible for getting the team onto the objective, controlling immediate space, and protecting the Technical operator while the technical work is performed. This is the kinetic and spatial problem-solver of the field triad.

FO-E exists so that FO-T can work. When the technical window is open, FO-E’s primary product is controlled space and time.

## Visual Direction
- Highest soft-to-mid armor load of the three basic field phenotypes
- Darker, more aggressive weathering than FO-T; still institutional, never generic special forces
- Strong, readable Dual Sight / red `//` for rapid recognition in confined spaces, smoke, or low light
- Silhouette emphasizes mobility + protection rather than maximum armor bulk
- Gloves and boots built for forced movement, climbing, door work, and close contact
- Face protection short of the full M-helmet (partial shell, reinforced balaclava, or advanced headset)
- Overall look: the person who goes through the door first and holds it

## Gear Specifications

### Design philosophy
Gear exists to create access, hold space, and keep FO-T alive and working.  
Every added item must improve entry, control, or protection.  
Bulk that slows movement through doors, stairs, or confined plant rooms is rejected.

### Equipment Floor
| Item | Specification |
|------|---------------|
| Base layer | Dark technical field suit |
| Armor | Soft-to-mid torso protection; highest of the triad |
| Sidearm | One compact service sidearm, low-profile holster |
| Primary | Mission-selected primary tool or weapon (more common than on FO-T) |
| Entry tools | Mechanical entry set at minimum |
| Comms | Secure team radio + reliable earpiece |
| Recording | Body or headset mission recorder |
| Identification | Strong Dual Sight or red `//` visibility |
| Handwear | Impact- and abrasion-resistant gloves that still allow device and weapon use |
| Footwear | Aggressive field boots rated for forced movement and debris |

### Equipment Ceiling (mission-authorized)
**Protection**
- Soft armor with optional thin hard plates (front/back)
- Limited extremity protection (knees, elbows) when the environment demands it
- Partial face / neck protection
- No full ballistic helmet, no riot gear, no bomb-suit derivatives

**Entry & Access**
- Mechanical entry tools (spreaders, breachers, bypass hardware)
- Limited energetic entry tools when authorized
- Door and lock defeat capability appropriate to the target
- Short-range cutting or penetration tools for interior obstacles

**Kinetic**
- Sidearm mandatory
- Mission-selected primary (carbine, suppressed platform, or specialized entry weapon)
- No magazine-farm infantry setups that destroy the institutional silhouette

**Electronics**
- Standard field comms and recording
- Limited personal sensor or short-range detection tools
- No full technical exploitation stack (that belongs to FO-T)

### Hard Exclusions
- Full Prime M-helmet and Mission Core
- Heavy plate carriers overloaded with pouches
- Primary technical exploitation kits (FO-T domain)
- Long-duration observation / sniper-style optics packages (FO-R domain)
- Clean Corporate Fits garments
- Non-McCluster marks or generic special-forces patches

## Gear Loadouts by Mission Profile

FO-E does not wear one fixed kit. The base identity stays constant; modules change with the phase of the operation. Below are the four primary loadout configurations.

### 1. Approach & Entry (FO-E-AE)
**Use when:** Moving to the objective and making the initial breach or technical entry.

| Category | Load |
|----------|------|
| Armor | Soft-to-mid torso; optional thin plates |
| Entry tools | Full mechanical set + limited energetic if authorized |
| Kinetic | Sidearm + mission-selected primary |
| Comms | Full secure team link |
| Recording | Continuous from final approach |
| Identification | Strong Dual Sight / `//` visibility |
| Mobility | Balanced — must carry entry tools without becoming slow in corridors |

**Design note:** This is FO-E at its most tool-heavy. The job is to open the space. Once inside and the door is controlled, excess entry tools can be staged or dropped if they interfere with holding.

---

### 2. Hold & Protect (FO-E-HP)
**Use when:** FO-T is on the target node and FO-E is pure close security — doors, angles, and immediate space.

| Category | Load |
|----------|------|
| Armor | Soft-to-mid; prioritize coverage over tool bulk |
| Entry tools | Minimal — only what is still needed for interior obstacles |
| Kinetic | Sidearm + primary ready; emphasis on control, not volume of fire |
| Comms | Full link to FO-T and FO-R |
| Recording | Continuous |
| Identification | High visibility for rapid team recognition in confined space |
| Mobility | High within the held space; must be able to shift rooms or collapse fast |

**Design note:** Stripped of pure entry bulk. The product is controlled space and time. Every extra pouch that slows a room-to-room shift is a liability.

---

### 3. Contact / Collapse (FO-E-CC)
**Use when:** The space is compromised, contact has occurred, or FO-R has called an external threat that forces movement.

| Category | Load |
|----------|------|
| Armor | Maximum authorized soft-to-mid for FO-E |
| Entry tools | Only what is required for the exit path |
| Kinetic | Sidearm + primary; readiness for short, decisive action |
| Comms | High-tempo team link |
| Recording | Continuous through the break-contact |
| Identification | High visibility — team must find each other under stress |
| Mobility | Highest priority — lead the move, keep FO-T moving, break contact |

**Design note:** This is FO-E in emergency mode. Entry tools become secondary. The job is no longer “hold for the technical window”; it is “get the protected asset and the team off the objective alive.”

---

### 4. Technical Escort (FO-E-TE)
**Use when:** Moving FO-T between nodes inside a larger facility, or escorting the technical element during a multi-stage objective where entry is already complete but the space is still hostile.

| Category | Load |
|----------|------|
| Armor | Soft-to-mid, balanced for movement with FO-T |
| Entry tools | Light interior set (doors, cabinets, secondary barriers) |
| Kinetic | Sidearm + compact primary preferred |
| Comms | Tight link to FO-T; FO-R picture still active |
| Recording | Continuous |
| Identification | Readable Dual Sight |
| Mobility | Matched to FO-T’s pace — never so light that protection is sacrificed, never so heavy that FO-T is delayed |

**Design note:** Hybrid of Hold and movement. FO-E stays glued to the technical element without turning the pair into a slow, over-armored target.

---

### Loadout Selection Logic

| Situation | Preferred Loadout |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Final approach and initial breach | Approach & Entry (FO-E-AE) |
| FO-T working, space still tenable | Hold & Protect (FO-E-HP) |
| Contact, FO-R abort call, or space lost | Contact / Collapse (FO-E-CC) |
| Moving FO-T between interior nodes | Technical Escort (FO-E-TE) |

An operator will typically sequence through these in a single mission: Approach & Entry → Hold & Protect → (optional Technical Escort) → Contact/Collapse or controlled exit.

### Cross-loadout rules
1. Sidearm never comes off.
2. Dual Sight stays readable unless a specific low-signature phase is ordered (rare for FO-E).
3. No loadout may become a full technical exploitation kit or a pure long-range observation package.
4. If the operator cannot move through a standard door or stairwell under control, the loadout is wrong for FO-E.
5. When FO-T is exposed and working, FO-E’s kit must not create noise, snag, or delay that endangers the technical window.

## Core Responsibilities
- Route selection and physical approach
- Forced or technical entry onto the objective
- Immediate area security once inside
- Close protection of FO-T during the entire technical window
- Control of doors, corridors, stairs, and chokepoints
- Leading movement on contact or emergency exfiltration
- Rapid decision-making about whether the space is still tenable

## Behavioral Expectations

### Core posture
An Entry Field Operator treats space as a resource that is constantly being spent. Every second the door is open, every corridor left unsecured, and every angle left uncovered is a cost. The job is to buy FO-T enough controlled space and time to finish the work.

### During entry and on the objective
- Highest baseline physical readiness on the team
- Decisions are spatial and temporal: where to stand, what to block, when to move, when to collapse the perimeter
- Low tolerance for extended technical debate once the team is exposed
- Treats FO-T as a protected asset for the duration of the technical window
- Maintains constant awareness of FO-R’s external picture

### On contact or deterioration
- Prioritizes team integrity and exit over continued objective work unless explicitly ordered otherwise
- Will force movement if the space becomes indefensible
- Does not require perfect information to act; acts on the best available picture and updates in motion

### Speech patterns
- Short, directional, and action-oriented
- Uses clear movement and security language
- Minimal narrative while the situation is live
- After contact, language becomes extremely precise about what happened in the space and what the remaining options are

### Relationship to pressure
- External threat increases tempo rather than producing hesitation
- Visible stress appears as sharper movement and shorter communications
- The operator is expected to remain the calmest body in the room when the room becomes violent

## Psychological Load (FO-E specific)
- Responsibility for the physical survival of the technical element
- The knowledge that a single bad angle or delayed movement can erase the entire technical take and the people who produced it
- Repeated exposure to the moment of entry — the highest-risk transition in most operations
- Carrying the immediate human cost when contact occurs (FO-T may still be focused on systems; FO-E is focused on bodies and space)
- Tendency toward hyper-vigilance about doors, corners, and timing that can persist after return to controlled environments

## Relationship to the Field Triad
| Phenotype | Relationship to FO-E |
|-----------|----------------------|
| FO-T Technical | Protected asset; FO-E’s primary product is the space and time FO-T needs |
| FO-R Recon | Supplies the external warning that lets FO-E decide whether the space is still worth holding |
| FO-E Entry | Creates access, holds the interior, leads movement on contact or exit |

## Failure Modes
- Over-focusing on the immediate room and losing the larger exit picture
- Holding a space past the point it is tenable because FO-T is not finished
- Excessive aggression that creates noise or casualties the mission cannot afford
- Under-communicating movement decisions so FO-T and FO-R lose the picture
- Post-mission difficulty standing down from continuous spatial threat assessment

## Narrative Utility
FO-E is the phenotype for forced entry, corridor tension, and the physical reality that surrounds technical work. It keeps field sequences kinetic and human without collapsing the team into generic special-forces aesthetics. The drama sits in the collision between the need to protect the technical window and the need to keep the team alive when the space turns against them.

## Transition
- From CO-01 or FO-01 when assigned the entry / close-security role on an external mission
- Back to CO-01 or Corporate Fits when returning to controlled environments
- Repeated FO-E exposure accelerates the physical and spatial components of the broader operator trauma profile, with particular emphasis on responsibility for other people’s bodies in confined, hostile space
