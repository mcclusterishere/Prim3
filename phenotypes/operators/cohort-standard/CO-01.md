# CO-01 — Cohort Operator (Standard)

## Function
The working uniform of an active cohort member once they have passed basic evaluation and are cleared for real (or near-real) operational work. This is the phenotype the audience sees most often once the story leaves pure campus life.

## Visual direction (to be locked)
- Matte black or dark charcoal technical base layers
- Light segmented soft armor or plate carrier that still reads corporate rather than pure military
- Visible but restrained Dual Sight / `//` accents on wrists, shoulders, or chest
- Modular forearm interface (data + tools)
- Rear or side compute module (lighter than full Mission Core)
- No full M-helmet yet — partial face protection or open tactical headset only
- Boots and gloves that look lived-in and serviceable

## Equipment ceiling
- Soft armor + one sidearm or mission-specific tool
- Cyberdeck / wrist module
- Body camera or mission recorder
- Comms and limited medical

## Exclusions
- Full Prime M-helmet and Mission Core (reserved for PRIME-N1)
- Heavy SWAT / plate-carrier overload
- Civilian corporate clothing mixed in
- Loud branding or fashion pieces

## Social signal
“I am no longer a student. I am being tested in the real system.”

## Behavioral Expectations

### Core posture
A CO-01 operator moves like someone who has already accepted that the next mission might remove them from the story permanently. They are competent enough to be useful and still human enough to be afraid. The fear is managed, not absent.

### Competence and limitation
- They execute tasks with professional focus but still reveal gaps in experience.
- They do not possess the near-omniscient systems intuition of the presiding Prime.
- When something unexpected happens, the first visible response is rapid assessment, not panic or bravado.
- They will attempt improvisation when doctrine fails, but the improvisation is still grounded in training rather than pure genius.

### Relationship to hierarchy
- They treat the institution as both employer and judge.
- Orders from above are followed with visible discipline, even when private doubt exists.
- They watch the people above them carefully — not only for instruction, but for survival cues.
- Open defiance is rare. Quiet, private re-evaluation of loyalty is constant.

### Relationship to the cohort
- Teammates are simultaneously the only people who understand the pressure and potential future liabilities.
- Genuine care for specific individuals coexists with the knowledge that the system may later require them to treat those same people as resources or risks.
- After a mission that produces attrition, the remaining operators show restrained grief mixed with heightened vigilance. They do not perform dramatic mourning in front of the institution.
- Humor, when it appears, is dry, short-lived, and usually directed at shared absurdity rather than at each other.

### Relationship to the mission
- They treat every operational problem as both a technical challenge and an evaluation event.
- Success is noted but not celebrated for long. Failure is studied immediately.
- They document and report with unusual precision because incomplete records are dangerous.
- They understand that “mission success” and “personal survival” are related but not identical goals.

### Emotional texture
- Fatigue is visible in posture and micro-expressions even when the operator is fully functional.
- Emotional range is compressed: fewer big displays, more controlled intensity.
- Moments of private doubt are allowed; public collapse is not.
- The operator is learning to separate what they feel from what the system needs them to do. This separation is incomplete and costly.

### Speech and communication
- Language is more precise and less casual than civilian or early-recruit speech.
- Technical terms appear naturally because they are tools, not performance.
- They avoid unnecessary speculation about the true nature of the organization in mixed company.
- When speaking to the presiding Prime or senior evaluators, economy of words becomes extreme.

### Failure modes (behavioral)
- Over-identification with the cohort at the expense of institutional survival instincts.
- Premature hardening that makes them treat teammates as already expendable.
- Paralysis when the cost of a correct action is the loss of a specific person they still care about.
- Quiet accumulation of resentment toward the evaluation process itself.

### What the audience should feel
Watching a CO-01 operator should produce a mixture of respect and dread. They are no longer outsiders, but they are also not yet the finished product. Every mission is simultaneously work and a test they cannot fully see the grading criteria for.

## Transition
From Corporate Fits or Recruit Student → CO-01 after first real operational exposure.  
From CO-01 → PRIME-N1 only at succession endpoint (or rare temporary issue).
