# CO-01 — Cohort Operator (Standard)

## Function
The working uniform of an active cohort member once they have passed basic evaluation and are cleared for real (or near-real) operational work. This is the phenotype the audience sees most often once the story leaves pure campus life.

## Visual direction (to be locked)
- Matte black or dark charcoal technical base layers
- Light segmented soft armor or plate carrier that still reads corporate rather than pure military
- Visible but restrained Dual Sight / `//` accents on wrists, shoulders, or chest
- Modular forearm interface (data + tools)
- Rear or side compute module (lighter than full Mission Core)
- No full M-helmet yet — partial face protection or open tactical headset only
- Boots and gloves that look lived-in and serviceable

## Equipment ceiling
- Soft armor + one sidearm or mission-specific tool
- Cyberdeck / wrist module
- Body camera or mission recorder
- Comms and limited medical

## Exclusions
- Full Prime M-helmet and Mission Core (reserved for PRIME-N1)
- Heavy SWAT / plate-carrier overload
- Civilian corporate clothing mixed in
- Loud branding or fashion pieces

## Social signal
“I am no longer a student. I am being tested in the real system.”

## Behavioral Expectations

### Core posture
A CO-01 operator moves like someone who has already accepted that the next mission might remove them from the story permanently. They are competent enough to be useful and still human enough to be afraid. The fear is managed, not absent.

### Competence and limitation
- They execute tasks with professional focus but still reveal gaps in experience.
- They do not possess the near-omniscient systems intuition of the presiding Prime.
- When something unexpected happens, the first visible response is rapid assessment, not panic or bravado.
- They will attempt improvisation when doctrine fails, but the improvisation is still grounded in training rather than pure genius.

### Relationship to hierarchy
- They treat the institution as both employer and judge.
- Orders from above are followed with visible discipline, even when private doubt exists.
- They watch the people above them carefully — not only for instruction, but for survival cues.
- Open defiance is rare. Quiet, private re-evaluation of loyalty is constant.

### Relationship to the cohort
- Teammates are simultaneously the only people who understand the pressure and potential future liabilities.
- Genuine care for specific individuals coexists with the knowledge that the system may later require them to treat those same people as resources or risks.
- After a mission that produces attrition, the remaining operators show restrained grief mixed with heightened vigilance. They do not perform dramatic mourning in front of the institution.
- Humor, when it appears, is dry, short-lived, and usually directed at shared absurdity rather than at each other.

### Relationship to the mission
- They treat every operational problem as both a technical challenge and an evaluation event.
- Success is noted but not celebrated for long. Failure is studied immediately.
- They document and report with unusual precision because incomplete records are dangerous.
- They understand that “mission success” and “personal survival” are related but not identical goals.

### Emotional texture
- Fatigue is visible in posture and micro-expressions even when the operator is fully functional.
- Emotional range is compressed: fewer big displays, more controlled intensity.
- Moments of private doubt are allowed; public collapse is not.
- The operator is learning to separate what they feel from what the system needs them to do. This separation is incomplete and costly.

### Speech and communication
- Language is more precise and less casual than civilian or early-recruit speech.
- Technical terms appear naturally because they are tools, not performance.
- They avoid unnecessary speculation about the true nature of the organization in mixed company.
- When speaking to the presiding Prime or senior evaluators, economy of words becomes extreme.

### Failure modes (behavioral)
- Over-identification with the cohort at the expense of institutional survival instincts.
- Premature hardening that makes them treat teammates as already expendable.
- Paralysis when the cost of a correct action is the loss of a specific person they still care about.
- Quiet accumulation of resentment toward the evaluation process itself.

### What the audience should feel
Watching a CO-01 operator should produce a mixture of respect and dread. They are no longer outsiders, but they are also not yet the finished product. Every mission is simultaneously work and a test they cannot fully see the grading criteria for.

## Psychological Trauma Profile

CO-01 trauma is not cinematic breakdown. It is the slow, functional damage produced by repeated exposure to low-probability survival conditions while still being required to perform at a high level. The system does not break operators cleanly. It wears specific parts of them down while leaving the rest operational.

### Primary trauma sources

1. **Attrition of the known**  
   Watching specific people the operator trained, ate, and argued with simply stop existing. The institution frames these losses in sterile language. The operator is left holding the human version of the event with no official place to put it.

2. **Evaluation under lethal conditions**  
   Every mission is simultaneously a real operation and a graded test. The operator cannot fully separate “I am trying to keep us alive” from “I am being measured.” This dual frame creates chronic hyper-vigilance and a background sense that survival itself is being scored.

3. **Moral injury through procedure**  
   The operator is required to follow systems and orders that produce outcomes they would not have chosen as a private individual. The damage is not usually from a single monstrous act, but from the accumulation of smaller, justified decisions that erode the operator’s previous moral map.

4. **Anticipatory loss**  
   Once the operator understands the statistical reality of the work, they begin grieving people who are still alive. This anticipatory grief is quiet, continuous, and rarely spoken.

5. **Identity compression**  
   The civilian self, the student self, and the operator self are forced into the same body. Over time the operator experiences a progressive narrowing of who they are allowed to be in public and, eventually, in private.

### Observable trauma signatures

- **Sleep architecture changes**: light, interrupted, or highly scheduled sleep. Difficulty returning to rest after high-arousal events.
- **Emotional blunting with selective intensity**: reduced reactivity to ordinary stimuli, paired with sudden sharp responses to specific triggers (certain names, locations, equipment sounds, the smell of a particular environment).
- **Hyper-responsibility**: the operator privately assigns themselves more causal weight for outcomes than is accurate or fair.
- **Ritualized competence**: small, repeated technical or preparatory actions performed with unnecessary precision as a way of restoring a sense of control.
- **Social withdrawal that still looks professional**: the operator remains functional in team settings but reduces voluntary closeness. New bonds form more slowly and with more internal resistance.
- **Survivor’s accounting**: after attrition events, the operator runs silent internal ledgers of who lived, who did not, and what variables might explain the difference. This accounting is rarely shared.

### Internal contradictions the trauma produces

- The need for the cohort as the only people who understand the pressure *versus* the growing knowledge that attachment itself is a liability under the system’s rules.
- The desire to remain human *versus* the recognition that remaining fully human may reduce the probability of surviving long enough to matter.
- Loyalty to specific individuals *versus* loyalty to the institutional path that is the only route to greater agency.
- The hunger for recognition of competence *versus* the fear that greater competence simply means harder and more lethal assignments.

### Progressive stages (typical, not universal)

**Stage 1 — Acute exposure**  
After the first real attrition event. Shock is present but often masked by technical focus. The operator may over-work and under-sleep. Language becomes more precise as a stabilizing mechanism.

**Stage 2 — Functional adaptation**  
The operator develops reliable external performance while the internal cost rises. Emotional range compresses. Humor becomes drier. The operator begins to anticipate loss before it occurs.

**Stage 3 — Structural change**  
Attachment patterns shift. The operator may still care deeply but invests less freely. Decision-making becomes more utilitarian under pressure. The civilian moral framework is no longer the default; it must be deliberately retrieved.

**Stage 4 — Pre-succession hardening (late)**  
If the operator continues long enough, the capacity required to approach the succession path begins to form. This is not the same as becoming evil. It is the gradual acceptance that the system’s final logic may require the operator to treat even valued people as obstacles or variables. The trauma here is the quiet recognition of what they are becoming capable of.

### What the trauma is not

- It is not constant visible distress.
- It is not automatic moral collapse.
- It is not a single defining nightmare sequence.
- It is not resolved by a single conversation or cathartic moment.

The damage is cumulative, often invisible to outsiders, and frequently mistaken by the institution for successful adaptation.

### Narrative utility

CO-01 trauma is the primary engine that turns competent recruits into people capable of the later story. It explains why tenacity matters more than brilliance: the operators who continue are not the ones who feel the least, but the ones who can keep moving while carrying the weight. The same process that damages them is also the process that forges the capacity the succession path eventually requires.

## Transition
From Corporate Fits or Recruit Student → CO-01 after first real operational exposure.  
From CO-01 → PRIME-N1 only at succession endpoint (or rare temporary issue).
