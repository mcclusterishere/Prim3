# FO-01 — Field Operator

## Function
The external deployment phenotype. This is the loadout and behavioral posture used when cohort members (or more experienced operators) are sent outside corporate facilities into real-world operational environments — foreign facilities, contested infrastructure, government-directed actions against adversarial actors, or any mission where the environment is not controlled by McCluster.

This is distinct from:
- **SOI-01 Security Officer** — internal facility and information protection
- **CO-01 Cohort Operator (Standard)** — general mission-capable institutional operator
- **PRIME-N1** — end-state succession identity

FO-01 is what the audience sees when the story leaves the building and enters the field.

## Visual direction (to be locked)
- Darker, more weathered, and more modular than standard CO-01
- Increased environmental protection (weather, debris, low-visibility)
- Soft-to-mid armor that prioritizes mobility over heavy plating
- Stronger Dual Sight / `//` visibility for rapid team identification in low light or chaos
- Mission-specific modular attachments (breaching, technical entry, surveillance, exfil)
- Face protection short of the full M-helmet (balaclava, partial shell, or advanced headset)
- Boots and gloves rated for real terrain and prolonged wear
- Overall silhouette still reads as high-end institutional rather than generic military special forces

## Gear Specifications

### Design philosophy
FO-01 gear is **mission-configured institutional equipment**.  
It must remain clearly McCluster in silhouette, materials, and marks while accepting that the field is hostile, dirty, and unpredictable.  
Mobility and modularity outrank heavy protection.  
Every added item must justify its weight and visual noise.

### Equipment Floor (minimum viable FO-01)
Every Field Operator deploys with at least:

| Item | Specification |
|------|---------------|
| Base layer | Dark technical under-suit / combat shirt and trousers, matte finish |
| Armor | Soft-to-mid protective vest or light plate carrier; torso coverage only |
| Sidearm | One compact service sidearm in low-profile retention holster |
| Comms | Secure team radio + bone or in-ear element |
| Recording | Body-worn or helmet/headset-mounted mission recorder |
| Compute | Field cyberdeck or advanced wrist module with offline capability |
| Identification | Visible Dual Sight or red `//` accent for rapid friendly recognition |
| Footwear | Rugged, quiet-soled boots rated for extended wear and mixed terrain |
| Handwear | Tactical gloves compatible with device use and weapon handling |

### Equipment Ceiling (authorized maximum)
The following may be added according to mission profile. Nothing beyond this list is standard.

**Protection**
- Soft armor with optional thin hard plates (front/back only)
- Limited extremity protection (knees, elbows) only when the environment demands it
- Neck or lower-face protection short of a full helmet
- No full ballistic helmet, no riot gear, no bomb suit derivatives

**Head / Face**
- Advanced tactical headset with integrated comms and recording
- Partial protective shell or reinforced balaclava
- Low-profile eye protection / visor
- **Forbidden:** full M-architecture helmet and Mission Core (PRIME-N1 only)

**Core Electronics**
- Field cyberdeck (larger than SOI version, still wearable)
- Redundant encrypted communications
- Mission recording suite (body + optional weapon or tool mount)
- Compact power bank / hot-swap battery system
- Limited electronic countermeasure or signal analysis module (mission-specific)

**Mission Modules** (attach only what the operation requires)
- Technical entry / bypass kit
- Breaching charge or mechanical entry tools (when authorized)
- Surveillance / sensor package
- Forensic imaging or data-extraction tools
- Short-range drone or throwable sensor (rare, high-value missions only)
- Medical blowout kit (individual)
- Signaling and marking devices

**Lethal / Decisive Tools**
- One sidearm (mandatory floor)
- One primary tool or weapon appropriate to the specific task (carbine, suppressed platform, or specialized technical tool)
- No standard issue long gun across the entire phenotype; weapon is mission-selected
- No visible spare magazine farms or excessive external pouches that turn the silhouette into conventional infantry

### Modular Logic
Gear is attached to a clean base chassis (vest + belt + optional light pack).  
Modules are added or removed per mission.  
An operator returning from a technical infiltration should not look identical to one returning from a contested extraction.  
The base identity (silhouette, marks, color language) remains constant; only the modules change.

### Marks & Identity Hardware
- Exact McCluster M appears in restrained, durable form (subdued or tonal preferred in field)
- Dual Sight and/or red `//` must remain readable for team identification in low light
- No non-McCluster patches, flags, or unit insignia
- No morale patches or personalization that breaks institutional uniformity

### Exclusions (hard)
- Full Prime M-helmet + Jetson-class Mission Core
- Clean Corporate Fits (CFI) office garments
- Conventional military plate carriers overloaded with pouches
- Visible ammunition management systems that dominate the silhouette
- Riot shields, batons as primary, or crowd-control equipment
- Any gear that makes the operator read as generic special forces or police SWAT
- Non-canon marks or commercial branding

### Comparison Snapshot

| Element | SOI-01 Security | CO-01 Standard | FO-01 Field |
|---------|------------------|----------------|-------------|
| Domain | Facility | Institutional / mixed | External / uncontrolled |
| Armor | Ultra-thin vest | Light soft armor | Soft-to-mid, modular |
| Primary weapon | Sidearm only | Sidearm + limited tool | Sidearm + mission-selected primary |
| Electronics | Cyberdeck | Wrist module / light compute | Full field cyberdeck + redundant comms |
| Recording | Body camera | Body / mission recorder | Full mission recording suite |
| Modules | Almost none | Minimal | Mission-configured |
| Environmental rating | Indoor / campus | Mixed | Full field |
| Visual noise | Lowest | Low | Controlled but higher |

### Load-bearing principles
1. If the item is not required for the specific mission, it is left behind.
2. Every external pouch or module must earn its place visually and functionally.
3. The operator must still be able to move, climb, enter confined spaces, and operate devices without fighting their own gear.
4. When in doubt, the silhouette should still say “McCluster institutional operator,” not “soldier.”

## Social signal
“We are no longer inside the system’s walls. The environment is hostile and the evaluation continues.”

## Behavioral Expectations

### Core posture
A Field Operator moves with the understanding that the environment itself is an active variable. Unlike facility work, the field does not care about institutional procedure. The operator must remain institutional while the world refuses to cooperate.

### Operational behavior
- Higher baseline arousal than CO-01 inside the building
- Faster decision loops; less time for perfect information
- Strong preference for reversible actions until the point of no return
- Explicit contingency thinking (“if this node fails, we move to…”)
- Reduced tolerance for internal debate once the team is exposed

### Relationship to the environment
- Treats architecture, networks, weather, civilians, and local forces as part of the problem set
- Constant low-level scanning for surveillance, ambush, and technical compromise
- Willingness to abandon gear or change plans when the environment demands it

### Relationship to the team
- Tighter physical and communicative spacing than in facility operations
- Clearer role specialization under pressure
- Faster assumption of leadership if the designated lead is degraded
- After contact or loss, the remaining operators re-stabilize the team before processing emotion

### Psychological load (field-specific)
- Isolation from institutional support structures
- Increased moral complexity when operating among civilians or under foreign legal regimes
- Greater anticipatory stress because extraction is not guaranteed
- The knowledge that failure in the field is harder to conceal or reframe than failure inside a McCluster facility

### Speech and communication
- Shorter transmissions
- Higher use of procedural and brevity codes
- Minimal speculation once the operation is live
- After-action language becomes extremely precise; emotional content is deferred

## Distinction from SOI-01 (Security Officer)
| Dimension | SOI-01 Security Officer | FO-01 Field Operator |
|-----------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| Domain | Inside / around McCluster facilities | External, uncontrolled environments |
| Primary mission | Protect people, data, continuity | Execute directed external objectives |
| Threat model | Internal breach, access control, cyber | Hostile actors, environment, surveillance, kinetic |
| Gear philosophy | Minimal, professional, cyber-first | Modular, mission-configured, higher risk tolerance |
| Visual signal | Corporate security | Institutional operator in the open world |

## Distinction from CO-01 (Standard)
CO-01 is the general institutional operator identity.  
FO-01 is the specialized external deployment configuration of that identity (or of more senior operators).  
A character may wear CO-01 inside the system and FO-01 when sent out.

## Transition
- From CO-01 or CO-02 when assigned to external operations
- From FO-01 back to CO-01 or Corporate Fits when returning to controlled environments
- Long-term or repeated FO-01 exposure accelerates the psychological progression described in the CO-01 trauma profile

## Narrative utility
FO-01 is the phenotype that lets the story leave the building. It is what the audience sees during government-directed missions, data-center raids, infrastructure operations, and any sequence where the cohort is operating against a real external adversary. It keeps the McCluster institutional signal while acknowledging that the field is a different, more lethal classroom.
