# EI-03 — Mission Sponsor / Accountable Officer

## Function
Owns a specific line of effort. This is the suit the cohort actually feels. When a field team goes out, an EI-03 has usually signed for the objective, the accepted risk, and the political consequence. Closer to the operators than EI-01 or EI-02. Still clearly institutional authority — not cohort, not field.

If EI-01 is the institution’s will and EI-02 is the machinery, **EI-03 is the name on the operation**.

## Place in Institutional Authority triad
| ID | Name | Focus |
|----|------|--------|
| **EI-01** | Executive Authority | Strategic control, final institutional weight |
| **EI-02** | Program / Operations Director | Portfolio machinery — programs, resources, tempo |
| **EI-03** | Mission Sponsor | One line of effort — accountable for results |

## Visual direction
- Institutional suit language (Authority Black or elevated Corporate Fits)
- May run slightly less formal than EI-01 in high-tempo periods; never becomes operator kit
- Restrained Dual Sight / exact M marks
- No FO armor, no SOI vest, no sidearm as identity
- Appears in secure briefings, TOC-adjacent rooms, post-mission accountability sessions, and occasional controlled facility spaces
- Silhouette reads: “My name is on this.”

### Hard exclusions
- Operator or Building Defense gear as identity
- Performing field presence by wearing FO kits for optics
- Loud branding or casual collapse of institutional signal
- Ceremony costume that erases working authority

## Core responsibilities
- Own the objective and the accepted risk for a mission or campaign line
- Receive CO-03 after-action truth and convert it into institutional reporting
- Protect or expose the team upward depending on standards, politics, and self-preservation
- Absorb political and career consequence when the line succeeds or fails
- Stay close enough to understand the work without becoming an operator
- Push for resources from EI-02 without promising the team what the portfolio cannot give
- Hold the line between “what the team needs” and “what the institution will tolerate”

## Behavioral expectations

### Core posture
An EI-03 lives closer to the heat than the other suits. They still do not take field risk as identity, but their career is tied to outcomes executed by people who do. They are the human hinge between the cohort’s reality and the institution’s story of that reality.

### Decision logic
- More available to CO-03 than EI-02 or EI-01
- Under pressure, may push for results harder than the team can safely deliver — known failure mode
- Can become the team’s shield **or** the team’s problem
- Must choose, repeatedly, whether to transmit the team’s truth upward or manage the narrative
- Wins by delivering the line; loses by either burning the team or failing the objective

### Relationship to the system
| Layer | Relationship |
|-------|----------------|
| **EI-01** | Distant source of legitimacy; rarely direct |
| **EI-02** | Immediate institutional boss for resources and permission |
| **CO-03** | Primary working relationship; mutual dependence and mutual danger |
| **Field triad** | Feels them as “the suit who sent us”; rarely present on objective |
| **SOI / BS-01** | Uses facility as assumed background unless the line depends on it |

### Speech
- Mixes operational detail with institutional framing
- More concrete than EI-02; less absolute than EI-01
- In briefings: clear objective, accepted risk, constraints
- After action: either the cleanest translator of CO-03’s truth — or the first editor of it

### Presence rules
- Most present of the three suits in operator-adjacent spaces
- Still an institutional event when entering pure field recovery or intimate cohort space
- Post-mission sessions are where EI-03 is most dangerous and most useful

## Psychological load (EI-03 specific)
- Career and identity tied to outcomes they do not personally execute
- Temptation to manage the story of the mission rather than the reality
- Tension between loyalty to operators and loyalty to the institution that judges them
- Proximity without shared risk — the moral hazard of the role
- Knowing that protecting the team can look like failure upward, and protecting upward can look like betrayal downward

## Failure modes
- Becoming pure pressure — extracting results until the element breaks
- Becoming pure advocate — losing institutional credibility and thus the ability to resource the team
- Editing after-action truth until EI-02/EI-01 operate on fiction
- Using the team’s loyalty as personal political capital
- Disappearing after failure and leaving CO-03 to carry both the operational and political cost
- Performing solidarity in the room and abandonment in the report

## Social signal
“I did not kick the door. My name is still on what happened when you did.”

## Distinction from EI-02
| EI-02 | EI-03 |
|-------|-------|
| Portfolio across many lines | One line of effort |
| Allocates capacity | Consumes capacity for a purpose |
| Rarely “my boss” to a field team | Often the suit the team actually feels |
| Succeeds when the institution stays coherent | Succeeds when this line delivers |

## Distinction from CO-03
| EI-03 | CO-03 |
|-------|-------|
| Suit / institutional accountablity | Operator / element lead |
| Signs for risk | Takes the risk |
| Converts truth into institutional language | Produces the truth under pressure |
| Career tied to the line’s story and result | Career and body tied to the element’s survival and result |

## Trifecta utility
- **Game:** Mission authority / quest-giver with real political weight. Can shield, burn, resource, or starve the player element. Relationship with CO-03 is a core progression and tension system.
- **Story:** Human face of institutional pressure. Best suit for scenes where the cohort argues with “the system” as a person. Seasonal arcs about loyalty, narrative control, and the cost of proximity without shared risk.
- **Real:** Accountable officer model — one named person owns the line of effort, signs for risk, and carries consequence without being the executor.

## Transition
- From senior institutional staff, exceptional EI-adjacent track, or rare lateral from high-performing CO-03 who left the operator path
- Toward EI-02 through demonstrated portfolio judgment (not just one successful line)
- Toward EI-01 is rare and political — not the default reward for good sponsorship
- Failure as EI-03 is often loud: a burned line, a broken team, or a report that does not survive contact with reality
