## Narrative vs editorial identity

For each cast character distinguish:
1. **Story identity:** name, occupation/phenotype, personality, relationship, grievance, motivation, age/time period, what is known at each season and the canon creator.
2. **Character mission:** in-world personal objective/gameplay/story function, which can change as consequences accumulate. **Not** automatically a live charitable `action_mission`.
3. **Editorial mission:** what this fictional performer publishes in short clips, music, discussions or scripted crossovers without breaking their story identity.
4. **Production identity:** rights, likeness sheet, voice/motion references, disclosure, last reviewed canon release, approved spoilers and media-asset lineage.
5. **Agent identity:** isolated logical persona, allowed knowledge, content planning capability, approval gates and legitimate account ownership. Not independent human authentication.

A character's **public performer** cannot narrate secret history beyond the audience's release permissions. A "tell the truth" script still has to honor the reverse-reveal law. Information withheld from the character in the show cannot freely appear in their public comments.
