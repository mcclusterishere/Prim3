# PRIM3 — Wildcard Intervention Authority

## Core fantasy

Most playable PRIM operations do **not** deploy Jordan Vale.

The normal game belongs to the ensemble: Aya, Chioma, Arjun, Rafael, Sari, Oksana, Minh, Jonas, Valeria, Thandiwe and the rest of the qualified roster should carry missions, make mistakes, get hurt, save one another and earn their own legends.

Jordan is mechanically exceptional because he is **not the default answer**.

He is the call you make when the answer you brought is failing.

Canonical ladder:

```text
NORMAL PRIM MISSION TEAM
        ↓ mission degrades
EXECUTIVE CALL-UP AVAILABLE
        ↓ approved
JORDAN VALE — WILDCARD
        ↓ critical mission still collapsing
APEX REQUEST AVAILABLE
        ↓ approved
PRIM2 — PRESIDING MANTLE HOLDER
```

The opposing side has its own escalation pressure:

```text
NORMAL HOSTILE / HITMAN PRESSURE
        ↓ eligibility + campaign pressure
ROYCE INCURSION
```

This is not a reset button. It is controlled escalation inside the same tactical mission.

Canonical shorthand:

> **The roster solves the mission. The Wildcard saves the plan. PRIM2 saves the board. Royce breaks the board.**

---

# 1. Jordan is not a default squad slot

During ordinary PLAY missions, Jordan is **not selectable in the normal pre-mission roster** unless that specific authored story mission explicitly assigns him.

This rule exists for three reasons:

1. the ensemble must remain mechanically important rather than becoming Jordan's backup cast;
2. Jordan's reputation as the Wildcard must be experienced as a gameplay fact, not only stated in dialogue;
3. calling him should feel like escalation.

Allowed Jordan states in PLAY:

```text
SCRIPTED ASSIGNMENT
Jordan is part of the mission from launch because the story specifically requires him.
No Wildcard Authorization is consumed.

OFF-BOARD WILDCARD
Jordan is not deployed. He may become callable if the mission reaches an eligible crisis state.

LOCKED OUT
Story geography, injury, secrecy, communications failure, authorization, or another authored condition makes Jordan unavailable.
```

Episode ownership remains separate from tactical presence. Jordan can be called into an Aya-owned, Chioma-owned or other character-owned episode without stealing emotional ownership of that episode.

---

# 2. Crisis-state grammar

Interventions are driven by mission state, not by a generic "summon hero" button.

Every playable mission tracks:

```text
STABLE
DEGRADED
CRITICAL
TERMINAL
```

### STABLE
The intended plan remains viable. Normal squad play only.

### DEGRADED
Meaningful loss of time, information, access, position, service health or operator health has occurred, but the assigned team still has a credible route to success.

### CRITICAL
At least one primary success path is about to collapse and the team needs exceptional intervention to preserve operators, the objective, civilians, evidence or extraction.

Typical CRITICAL triggers include one or more of:

- a named operator is Downed and remains at material risk;
- Window Clock is at 2 or fewer with the primary technical result still unsecured;
- Extract Clock is at 2 or fewer while a casualty, evidence package or critical operator remains outside SAFE;
- primary Objective Integrity enters its authored critical band;
- a required Control Zone is LOST while technical work remains unfinished;
- PictureState is HOSTILE while a time-critical objective remains active;
- civilian/life-safety state enters imminent-loss territory;
- the current mission plan has become impossible but an alternate route may still exist.

### TERMINAL
The authored state has crossed the point where the current mission can be fully rescued. Intervention may still save people, evidence, partial objectives or future campaign state, but it may not magically restore an impossible S-grade.

Intervention preserves consequence. It does not erase it.

# 3. Executive Call-Up — Jordan Vale / Wildcard

## 3.1 Request flow

When an eligible mission reaches CRITICAL and Jordan is off-board, the command interface exposes:

```text
EXECUTIVE CALL-UP
REQUEST WILDCARD
```

The request goes upward through the mission's authority chain. At lower story clearance, the player may not know exactly who is watching or who grants the authorization.

Jordan does not teleport.

Each eligible mission authors a plausible:
- insertion route;
- arrival delay;
- remote-to-physical transition if applicable;
- communications requirement;
- and extraction consequence.

Default gameplay arrival is **1–3 rounds after approval**, modified by mission geography and story.

## 3.2 Wildcard Authorizations

Default campaign balance:

- **2 Wildcard Authorizations per season** for non-scripted Jordan call-ups;
- unused authorizations **do not bank** into the next season;
- an authored reward may grant **1 bonus Wildcard Authorization** for exceptional Standing or a specific story achievement;
- a scripted mission that begins with Jordan assigned does **not** spend this resource.

These numbers are canonical v1 balance defaults. They may be tuned for difficulty modes without changing the fiction that Jordan call-ups are scarce executive resources.

## 3.3 What Jordan mechanically is

Jordan is the **Wildcard**, not a fourth permanent phenotype.

He can cross R/E/T operational lanes because his strength is rapid systems inference, improvisation, practical infrastructure fluency and finding another viable path when the planned route fails.

He should not permanently outperform a top specialist at that specialist's deepest work.

Jordan's default intervention kit includes:

### ANOTHER PATH — once per intervention
Using already earned/visible mission evidence, Jordan exposes one previously unselected **credible alternate route, dependency, interaction or objective approach**.

This does not fabricate hidden truth and does not auto-complete the objective.

### ADAPTIVE ROLE
At the beginning of each Jordan activation, designate one emphasis until his next activation:

- `R` — Picture / movement / evidence support;
- `E` — control / protection / rescue support;
- `T` — technical / dependency / recovery support.

He gains access to a bounded subset of that lane's actions for the turn.

### RALLY THE ROOM — once per intervention
Remove `Staggered` from one ally or grant one adjacent/linked ally a 1-AP reaction opportunity that must be spent on movement, stabilization, verification or mission-objective work.

It is not a free attack generator.

### FAILOVER INSTINCT — passive
When the current primary plan becomes invalid, Jordan suffers no AP surcharge for the first legal switch to a verified alternate route that round.

## 3.4 Calling Jordan has a cost

A Jordan rescue is still a legitimate success, but it is not the same as completing the mission independently.

Aftermath records:

```text
WILDCARD_USED: true
INDEPENDENT_COMMAND_BONUS: unavailable
EXECUTIVE_ATTENTION: +1
```

The player may still earn a strong mission grade. The intervention primarily costs scarcity and institutional independence, not arbitrary punishment.

# 4. Apex Request — PRIM2

## 4.1 Eligibility

PRIM2 is not a second ordinary rescue button.

A non-scripted Apex Request becomes eligible only when **all** of the following are true:

1. the mission is tagged `CRITICAL`, `STATE`, `STRATEGIC`, or equivalent high-value classification;
2. Jordan is already physically/operationally present, either from launch or via Wildcard call-up;
3. after Jordan's arrival, the mission remains in CRITICAL state or re-enters CRITICAL state for at least one full tactical round;
4. communications/authority conditions still permit an executive request;
5. PRIM2 is not story-locked out.

This produces the escalation fantasy:

> **If the Wildcard is already on the board and the board is still dying, call the person who owns the board.**

## 4.2 Scarcity

Default campaign balance:

- **3 non-scripted Apex Authorizations across the entire seven-season campaign**;
- they do **not** refresh each season;
- they cannot be replenished through ordinary Budget spending;
- scripted PRIM2 appearances do not consume them;
- difficulty modes may reduce or increase the pool, but the canonical standard campaign pool is three.

Three is intentionally small enough that the player remembers every time they asked for PRIM2.

## 4.3 Early-game spoiler handling

Before the story has openly identified PRIM2, the player-facing control may read:

```text
REQUEST EXECUTIVE OVERRIDE
```

or

```text
APEX SUPPORT AVAILABLE
```

The underlying system still records the intervention as PRIM2.

After the Mantle reveal, the UI may name him directly.

## 4.4 PRIM2 tactical identity

PRIM2 should feel qualitatively different from Jordan.

Jordan finds another path.

PRIM2 arrives with a broader picture, sovereign authority and elite multi-domain capability.

Default PRIM2 intervention rules:

- **3 AP on his arrival activation**;
- **2 AP on later activations**;
- very high Will;
- high combat competence;
- broad but bounded R/E/T access;
- one command action per round that does not replace another operator's entire turn.

Unique abilities:

### SOVEREIGN PICTURE
On arrival, PRIM2 exposes one additional mission-relevant information package his authority legitimately possesses. It is labeled with provenance as `PRIM2-SOURCED`; it is **not automatically promoted to VERIFIED** if independent validation is still required.

### COMMAND PRIORITY — once per round
Grant one allied operator a 1-AP command action restricted to:
- Move;
- Stabilize;
- Verify;
- Seal;
- Abort;
- Guard;
- or another mission-authored non-attack emergency action.

### SOVEREIGN EXCEPTION — once per intervention
PRIM2 may authorize one action that was previously blocked only by mission-level authorization. The action still carries any physical, technical, ethical and narrative consequences.

Authority can change permission. It cannot change reality.

### ROOT PRESENCE — passive
Certain Site 0/Pylon/PRIM-authority objects may respond differently while the living Mantle holder is present. Episode-specific behavior must be authored and may never become a universal magic-door key.

## 4.5 Apex consequence

Aftermath records:

```text
APEX_USED: true
INDEPENDENT_COMMAND_MASTERY: capped
EXECUTIVE_ATTENTION: +2
PRIM_DEBT: +1
```

`PRIM_DEBT` is initially presented as institutional dependency/obligation. Later story context may reveal how personally PRIM2 was observing these interventions.

Apex intervention is designed to save a campaign from a brutal loss without making failure meaningless.

# 5. Intervention cannot erase death or bad decisions retroactively

A call-up acts on the **current** battlefield state.

It may:
- stabilize a still-living operator;
- create a viable extraction;
- preserve remaining evidence;
- restore a recoverable service;
- expose a different route;
- change authorization;
- stop the mission from becoming worse.

It may not:
- resurrect a dead operator;
- restore evidence already destroyed beyond recovery;
- reverse a story-locked death;
- reset Detection or Heat to zero;
- rewind the mission clock;
- retroactively make an unauthorized action authorized;
- restore an S-grade whose required conditions are already permanently lost.

The mechanic is mercy through escalation, not save-scumming disguised as canon.

# 6. Royce Incursion — Hitman's Wildcard

## 6.1 Core role

Royce is the opposing-side answer to the player's rescue hierarchy.

He is not a random elite guard and not a reskinned Jordan.

Royce is the current Hitman-side apex operator/successor figure: aggressive, power-hungry, physically dangerous, experienced with the organization Jordan left behind and personally entangled with Jordan's old Benji history.

Gameplay purpose:

> **Just when the player believes the mission is under control, the other board is allowed to escalate too.**

## 6.2 Incursion eligibility

Royce may enter an eligible mission when one or more campaign conditions are high enough:

- Hitman pressure / hostility;
- mission strategic value;
- repeated player interference with Hitman objectives;
- high institutional Heat;
- a story-authored rivalry flag;
- Jordan Wildcard deployment on a mission where Hitman has visibility;
- or a specific episode trigger.

His appearance may be telegraphed, partially telegraphed or surprising depending on earned intelligence.

## 6.3 Frequency cap

Default standard-campaign cap:

- maximum **2 non-scripted Royce Incursions per season**;
- maximum **1 Royce Incursion per mission**;
- after a non-scripted appearance, Royce has a **2-mission cooldown** before another random/pressure-driven incursion;
- story missions may deliberately override the cap;
- final RAID chronology is story-authored and is not governed by the random-incursion limit.

This keeps him frightening without making him exhausting.

## 6.4 Royce mechanical identity

Royce's fantasy is **violent disruption and pressure**, not technical omniscience.

He should be able to:
- break or contest Control Zones quickly;
- pressure wounded or isolated operators;
- force route changes;
- damage objective tempo;
- apply Will pressure;
- command Hitman units more effectively;
- punish a team that overextended to chase a perfect grade.

He should **not** solve deep technical LAB_NODE tasks merely because he is a boss character.

Representative abilities:

### TAKE THE ROOM
Royce rapidly converts one vulnerable SECURE/CONTESTED zone toward hostile control if the player has insufficient protection there.

### PRESSURE CASCADE
When Royce Downs or severely pressures an operator, nearby allies face increased Will pressure for that round.

### HITMAN COMMAND
Once per round, one Hitman unit may reposition or take a bounded support action around Royce.

### OLD BUSINESS
If Jordan is present, both characters gain unique contextual dialogue and selected rivalry modifiers. This must create risk for **both** sides rather than simply buffing Royce.

## 6.5 Royce is persistent

A non-finale Royce encounter normally ends through:
- player extraction;
- Royce withdrawal;
- mission-objective resolution;
- authored separation;
- or a temporary tactical defeat that does not contradict his later canonical survival.

Do not allow a random side mission to permanently kill Royce before the locked endgame.

# 7. Player-facing escalation rail

The command UI should show intervention state without cluttering ordinary play.

Suggested rail:

```text
MISSION STATE      STABLE / DEGRADED / CRITICAL / TERMINAL
WILDCARD           2 / 2
APEX                3 / 3 CAMPAIGN
HOSTILE ESCALATION UNKNOWN / POSSIBLE / LIKELY / ACTIVE
```

When conditions are unmet, intervention controls remain hidden or disabled with a diegetic reason.

When CRITICAL begins, the UI may interrupt with:

```text
EXECUTIVE CHANNEL OPEN
WILDCARD REQUEST AVAILABLE
```

If Jordan is already present and the Apex requirements become true:

```text
CRITICAL FAILURE PERSISTS
APEX REQUEST AVAILABLE
```

Royce Incursion warnings depend on Picture/Intel quality. Strong reconnaissance may change `UNKNOWN` into `POSSIBLE`, identify his approach, or buy preparation time. It should not always prevent the incursion.

# 10. Grade and mastery interaction

Interventions should make the player **glad they used them**, not ashamed to touch the mechanic.

Therefore:

- Jordan intervention does not automatically cap the final mission grade;
- PRIM2 intervention does not automatically force a bad grade;
- the actual battlefield state still determines outcome grade;
- separate mastery ribbons record whether the operation was completed independently.

Possible ribbons:

```text
INDEPENDENT COMMAND
WILDCARD RECOVERY
APEX RECOVERY
SURVIVOR EXTRACTION
NO ONE LEFT
OBJECTIVE SALVAGED
ROYCE SURVIVED
ROYCE REPULSED
```

This separates **mission outcome** from **how much institutional help the player needed**.

# 11. Narrative integration

The intervention system must deepen the story rather than sit outside it.

Jordan call-ups create:
- resentment from operators who think he gets special treatment;
- relief from people he saved;
- pressure on Jordan to become everybody's emergency answer;
- evidence of his strange cross-lane competence;
- opportunities to hint at a past nobody fully understands.

PRIM2 call-ups create:
- awe;
- discomfort;
- questions about why the apex knew so much so quickly;
- dependency on a person the cast barely understands;
- later evidence that PRIM2 was observing far more than anyone realized.

Royce incursions create:
- dread before he appears;
- scars and relationship consequences after he leaves;
- old-history reactions from Jordan that become reverse-reveal breadcrumbs;
- a growing sense that Hitman is not merely another enemy faction but another board with its own elite human center.

An intervention must therefore write to the relationship/psychology continuity ledger.

# 13. Anti-protagonist-collapse rule

The mechanic must never become:

> play badly until Jordan arrives and does the cool part.

Therefore:

- normal characters receive the majority of tactical turns across the campaign;
- critical objectives remain solvable without Jordan in competent play;
- Jordan's best contribution is restoring options, not auto-solving LAB_NODEs;
- PRIM2 is vastly scarcer than Jordan;
- Royce ensures escalation can cut both ways;
- some missions lock Jordan out entirely;
- some story missions require specific other characters to make the decisive choice even after Jordan/PRIM2 arrives.

The intended feeling is:

> **I care about this roster enough to spend something precious when they are in real danger.**

That turns character attachment into strategy.

---

# 14. Governing rules

1. **Most missions launch without Jordan.**
2. **Jordan is a scarce rescue/escalation unit, not the default protagonist pawn.**
3. **PRIM2 is an even scarcer second-stage intervention available only on genuinely critical high-value missions after Jordan is already involved.**
4. **Royce gives the opposing board its own elite escalation threat.**
5. **Interventions operate on current state; they do not rewind consequences.**
6. **Named characters remain mechanically valuable before, during and after a Wildcard intervention.**
7. **The player should sometimes spend a scarce intervention to save a person rather than maximize the objective. That is a feature.**
8. **Every intervention creates campaign and relationship residue.**

Final shorthand:

> **Everybody gets to be the hero. Jordan is who they call when heroism is not enough. PRIM2 is who Jordan calls when even that is not enough. Royce is why sometimes none of it feels like enough.**
