# PRIM3 Adaptive Cyber Range Authority

## 1. Product definition

PRIM3 is one learning system expressed through four coordinated modalities:

1. **P · PRINCIPLES · Learn it.** Conventional instruction and assessment.
2. **R · RHYTHM · Remember it.** Music based recall and mnemonic reinforcement.
3. **I · IMMERSION · See it.** Story, episode, character, and consequence context.
4. **M · MISSIONS · Do it.** Turn based tactical application inside a browser based cyber range.

Principles is sufficient as the conventional instructional layer. Rhythm and Immersion reinforce learning. Missions create applied performance evidence. No modality may falsely award mastery owned by another modality.

## 2. Fixed instructional and story contracts

The Principles layer contains **66 modules**.

```text
M01 through M03
songless certification foundations

M04 through M66
21 canonical PRIM3 source units x 3 Principles modules each
```

The story remains:

```text
7 seasons
3 canonical episodes per season
21 canonical episodes
21 canonical source slots
21 canonical story Missions
```

M01 through M03 receive three noncanonical **Training Range** Missions. They do not create additional episodes, songs, or story canon.

The specialized authority for those three missions is `game/TRAINING-RANGE-AUTHORITY.md`.

Rhythm and Immersion may occur before, between, or after Principles lessons depending on mode. They may not substitute for required Principles work.

## 4. Mission Readiness Profile

Before a scored Mission, PRIM builds a concept level Mission Readiness Profile from available evidence.

Inputs may include:

1. Principles concept mastery.
2. Certification objective mastery.
3. Assessment score and attempts.
4. Prior Mission Technical Mastery.
5. Prior Mission Independence.
6. Prior Mission Transfer performance.
7. Rhythm reinforcement state.
8. Immersion context state.
9. Campaign and roster state.

A single aggregate quiz score is never the only adaptation signal.

## 5. Fair preparation law

Academic performance may change **preparedness, agency, and challenge**. It may not remove the minimum resources required for a fair solution.

Every adaptive Mission declares `minimum_required_toolset`.

A stronger learner may receive:

1. Preparation Credits.
2. Better initial intelligence confidence.
3. More route or loadout choice.
4. Additional diagnostic options.
5. Reduced instructional prompting.
6. More difficult optional objectives.
7. Earlier access to transfer variants.

A developing learner may receive:

1. The same minimum required tools.
2. More explicit task boundaries.
3. Additional coaching prompts.
4. More visible evidence provenance.
5. Reduced distractor density.
6. A standard objective rather than an elite optional objective.

The system may never create the loop `weak learner -> missing required tools -> harder failure -> less learning`.

## 6. PRIM modality feeders

### P · Principles

Principles owns academic instruction and conventional knowledge mastery.

Principles may influence starting technical intelligence, Preparation Credits, optional tool choice, Technical Challenge eligibility, and Scaffolding recommendations.

### R · Rhythm

Rhythm may create a limited **Recall** resource for relevant concepts.

Recall may surface a mnemonic, lyric cue, term, or doctrine reminder. It may not reveal the technical answer, execute a technical action, or award mastery by itself.

### I · Immersion

Immersion may create **Context Intel** that was legitimately established in story canon and would be available to the deployed team.

Context Intel may include system ownership, organizational stakes, character relationships, prior incident history, or consequence framing. It may not invent or reveal a hidden technical solution.

### M · Missions

Missions create applied performance evidence.

Mission completion alone does not imply concept mastery.

## 7. Two challenge axes plus Scaffolding

PRIM separates game skill from technical skill.

### Tactical Challenge

May tune enemy pressure, detection, reinforcement timing, mission clocks, Will pressure, environmental interruption, intervention scarcity, and recovery windows.

### Technical Challenge

May tune fault ambiguity, distractor quality, evidence completeness, concept integration, transfer distance, valid solution paths, and validation depth.

Canonical vocabulary:

```text
FOUNDATION
APPLIED
ADVANCED
TRANSFER
```

### Scaffolding

May tune prompt specificity, hint availability, evidence labeling, task decomposition, interface guidance, and explanation after reversible mistakes.

Canonical vocabulary:

```text
HIGH
STANDARD
LOW
NONE
```

Tactical Challenge remains independent from Technical Challenge. A harder tactical setting may not inflate Technical Mastery.

## 8. Four adaptive systems

Adaptive behavior is divided into four authorities.

### 8.1 Mission Variant Compiler

Runs before deployment.

It selects a legal Mission variant from authored and validated primitives using concept requirements, readiness, certification objective coverage, prior evidence, campaign state, Technical Challenge, and Scaffolding.

It may choose the approved fault variant, transfer demand, distractor density, starting intelligence quality, optional objectives, preparation advantages, and LAB_NODE adapter requirements.

It may not invent arbitrary third party targets or unvalidated technical primitives.

### 8.2 Mission Director

Runs during play.

The Director controls pacing and pressure, not hidden technical truth.

It may alter patrol timing, reinforcement timing, interruption pressure, environmental complications, temporary quiet, authored false or stale signals, and threat tempo.

It may not secretly change the correct technical answer after the learner acts correctly.

### 8.3 Threat AI

Threat AI controls hostile actors and hostile systems.

It may use only information those actors are entitled to know in the fictional world. It may not inspect learner quiz answers, hidden readiness scores, or grading data to cheat tactically.

Learner weakness can influence pre Mission variant selection. It does not give enemies supernatural knowledge during play.

### 8.4 Learning Evaluator

The Learning Evaluator scores evidence traces and produces at least:

1. Technical Mastery.
2. Independence.
3. Transfer.
4. Compliance.

The evaluator is logically separate from the Mission Variant Compiler.

Operational Grade remains a separate tactical result.

## 9. Stealth assessment and evidence traces

PRIM prefers embedded performance evidence over school style correctness interruptions.

Evidence traces may record whether the learner:

1. Observed relevant state before escalation.
2. Compared independent sources.
3. Assigned confidence correctly.
4. Chose an appropriate containment boundary.
5. Checked scope before change.
6. Preserved evidence before destructive remediation.
7. Applied a correct configuration.
8. Independently validated restoration.
9. Requested a hint.
10. Reversed a poor configuration.
11. Requested intervention.
12. Completed a required step without assistance.
13. Solved a changed transfer scenario.

Evidence must preserve enough provenance to explain a mastery decision later. Mastery cannot depend only on an opaque AI judgment.

## 10. Separate result channels

A Mission writes separate result channels:

```text
OPERATIONAL GRADE
TECHNICAL MASTERY
INDEPENDENCE
TRANSFER
COMPLIANCE
```

A learner may save the team while still requiring technical remediation.

A learner may demonstrate correct technical work while receiving a weaker Operational Grade because of exposure, casualties, delay, or unnecessary intervention use.

## 11. Browser tactical cyber range

The primary Missions client is browser first.

Preferred current application composition:

```text

+-- Rhythm player
+-- Immersion player
+-- Missions
    |
```

The preferred current tactical renderer is **PlayCanvas** because PRIM requires a browser native 3D or isometric tactical world that can coexist with ordinary web components.

Renderer choice is an implementation preference rather than permanent lore. Any replacement must preserve the shared mission contract.

## 12. One world, four semantic views

The learner should move from a physical tactical object into its technical interface without leaving the Mission context.

## 14. Live lab execution law

The browser never receives infrastructure credentials that can directly control the lab fleet.

```text
TACTICAL CLIENT
    |
    v
CLOUDFLARE CONTROLLED API
    |
    v
CORE ORCHESTRATOR
    |
    v
ISOLATED LAB COMPUTE
    |
    v
DETERMINISTIC VERIFIER
    |
    v
TRUSTED RESULT
    |
    v
TACTICAL RESOLVER + SUPABASE EVIDENCE
```

Cloudflare is the controlled edge.

Supabase is canonical learner and Mission memory.

The Core VPS is orchestration and persistent analysis.

Lab compute is disposable execution capacity and may scale independently from the Core VPS.

The VPS must not become a shadow learner database.

## 15. Lab isolation and recipe safety

Every live technical environment must be isolated from unauthorized third party systems.

A generated lab recipe may use only approved primitives and approved templates.

AI may propose a recipe. Deterministic infrastructure code must validate and compile that recipe before execution.

Approved primitive classes may include:

```text
CREATE_ISOLATED_ENVIRONMENT
CLONE_APPROVED_TEMPLATE
CREATE_PRIVATE_NETWORK
SET_APPROVED_CONFIGURATION_STATE
SEED_SYNTHETIC_LOG
PLACE_SYNTHETIC_EVIDENCE
CREATE_TRAINING_IDENTITY
SET_TRAINING_ACL
START_APPROVED_SERVICE
STOP_APPROVED_SERVICE
RESTORE_SNAPSHOT
DESTROY_ENVIRONMENT
```

The platform must not require live credential abuse, unauthorized exploitation, fraud, sabotage, or third party targeting.

## 16. Deterministic verification

Technical tasks are graded primarily by resulting state and evidence, not by an exact click path.

A verifier may inspect service state, routes, interfaces, DNS, DHCP, firewall state, account membership, permissions, evidence integrity, logs, monitoring state, recovery state, or application dependency health.

Multiple valid paths to a correct result should be accepted where the objective permits them.

`CONFIGURE` never automatically equals `VERIFY`.

## 17. Tactical coupling

Technical success changes the battlefield when the Mission fiction supports it.

Examples include:

1. Restore DNS, command systems regain trusted name resolution.
2. Restore authenticated wireless, the Picture becomes more reliable.
3. Restore access control, doors or checkpoints change state.
4. Contain malware, propagation pressure stops increasing.
5. Recover monitoring, threat warning quality improves.
6. Preserve evidence, later intelligence confidence improves.
7. Restore a continuity service, extraction or medical support becomes available.

Tactical pressure may affect technical work through access, safety, interruption, time, power, equipment damage, or evidence integrity.

## 19. Adaptive remediation loop

After a Mission, the Learning Evaluator writes concept level evidence back to the learner model.

The system may recommend:

1. A specific Principles section.
2. A certification objective review.
3. A Rhythm cue.
4. An Immersion scene.
5. A guided replay.
6. A changed transfer Mission.

Failure is evidence, not merely punishment.

Repeated successful transfer with low assistance should reduce future Scaffolding for that concept.

## 20. Research and analytics boundary

PRIM may compare learning outcomes across modality combinations when learner consent and applicable privacy rules permit that analysis.

Analytics must distinguish correlation from causation. Rhythm or Immersion participation may be recorded as instructional exposure, but the platform may not claim those modalities caused improvement unless the study design supports that conclusion.

## 22. Governing product law

> **Principles teaches the truth. Rhythm helps memory retrieve it. Immersion gives it human context. Missions require the learner to use it. The range records what the learner actually proved and feeds that evidence back into what they learn next.**
