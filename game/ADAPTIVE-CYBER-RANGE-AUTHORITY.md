# PRIM3 Adaptive Cyber Range Authority

## 1. Purpose

PRIM3 is not an LMS with a game attached to it. It is one learning system expressed through four coordinated modalities:

1. **P · PRINCIPLES · Learn it.** Conventional instruction and assessment.
2. **R · RHYTHM · Remember it.** Music based recall and mnemonic reinforcement.
3. **I · IMMERSION · See it.** Story, episode, character, and consequence context.
4. **M · MISSIONS · Do it.** Turn based tactical application inside a browser based cyber range.

The course must remain educationally valid even if a learner uses only Principles. Rhythm and Immersion reinforce learning. Missions create applied performance evidence.

No modality may falsely award mastery owned by another modality.

## 2. Fixed instructional and story contracts

The conventional learning layer contains **66 Principles modules**.

The first three modules are songless certification foundation modules. They exist to teach material needed before the 21 source units and to reduce cramming inside later modules.

The remaining 63 Principles modules are organized as three instructional modules for each of the 21 canonical PRIM3 source units.

The story contract remains:

```text
7 seasons
3 canonical episodes per season
21 canonical episodes
21 canonical songs or protected source slots
21 canonical story missions
```

The additional first three Principles modules do **not** create three new television episodes or songs.

They receive three noncanonical **Training Range** missions. Training Range missions are simulator exercises used to prove foundational practical skills before or alongside the canonical campaign.

## 3. Canonical learning loop

The governing learning loop is:

```text
P PRINCIPLES
instruction + conventional assessment
        |
        v
R RHYTHM
optional or assigned recall reinforcement
        |
        v
I IMMERSION
optional or assigned narrative reinforcement
        |
        v
MISSION READINESS PROFILE
        |
        v
M MISSIONS
turn based tactical cyber range
        |
        v
EVIDENCE TRACE
        |
        v
MASTERY + REMEDIATION
        |
        +--------------------> P PRINCIPLES review or next lesson
```

Rhythm and Immersion may occur before, between, or after Principles lessons depending on mode. Principles remains the instructional authority.

A learner may not substitute listening to a song or watching an episode for passing required Principles work.

## 4. Mission Readiness Profile

Before a scored mission, PRIM produces a **Mission Readiness Profile** from the learner's current evidence.

Inputs may include:

1. Principles concept mastery.
2. Principles objective mastery.
3. Assessment score and attempts.
4. Prior Mission technical mastery.
5. Prior Mission independence.
6. Prior Mission transfer performance.
7. Rhythm reinforcement state.
8. Immersion context state.
9. Campaign state and authorized roster state.

The profile is concept level where possible. A single aggregate quiz score is never the only mission adaptation signal.

Example conceptual profile:

```text
Identity                  94
Network troubleshooting   82
DNS                        68
Evidence preservation     91
Incident triage            77
Independence               73
Transfer                   81
Rhythm reinforced          yes
Immersion context          yes
```

## 5. Fair preparation law

Academic performance may change **preparedness, agency, and challenge**. It may not remove the minimum resources required for a fair solution.

Every generated mission must declare a `minimum_required_toolset`. The readiness system may add preparation advantages above that floor but may not remove required tools because a learner scored poorly.

A strong learner may receive:

1. Preparation Credits.
2. Better initial intelligence confidence.
3. Additional route or loadout choice.
4. An extra diagnostic tool.
5. Reduced instructional prompting.
6. More difficult optional objectives.
7. Earlier access to challenge variants.

A developing learner may receive:

1. The same minimum required technical tools.
2. More explicit task boundaries.
3. Additional coaching prompts.
4. More visible evidence provenance.
5. Reduced distractor density.
6. A standard objective rather than an elite optional objective.

The learning system must never create the loop `weak learner -> fewer required resources -> harder failure -> less learning`.

## 6. PRIM modality feeders

### P · Principles

Principles owns academic instruction and conventional knowledge mastery.

Principles may influence:

1. Starting technical intelligence.
2. Preparation Credits.
3. Tool choice above the required floor.
4. Technical challenge eligibility.
5. Scaffolding recommendations.

### R · Rhythm

Rhythm provides memory reinforcement.

A mission may convert qualifying Rhythm engagement into a limited **Recall** resource. Recall may replay or surface a mnemonic, lyric cue, term, or doctrine reminder.

Recall may not reveal the technical answer, execute a lab action, or award mastery by itself.

### I · Immersion

Immersion provides context.

A mission may convert qualifying story engagement into **Context Intel**, such as system ownership, organizational stakes, character relationships, previous incident history, or consequence framing.

Context Intel may not reveal a hidden technical answer unless that information was actually established in story canon and would be known to the deployed team.

### M · Missions

Missions provide applied performance evidence.

Mission completion alone does not imply concept mastery. The Learning Evaluator must inspect what the learner actually did.

## 7. Two independent challenge axes plus scaffolding

PRIM separates game skill from technical skill.

### Tactical Challenge

May tune:

1. Enemy aim and durability.
2. Detection pressure.
3. Reinforcement timing.
4. Mission clocks.
5. Will pressure.
6. Environmental interruption.
7. Intervention scarcity.
8. Recovery windows.

### Technical Challenge

May tune:

1. Number of relevant technical states.
2. Fault ambiguity.
3. Number and quality of distractors.
4. Evidence completeness.
5. Required concept integration.
6. Transfer distance from taught examples.
7. Number of valid solution paths.
8. Validation depth.

### Scaffolding

May tune:

1. Prompt specificity.
2. Hint availability.
3. Evidence labeling.
4. Task decomposition.
5. Interface guidance.
6. Explanation after reversible mistakes.

A learner can therefore play, for example:

```text
TACTICAL CHALLENGE: VETERAN
TECHNICAL CHALLENGE: FOUNDATION
SCAFFOLDING: HIGH
```

or:

```text
TACTICAL CHALLENGE: STANDARD
TECHNICAL CHALLENGE: ADVANCED
SCAFFOLDING: LOW
```

Technical mastery may not be increased merely because the player selected a harder tactical setting.

## 8. Four adaptive systems

Adaptive behavior is divided into four authorities. They may exchange state, but they must not collapse into one omniscient cheating agent.

### 8.1 Mission Variant Compiler

Runs before deployment.

It selects a legal mission variant from authored and validated primitives using:

1. Mission concept requirements.
2. Readiness profile.
3. Certification objective coverage.
4. Prior mission evidence.
5. Campaign state.
6. Technical challenge target.
7. Scaffolding target.

It may choose:

1. Which approved fault or incident variant is active.
2. Which concepts require transfer.
3. Distractor density.
4. Starting intelligence quality.
5. Optional objectives.
6. Permitted preparation advantages.
7. Which lab backend adapter is required.

It may not invent unsafe live targets, arbitrary third party systems, or unvalidated technical primitives.

### 8.2 Mission Director

Runs during play.

The Director controls pacing and pressure, not hidden technical truth.

It may schedule:

1. Patrol and reinforcement timing.
2. Interruption pressure.
3. Environmental complications.
4. Temporary quiet periods.
5. False or stale signals that are valid authored scenario elements.
6. Threat tempo.
7. Recoverable tactical complications.

The Director may not secretly change the correct technical answer after the learner acts correctly.

### 8.3 Threat AI

Threat AI controls individual hostile actors and hostile systems.

Threat AI may use only information it is entitled to know in the fictional world. It may not inspect learner quiz answers, hidden readiness scores, or grading data to cheat tactically.

A learner weakness may influence which scenario variant is selected before mission start. It does not give enemies supernatural knowledge during play.

### 8.4 Learning Evaluator

The Learning Evaluator scores evidence traces.

It is logically separate from the Mission Variant Compiler. The component that chooses a scenario must not be trusted to simply declare the learner successful.

The evaluator produces at least:

1. Technical Mastery.
2. Independence.
3. Transfer.
4. Compliance.

Operational Grade remains a separate tactical result.

## 9. Stealth assessment and evidence traces

PRIM prefers embedded performance evidence over interrupting tactical play with school style correctness popups.

Evidence traces may include:

1. Observation performed before escalation.
2. Independent sources compared.
3. Correct confidence state assigned.
4. Correct containment boundary chosen.
5. Scope checked before change.
6. Evidence preserved before destructive action.
7. Correct configuration applied.
8. Restoration independently validated.
9. Hint requested.
10. Misconfiguration reversed.
11. Wildcard requested.
12. Required step completed without assistance.
13. Changed scenario solved using the same concept.

Evidence records must include enough provenance to explain a mastery decision later.

The learner report should be explainable from recorded actions rather than an opaque AI judgment.

## 10. Operational Grade and learning evidence

A mission writes separate result channels:

```text
OPERATIONAL GRADE
TECHNICAL MASTERY
INDEPENDENCE
TRANSFER
COMPLIANCE
```

Examples:

A learner can save the team and earn an A operational grade while still requiring concept remediation.

A learner can demonstrate technically correct work but receive a weaker operational grade because of exposure, casualties, delay, or unnecessary intervention use.

This separation is mandatory.

## 11. Browser based tactical cyber range

The primary Missions client is browser first.

Preferred architecture:

```text
PRIM WEB APPLICATION
|
+-- Principles LMS
+-- Rhythm player
+-- Immersion player
+-- Missions
    |
    +-- Tactical renderer
    +-- PRIM command HUD
    +-- Systems View
    +-- Node View / Lab Workbench
```

The preferred tactical renderer is **PlayCanvas** because the current product requires a browser native 3D or isometric tactical world that can coexist with ordinary web interface components.

This is a preferred implementation choice, not a permanent lore dependency. A different renderer may replace it if it preserves the mission contract and shared resolver.

## 12. One world, four semantic views

The existing Omniscient Command Interface remains authoritative:

1. Area View.
2. Tactical View.
3. Systems View.
4. Node View.

Node View is where a LAB_NODE may expose a real technical environment.

A learner should be able to move from a physical tactical object into its real or simulated technical interface without leaving the mission context.

## 13. LAB_NODE backend adapters

Every substantive LAB_NODE declares an adapter type.

Supported design classes include:

1. `SIMULATION`, deterministic browser simulation.
2. `TERMINAL`, browser terminal attached to an isolated environment.
3. `REMOTE_DESKTOP`, browser streamed Windows or Linux desktop.
4. `NETWORK_RANGE`, isolated network topology and device consoles.
5. `PACKET_ANALYSIS`, packet capture and analysis workspace.
6. `SIEM`, isolated monitoring and investigation workspace.
7. `HARDWARE_SIM`, browser simulation for hardware concepts that cannot be safely virtualized.
8. `DOCUMENT_EVIDENCE`, evidence, scope, policy, or chain of custody workspace.

Candidate implementation adapters include xterm.js for terminal presentation, Apache Guacamole for browser remote desktop or SSH presentation, and GNS3 for isolated network topologies. These are implementation adapters, not canonical learner brands.

## 14. Live lab execution law

The tactical client never receives infrastructure credentials that can directly control the lab fleet.

The flow is:

```text
TACTICAL CLIENT
    |
    v
CLOUDFLARE CONTROLLED API
    |
    v
CORE ORCHESTRATOR
    |
    v
ISOLATED LAB COMPUTE
    |
    v
DETERMINISTIC VERIFIER
    |
    v
SIGNED OR TRUSTED RESULT
    |
    v
TACTICAL RESOLVER + SUPABASE EVIDENCE
```

Cloudflare is the controlled edge.

Supabase is canonical learner and mission memory.

The Core VPS is orchestration and persistent analysis.

Lab compute is disposable execution capacity and may scale independently from the Core VPS.

The VPS must not become a shadow learner database.

## 15. Lab isolation and safety

Every live technical environment must be isolated from unauthorized third party systems.

A generated lab recipe may use only approved primitives and approved templates.

AI may propose a recipe, but deterministic infrastructure code validates and compiles that recipe before execution.

Examples of approved primitive classes include:

```text
CREATE_ISOLATED_ENVIRONMENT
CLONE_APPROVED_TEMPLATE
CREATE_PRIVATE_NETWORK
SET_APPROVED_CONFIGURATION_STATE
SEED_SYNTHETIC_LOG
PLACE_SYNTHETIC_EVIDENCE
CREATE_TRAINING_IDENTITY
SET_TRAINING_ACL
START_APPROVED_SERVICE
STOP_APPROVED_SERVICE
RESTORE_SNAPSHOT
DESTROY_ENVIRONMENT
```

The platform must not require live credential abuse, unauthorized exploitation, fraud, sabotage, or third party targeting.

## 16. Deterministic verification

A technical task is graded primarily by resulting state and evidence, not by whether the learner clicked an exact sequence of buttons.

A verifier may inspect, where appropriate:

1. Service state.
2. Route or interface state.
3. DNS or DHCP state.
4. Firewall state.
5. Account and group membership.
6. Permissions.
7. Evidence integrity.
8. Log or monitoring state.
9. Backup or recovery state.
10. Application or dependency health.

Multiple valid paths to a correct state should be accepted when the objective permits them.

`CONFIGURE` never automatically equals `VERIFY`.

## 17. Tactical coupling

Technical success must change the battlefield when the mission fiction supports it.

Examples:

1. Restore DNS, command systems regain trusted name resolution.
2. Restore authenticated wireless, the Picture becomes more reliable.
3. Restore access control, doors or checkpoints change state.
4. Contain malware, propagation pressure stops increasing.
5. Recover monitoring, threat warning quality improves.
6. Preserve evidence, later intelligence or debrief confidence improves.
7. Restore a continuity service, extraction or medical support becomes available.

Tactical pressure may also affect technical work through access, safety, interruption, time, power, equipment damage, or evidence integrity.

## 18. Training Range missions

Before or alongside the canonical campaign, PRIM exposes three noncanonical simulator missions corresponding to the first three Principles foundation modules.

### Training Range 01 · Observe and Verify

Primary skills: events, alerts, telemetry, baselines, triage, confidence, corroboration, validation.

### Training Range 02 · Scope and Evidence

Primary skills: authorization, least privilege, scope boundaries, evidence provenance, preservation, documentation.

### Training Range 03 · Infrastructure Under Pressure

Primary skills: endpoints, identity, networks, services, monitoring, dependency reasoning, restoration validation.

These missions may use synthetic training operators or explicitly simulator framed versions of known systems. They do not create new episode canon.

## 19. Adaptive remediation loop

After a Mission, the Learning Evaluator writes concept level evidence back to the learner model.

The system may then recommend:

1. A specific Principles section.
2. A specific objective review.
3. A Rhythm cue.
4. An Immersion scene.
5. A guided replay.
6. A changed transfer mission.

A failed mission is therefore evidence, not merely punishment.

Repeated successful transfer with low assistance should reduce future scaffolding for that concept.

## 20. Research and analytics boundary

PRIM may compare learning outcomes across modality combinations, for example Principles only versus Principles plus Rhythm, or Principles plus Immersion, when learner consent and applicable privacy rules permit that analysis.

Product analytics must distinguish correlation from causation. Participation in Rhythm or Immersion may be tracked as an instructional exposure, but it may not be described as causing improvement unless the study design supports that conclusion.

## 22. Governing product law

> **Principles teaches the truth. Rhythm helps memory retrieve it. Immersion gives it human context. Missions require the learner to use it. The range records what the learner actually proved and feeds that evidence back into what they learn next.**
