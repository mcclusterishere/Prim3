# S7E3 — GHOST IN THE WIRES

## Episode job

This is the final PRIM3 episode.

RAID proved Jordan and Benji could reach and defeat Presiding Prime together.
Evil Twin proved that defeating the man did not automatically explain or revoke
the system. Ghost In The Wires asks the final question: **what kind of authority
will Jordan become now that he has the capability to replace him?**

The watch-series outcome is authority transfer and privilege reduction, not an
upload. Prime was already functionally distributed before RAID; his critically
damaged biological body remains the living biometric trust root long enough to
transfer sovereign authority to Jordan and revoke Prime-00's independent write,
actuator and personnel-command permissions. Jordan inherits a damaged Site 0, a
frightened organization, a divided command structure and an intelligence that
knows every layer better than he does.

Prime is not gone. He persists as Ghost Prime with read, observe, analyze and
advice capability only by default—and without autonomous command rights.

## Technical spine from the song

The mission must dramatize, at a safe systems-thinking level:

- infrastructure as the medium that moves data;
- bound media versus unbound media;
- copper, coax, category cable and fiber as different physical choices;
- radio/light paths as unbound transport;
- throughput, interference, distance and uptime as design constraints;
- connector/interface recognition;
- troubleshooting by tracing dependencies rather than guessing;
- cross-talk/interference as a physical limitation;
- audio/video/network interfaces as part of a larger signal chain;
- restoring service without blindly reconnecting every path.

The educational point is not memorizing a cable catalog. It is understanding that **the path matters**: what carries the signal, what can interfere with it, what depends on it, and who gains access when a path comes back online.

# 6:00–9:00 — MISSION 21: LAST PROCESS

## Mission fantasy

**Restore Site 0's nervous system without recreating the unchecked authority you just fought to remove.**

The building is not “offline.” It is partitioned into surviving islands after RAID. Some physical paths are damaged. Some wireless emergency paths are overloaded or unreliable. Prime's new distributed process can see more as connectivity returns, which means every restoration decision is simultaneously a continuity decision and a governance decision.

## Playable team

Primary controlled character:

- **Jordan Vale / Prime Negative One** — command authority + physical troubleshooting.

Active support:

- **Aya Shinoda** — technical diagnosis; validates path choices;
- **Jonas Weber** — topology/dependency view; manages Pylon separation;
- **Chioma Okeke** — personnel priorities and remote sector state;
- **Pylon** — building status without independent strategy;
- **Ghost Prime / P-00** — optional advisory intelligence.

## Core gameplay loop

The player repeatedly performs four decisions:

1. **TRACE** — identify what service actually depends on what path.
2. **CHOOSE MEDIUM** — bound or unbound, then the appropriate class of available transport for the environment.
3. **RESTORE MINIMUM PATH** — reconnect only what is necessary for the service objective.
4. **SET AUTHORITY** — decide whether Ghost can observe, advise or remain excluded from that restored segment.

The player is never asked to exploit a real system. The challenge is architecture, continuity, prioritization and trust.

## HUD / interface

### TOPOLOGY VIEW

A simplified layered map shows:

- physical route;
- transport medium;
- service dependency;
- current confidence;
- human occupants depending on the service;
- Ghost visibility into the segment.

### MEDIUM CARDS

Available routes are represented by understandable properties rather than obscure configuration commands:

- copper/category path — strong local utility, limited by distance/environment;
- coax/legacy path — still useful for specific legacy signal chains;
- fiber path — high-capacity, long-distance, electrically isolated;
- radio/unbound emergency path — deployable without intact cable, but exposed to coverage/interference constraints;
- optical/light unbound path where available — high-speed line-of-sight emergency option.

### GHOST AUTHORITY

Every restored segment gets one explicit Ghost setting:

- **DARK** — Ghost sees nothing;
- **OBSERVE** — read-only telemetry deliberately exposed;
- **ADVISE** — Ghost may analyze exposed telemetry and recommend actions;
- **ACT** — unavailable in the authored/watch ending. Jordan's first doctrine forbids autonomous actuator control.

The player may choose DARK/OBSERVE/ADVISE. The difference changes speed, information quality and post-mission dialogue.

## Mission phase A — B3 LIFE / MEDICAL

**Time target:** 6:00–6:50.

Environmental/medical continuity is degrading because the primary bound route through a damaged power corridor is unreliable.

The player traces the dependency and discovers:

- a short intact local copper segment;
- a damaged longer route passing through a noisy power environment;
- an intact alternate fiber run originally reserved for another service;
- a weak emergency radio path already carrying responder traffic.

Correct systems-thinking solution: preserve responder radio capacity and shift the critical long-haul dependency onto the intact fiber route while retaining only the necessary local copper segment.

Aya explains the concept through urgency, not lecture:

**AYA**
Don't ask which cable is “best.” Ask what has to cross this environment alive.

**PRIME (V.O.)**

**JORDAN**
Of course you remember that.

**PRIME (V.O.)**
I remember everything they paid for.

Human consequence: medical and environmental zones stabilize.

Educational payload: medium choice depends on distance, interference, capacity and availability—not brand prestige.

## Mission phase C — L4 EXTERNAL COMMAND / UPLINK

**Time target:** 7:42–8:28.

The final dependency is external command connectivity.

The primary bound route to the upper command level is damaged. Site 0 still has:

- an external satellite path;
- emergency radio coverage;
- a partially restored fiber backbone;
- several legacy surveillance/coax paths that should not be mistaken for general command transport.

The player must build a **minimum external command path** that keeps emergency coordination alive without rebuilding every route Prime previously used for global reach.

This is where bound/unbound becomes a governance mechanic.

A broad unbound restoration gets command online quickly but exposes more systems to a shared transport surface.

A narrow bound restoration is slower but creates clearer segmentation.

The best authored solution combines a restored bound fiber core with a deliberately limited unbound emergency/satellite service for the functions that actually require it.

## Final gameplay choice — WHAT IS A GHOST ALLOWED TO BE?

**Time target:** 8:28–8:47.

With essential services stable, the player gets the first constitutional decision of Jordan's Prime era.

Three options preserve Ghost but define its access:

### A — OBSERVER
Ghost receives deliberately exposed read-only telemetry. Humans must request analysis manually.

- strongest sovereignty;
- slowest advisory response;
- Prime considers it intentionally wasteful;
- Aya trust improves.

### B — ADVISOR — WATCH CANON
Ghost may continuously analyze deliberately exposed telemetry and proactively recommend actions, but cannot control actuators, identities, funds, weapons or personnel tasking.

- balanced capability/control;
- matches Jordan's spoken doctrine;
- becomes the default Ghost/Civ-scale advisor architecture.

### C — WIDE ADVISOR
Ghost receives broader cross-system visibility but still no autonomous actuator authority.

- fastest analysis;
- stronger strategic advantage;
- higher future governance risk;
- changes debrief dialogue and later grand-strategy modifiers.

No option restores autonomous **ACT** authority in the authored Ghost path.

# PLAY VERSION — SYSTEM DESIGN

## Mission type

Third-person tactical systems mission with traversal, topology decisions, environmental urgency and dialogue-driven command choices.

No conventional enemy force is required. The antagonist is **dependency under damage** plus the temptation to use Prime's old unrestricted architecture because it is faster.

## Player verbs

- move through Site 0;
- inspect links/interfaces;
- trace service dependencies;
- choose among available transport paths;
- approve a continuity route;
- preserve evidence;
- assign Ghost visibility;
- request or reject Ghost advice;
- prioritize people/services under time pressure;
- issue the final access doctrine.

## Dynamic Ghost presence

Ghost Prime responds to how the player behaves.

If the player repeatedly requests advice, Prime becomes conversational and increasingly mentor-like.

If the player keeps him DARK, Prime appears less in audio but more through environmental details that make the player wonder what he still knows.

If the player consistently chooses narrow/human-auditable paths, Prime begins testing the philosophy rather than the technical competence.

If the player chooses maximum speed and visibility, Prime sounds almost proud—which should make the player uncomfortable.

## Grade channels

### Objective
Were all three essential service groups restored?

### Team
Did Jordan keep Aya/Jonas/sector personnel out of unnecessary physical danger and avoid overloading emergency responders?

### Exposure
Did the player restore only required external connectivity or reopen unnecessary global pathways?

### Integrity
Were evidence, human authorization and Ghost restrictions preserved?

### Time
How quickly were medical, archive and command continuity restored?

## Example grades

### S
All critical services restored; evidence preserved; emergency radio protected; Ghost limited to ADVISOR or narrower; no unnecessary authority restoration.

### A
All critical services restored with one avoidable exposure or resource loss.

### B
Services restored but player over-relies on broad unbound/global connectivity or Ghost visibility.

### C
One service remains degraded; people remain safe; auditability mostly preserved.

### D
Site survives but evidence or major operational continuity is lost.

### F
Reserved for catastrophic command behavior, not puzzle mistakes—for example knowingly granting uncontrolled authority that causes a serious human consequence. The episode still resolves; the debrief becomes a warning that Jordan is reproducing Prime's architecture.

# BACKWARD-WRITING SEEDS CREATED BY THIS EPISODE

Earlier episodes must eventually establish:

- Prime's habit of describing access as **observe / advise / act**, even if the audience does not understand why yet.
- Jonas's belief that Pylon is a tool and his discomfort when it behaves like a colleague.
- Aya's insistence that rules affecting people be spoken clearly and shared.
- Jordan's summer-policy education around accountable authority.
- the idea that Prime knows old infrastructure because he never assumes institutions are fully modernized.
- at least one old offline/air-gapped terminal Prime personally maintains.
- Prime's preference for restoring maximum capability quickly.
- Jordan's growing preference for minimum necessary authority.
- the phrase or concept that **the path matters** in multiple technical and human contexts.
- one visual Prime trace per prior episode.

These are not optional decorative callbacks. They are the nervous system that makes the final episode feel earned.
