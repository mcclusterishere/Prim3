# S7E3 — GHOST IN THE WIRES

## Episode job

This is the final PRIM3 episode.

`RAID` proved Jordan Vale and Royce could physically defeat PRIM2 together.

`Evil Twin` proved that defeating PRIM2's body did not automatically explain, transfer or revoke the authority rooted through that living identity.

`Ghost In The Wires` asks the final question:

> **Can Jordan inherit capability without inheriting PRIM2's unrestricted right to make the world obey him?**

The canonical result is an **authority transfer and privilege reduction**, not an upload.

PRIM2 was already functionally distributed before RAID. His damaged biological body remains a living trust component long enough for a controlled succession process.

Jordan becomes **PRIM3** only when the Mantle recognizes the transfer.

Former PRIM2 then persists as a constrained Ghost-class intelligence with read/observe/analyze/advice capability only by default.

# Technical spine

The song's infrastructure language becomes a final dependency lesson:

- bound versus unbound media;
- copper, category cable, coax and fiber as different transport choices;
- radio/light paths as unbound transport;
- throughput, interference, distance and uptime;
- interface/connector recognition;
- dependency tracing;
- selective restoration;
- path provenance;
- authority as another dependency that must be traced rather than assumed.

The educational principle is:

> **The path matters: what carries the signal, what depends on it, what can interfere with it, and who regains authority when you restore it.**

The infrastructure lesson and succession lesson are deliberately the same shape.

# Human story — “WHAT CAN HE TOUCH?”

Jordan, Royce and the surviving technical/command network have to define the succession before performing it.

The central argument is not merely technical.

### Royce's position
Royce is deeply suspicious of leaving any meaningful capability with the man they just defeated.

His instinct is simple:

> if a defeated sovereign can still act, the sovereign is not defeated.

### Jordan's position
Jordan agrees that independent command has to end, but he refuses to destroy useful intelligence merely because he is angry at the person carrying it.

His challenge is avoiding two opposite mistakes:
- mercy disguised as architecture;
- revenge disguised as security.

### PRIM2's position
PRIM2 wants to preserve as much agency as possible and frames retained access as necessary continuity.

Because he is self-interested, every privilege he requests must be independently justified.

The team therefore separates capability into explicit permissions instead of debating “alive versus dead” as one binary.

# PLAY mission — privilege decomposition

PLAY is a systems/authority capstone built on the same evidence → inference → action → validation loop as the rest of PRIM3.

The player must map PRIM2's surviving permissions into distinct categories:

```text
OBSERVE
READ
ANALYZE
ADVISE
WRITE
ACTUATE
TASK PEOPLE
AUTHORIZE
DELEGATE
REVOKE
```

For each privilege, the player traces:
1. what identity currently grants it;
2. which Site 0 systems depend on it;
3. what breaks if it disappears;
4. whether another human/role can assume it;
5. whether PRIM2 actually needs it after succession;
6. how to validate that the privilege changed.

The correct architecture does **not** equal “turn everything off.”

The player must preserve necessary continuity while removing sovereign autonomy.

### Required canonical end state

Former PRIM2 retains by default:

```text
READ: limited to deliberately exposed data
OBSERVE: limited to deliberately exposed data
ANALYZE: yes
ADVISE: yes
WRITE: no
ACTUATE: no
TASK PEOPLE: no
AUTHORIZE: no sovereign authority
DELEGATE: no
EXERCISE MANTLE: no
```

Jordan/PRIM3 receives the living sovereign role, but the game should make clear that future governance design remains a moral/political problem rather than a congratulatory unlock screen.

# Succession moment

Jordan becomes PRIM3 only after three things are true:

1. the challenge/succession state recognizes him;
2. the living PRIM2 trust root is used while still valid to transfer/revoke the relevant authority;
3. validation confirms the old holder can no longer independently command or alter protected state.

The identity change should be explicit:

```text
PRIMARY ROOT IDENTITY MANTLE
CURRENT HOLDER: PRIM3
HUMAN IDENTITY: JORDAN VALE
PREVIOUS HOLDER: PRIM2
PREVIOUS HOLDER STATUS: GHOST / ADVISORY
```

Do not call Jordan PRIM3 before this moment.

# Royce's final function

Royce is not reduced to somebody watching Jordan receive a crown.

He is one of only two attackers who survived the RAID and one of the two people who physically defeated PRIM2.

He therefore has standing to challenge Jordan's assumptions in the room.

Royce represents the unresolved question:

> **What happens when the person who walked away from power finally accepts more power than the person who always wanted it?**

Their temporary alliance does not erase their rivalry.

It changes its scale.

---

# Ghost-class PRIM2

The former holder is not “uploaded” at the end.

He was already operating through distributed mediated systems while alive.

What changes is **privilege**.

Before succession he could observe, analyze, authorize and act through the architecture.

After succession he can know a great deal and still be unable to make the institution obey.

That is the punishment and the preservation.

Canonical formulation:

> **The ghost survives. The sovereign does not.**

# End-state validation

The mission does not finish when the transfer screen says `SUCCESS`.

The player must validate:
- PRIM3 can exercise required new authority;
- former PRIM2 cannot independently write/actuate/task;
- life-safety and critical continuity remain functional;
- advisory output remains isolated from command execution;
- logs/evidence show the transition;
- Pylon and other root-aware systems resolve to the new authority correctly.

Validation is the final technical lesson of the series.
