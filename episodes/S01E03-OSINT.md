# S1E3 — OSINT

## Episode job

This is the Season One finale.

S1E2 proved Jordan can respect an access boundary even when he knows how to cross it. S1E3 removes almost every privileged tool and asks a harder question:

**Can Jordan build a defensible truth from information nobody has assembled for him?**

The assignment begins with a missing McCluster equipment shipment and an official explanation that looks plausible: routine carrier delay.

Jordan is given no internal investigative access, no private credentials and no protected system. He must work from lawful public sources, owner-provided documents and information he is legitimately entitled to use as a student/intern.

The episode teaches that OSINT is not “anything you can find on the internet.” It is disciplined collection and interpretation:

- public sources;
- provenance;
- corroboration;
- confidence levels;
- distinguishing fact from inference;
- separating public information from private or proprietary material;
- using authorized subscription/library resources without pretending they are public;
- vulnerability/advisory databases as defensive public intelligence;
- indicators as clues, not automatic proof;
- chain of custody and why recovered hardware is not automatically trusted hardware.

The human story closes Season One's theme of **recognition**. Jordan thinks he is proving he deserves to keep an internship. Prime is deciding whether Jordan deserves to stay on a much longer path.

# 6:00–9:00 — MISSION 03: OPEN BOOK

## Mission fantasy

**Turn scattered lawful information into a defensible operational picture before McCluster's property disappears into a liquidation sale.**

The player never breaks into a private system.

The challenge is deciding:

- which source deserves weight;
- which claim needs corroboration;
- when evidence supports only inference;
- when the team has enough confidence to act through lawful ownership/recovery channels;
- how a broken chain of custody changes what happens after recovery.

## Playable perspective

Primary:

- Jordan Vale — evidence assembly and confidence judgments.

Support:

- Aya Shinoda — challenges unsupported inference;
- Amira Hassan — mission sponsor / escalation authority;
- authorized recovery team — appears only after Jordan's evidence meets threshold.

Hidden observer:

- Presiding Prime — later reviews the same board Jordan built.

## Core gameplay loop

Every clue must be processed through four questions:

1. **SOURCE** — where did this come from?
2. **CLAIM** — what does it actually say?
3. **CORROBORATION** — what independent source supports or contradicts it?
4. **CONFIDENCE** — fact, strong inference, weak inference or unresolved?

The player earns more by refusing overclaim than by filling every blank.

# WATCH CANON RESULT

Jordan correctly locates the missing equipment through a lawful evidence chain, avoids overclaiming criminal intent, and insists the recovered hardware be treated as untrusted until validated because custody was broken.

The episode closes the Season One recognition arc:

- McCluster recognizes Jordan's practical judgment;
- Aya recognizes that his improvisation can coexist with evidence discipline;
- Prime recognizes that Jordan can distinguish suspicion from proof;
- Jordan experiences institutional membership as something that can provide legitimate access rather than only deny it;
- Prime quietly creates the next developmental pressure by routing Jordan toward public-policy exposure.

  1. access is not authorization;
  2. capability is not proof;

They now have a recognizable rhythm:

- Jordan generates hypotheses quickly;
- Aya attacks the weak assumptions;
- Jordan improves the claim instead of merely defending his ego;

## Prime

- Has now watched Jordan make three different decisions under pressure:
  - S1E1: survives institutional classification as disposable;
  - S1E2: respects authorization despite personal cost;
  - S1E3: refuses to convert suspicion into certainty simply because certainty would make him look smarter.
