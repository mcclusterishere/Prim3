# S1E2 — WHITE GREY BLACK HAT / PERMISSION SLIP

## Episode promise

Episode 2 is the audience's first full look at ordinary PRIM life after the future shock of `High Alert`.

It must prove four things immediately:

1. PRIM3 is an ensemble workplace, not Jordan plus assistants;
2. technical competence and authorization are separate forms of power;
3. ordinary ethical process can be dramatically difficult even before the hidden PRIM2 conspiracy becomes visible;
4. PLAY is a real tactical field operation, not an internal-menu minigame.

The internal Jordan access problem is **Act I setup**.

The actual mission is an off-site lawful task-force operation at a fictional multi-tenant edge/data facility where the team possesses strong legal authority but incomplete system knowledge — **white hat / black box**.

Canonical thematic line:

> **Capability is not permission. Permission is not knowledge. Knowledge is not innocence or guilt.**

# Source-song concept authority

The song distinguishes two different dimensions:

### HAT
The operator's intent/authorization relationship to the work.

### BOX
How much prior system knowledge the operator possesses.

The episode therefore teaches that the following combinations are possible:

```text
WHITE HAT + WHITE BOX
Authorized and deeply informed.

WHITE HAT + GREY BOX
Authorized with partial system knowledge.

WHITE HAT + BLACK BOX
Authorized but entering with little reliable system knowledge.

UNAUTHORIZED + DEEP KNOWLEDGE
Still unauthorized.

AUTHORIZED + HIGH COMPETENCE
Still constrained by scope.
```

The mission deliberately begins as **WHITE HAT / BLACK BOX**.

# Longform WATCH structure

## ACT I — THE BUILDING HAS RULES (approx. 0:00–8:00)

### Cold open — ordinary friction

Morning inside the PRIM/McCluster building.

Nusrat and Miguel are already working access before Jordan arrives.

Use the opening to establish workplace recurrence rather than exposition:
- Miguel knows which staff always forget something;
- Priya is already moving people around for cohort onboarding;
- Maren wants final names before she publishes anything internally;
- Pylon announces access/elevator/status information with boring consistency;
- Chioma passes through and notices more than she says;
- Aya is collecting/testing issued technical equipment.

Nusrat stops a small routine exception before Jordan ever appears. It should not be dramatic. The point is that she applies the same rule to familiar people.

### Jordan's manifest discrepancy

Jordan arrives for formal onboarding.

One diagnostic/security appliance in his issued equipment does **not** appear on his manifest.

The appliance is not stolen and Jordan did not request the mismatch.

He knows enough from prior infrastructure work to understand what the tool could probably do.

He also knows he has not been told he is permitted to use it.

Jordan brings the discrepancy forward himself.

This creates the first tension:
- Priya's schedule slips;
- Maren needs to know whether this is an embarrassing onboarding mistake or a real security problem;
- Aya can identify the device class but cannot establish issuance authority;
- Miguel keeps the interaction human;
- Nusrat places the device into safe custody and refuses to wave Jordan through with it.

### Executive temptation

Someone asks Helen whether she can simply authorize the exception.

Helen asks Nusrat:

> **Would you authorize him if I were not asking?**

Nusrat says no.

Helen's answer:

> **Then I'm not asking.**

The audience receives an important baseline: visible authority is not automatically corrupt.

### Jordan/Aya micro-beat

Aya expects Jordan to push harder because he can explain exactly why the tool might be useful.

Jordan gives her the technical hypothesis and then stops.

He does **not** translate “I know” into “therefore I decide.”

Aya remembers that.

## ACT II — THE CALL (approx. 8:00–12:00)

While onboarding is still being repaired, McCluster receives an urgent field-support request from a fictional regional cybercrime/task-force partner.

### Mission site

**Harborline Edge Exchange** — a fictional multi-tenant edge/data facility serving unrelated commercial and civic customers.

A lawful task force is executing a narrowly scoped seizure/search against one tenant suspected of using specific equipment for serious criminal operations.

PRIM's field assignment is not “hack the building.”

It is to:
- help identify the specified assets;
- preserve evidence;
- maintain unrelated tenant/service continuity where possible;
- provide technical interpretation under task-force authority;
- stop physical interference with the authorized operation;
- document anything that requires a scope change rather than silently expanding the mission.

### Why Nusrat leads

The case is fundamentally about **scope under uncertainty**.

Chioma/command selects Nusrat as mission lead because she just demonstrated she will not bend an access boundary because somebody important is impatient.

This is both a reward and a pressure test.

### Why Jordan stays

Jordan asks whether he is going.

Answer: **No.**

His field clearance is not complete.

That matters.

The story has just taught the audience that Jordan can help and still be denied.

He accepts it.

No Wildcard button exists yet.

### Mission team

Playable:
- Nusrat — scope/access/mission lead;
- Miguel — physical control/protection/human coordination;
- Aya — technical characterization/validation.

Chioma remains command-side support.
Helen owns executive/governance escalation.

# ACT III — FIELD OPERATION / PLAY (approx. 15:00–27:00 WATCH; 25–45 min PLAY)

## Battlefield

Harborline Edge Exchange is a compact XCOM-style tactical map with distinct control/scope zones:

```text
ZONE A — public/service lobby
ZONE B — loading/service corridor
ZONE C — authorized target tenant cage
ZONE D — shared cross-connect / service room
ZONE E — adjacent tenant cages
ZONE F — power/environmental support corridor
ZONE G — evidence/extraction lane
```

Not every zone is legally equivalent.

### Starting authorization state

```text
ZONE A: AUTHORIZED
ZONE B: AUTHORIZED
ZONE C: AUTHORIZED
ZONE D: OBSERVE-ONLY pending demonstrated necessity
ZONE E: OUT-OF-SCOPE
ZONE F: EMERGENCY-ELIGIBLE for life/safety only
ZONE G: AUTHORIZED
```

The player must care about both tactical geography and authority geography.

---

# Mission phase 1 — SECURE THE WINDOW

The task force has gained initial site control but cannot guarantee the interior remains stable.

The PRIM team enters behind the first physical sweep.

Player jobs:
- establish a safe path to Zone C;
- keep facility staff/civilians out of contested lanes;
- confirm the target cage and specified asset identities;
- prevent physical removal/destruction of target evidence;
- maintain chain/provenance state.

Miguel creates/holds physical Control.
Nusrat verifies scope and identity.
Aya begins technical characterization.

This is the first time the audience sees the song's ideas become simultaneous tactical pressures.

---

# Mission phase 2 — THE WRONG EASY ANSWER

Aya discovers that one target device appears to have a live dependency extending beyond the tenant cage.

The task-force paperwork identifies the physical asset correctly.

The system relationship around the asset is incomplete.

Simply isolating/seizing it may interrupt an unrelated live service used by another tenant.

The easiest tactical answer is:

> pull/seize the asset now.

The correct professional answer is:

> **we do not yet know enough to predict the blast radius.**

This changes the mission from:

```text
WHITE HAT / BLACK BOX
```

into a decision about whether and how the team can earn enough knowledge to act without silently exceeding authorization.

---

# Mission phase 3 — HOSTILE RECOVERY PRESSURE

A hostile recovery element tied to the target tenant attempts to reach/remove/destroy specified evidence during the uncertainty window.

Keep adversary implementation fictional and abstract.

Their purpose is to create a genuine XCOM battlefield problem:
- contested corridors;
- threatened evidence;
- civilian displacement;
- time pressure;
- cover/positioning decisions;
- opportunity cost between fighting, verifying and preserving the objective.

Miguel cannot simply chase every hostile because Aya needs a protected technical window.

Aya cannot finish characterization if Control collapses.

Nusrat cannot casually expand scope just because combat makes patience inconvenient.

The three jobs become mechanically interdependent.

---

# Mission phase 4 — SCOPE ENVELOPE

Evidence indicates the shared service room (Zone D) can confirm the dependency without entering unrelated tenant cages.

Zone D is `OBSERVE-ONLY` under the initial authorization.

Nusrat must decide whether the evidence justifies a narrow extension request.

The player chooses between:

### PATH A — DOCUMENTED EXTENSION
Spend mission time and preserve Compliance/Integrity to request a bounded scope expansion for the exact observation/validation task.

### PATH B — NON-INVASIVE ALTERNATIVE
Use already-authorized observations and existing telemetry to attempt a slower/noisier corroboration path.

### PATH C — EMERGENCY EXCEPTION
Available only if life/safety conditions become genuinely imminent. Faster, but creates mandatory review and cannot be used merely to save mission grade.

### PATH D — UNAUTHORIZED ACTION
The UI permits deliberate violation only when physically possible, but records it as an explicit Compliance breach with story/campaign consequences. It is never mislabeled as clever play.

This is where “permission” becomes gameplay instead of dialogue.

---

# Mission phase 5 — PROVE, THEN MOVE

Once enough evidence exists, the team learns the safe action is **not** the original easy action.

The technical objective resolves through a bounded, validated intervention that:
- preserves the specified evidence;
- keeps unrelated services operating if the player earned the correct picture;
- keeps out-of-scope tenant assets untouched;
- allows the task force to complete its lawful objective.

`CONFIGURE/ISOLATE/SEIZE` is not enough.

The mission ends only after the player performs a separate `VERIFY` stage.

# Game-state objectives

## Primary

1. identify the authorized target assets;
2. preserve evidence integrity;
3. prevent hostile removal/destruction of the target evidence;
4. resolve the target dependency without unjustified out-of-scope action;
5. extract the team/evidence.

## Secondary

- zero civilian casualties;
- zero unrelated-tenant disruption;
- no unauthorized Zone E access;
- preserve full chain/provenance record;
- keep Service Health above mission threshold;
- complete without Emergency Exception.

---

# Failure / consequence states

There should be very few binary mission-failure conditions.

Possible continuing outcomes include:

### CLEAN
Target secured, unrelated service preserved, evidence defensible, no scope breach.

### LAWFUL BUT COSTLY
Target secured, legal process preserved, but service or timetable suffers.

### OBJECTIVE SAVED / INTEGRITY DAMAGED
Evidence recovered but the team used an avoidable emergency exception or created collateral service impact.

### TACTICAL SUCCESS / COMPLIANCE FAILURE
Hostiles stopped and evidence seized, but the player entered/changed out-of-scope state without sufficient authority.

### PARTIAL
Some evidence lost/damaged, but civilians and service preserved.

The player should learn immediately that “we got the box” is not the same as “we did the job correctly.”

# ACT IV — AFTERMATH (approx. 27:00–33:00)

## Task-force debrief

Some task-force personnel are irritated that Nusrat slowed the seizure while hostile pressure was active.

Others recognize that her delay prevented the team from disrupting unrelated services and contaminating the operation's legitimacy.

Nusrat does not get universal applause.

That matters.

Ethical process should sometimes feel socially expensive.

## Helen / Nusrat

Helen backs the defensible decision in review.

She does not tell Nusrat she was brave.

She asks whether Nusrat can document exactly why she stopped the operation and exactly why it restarted.

Nusrat can.

That is the compliment.

## Miguel

A facility employee thanks Miguel because he explained what was happening instead of barking orders.

Miguel shrugs it off.

Nusrat notices.

## Aya

Aya returns with a stronger belief that scope discipline protects technical people from being forced to convert uncertainty into confidence for somebody else's convenience.

She also remembers Jordan's earlier restraint.

## Priya / Maren

The polished onboarding day did not happen as planned.

Priya initially wants to erase the mess from the internal story.

Maren argues that the organization does not need to pretend competent institutions never experience friction.

They compromise on a truthful but non-dramatic internal communication.

This is the first tiny step in their future disagreement over whether communications should make the institution look good or make the institution legible.

## Chioma

Chioma reviews the field report.

Her strongest notes are not about who had the best technical answer.

She flags:
- Nusrat held scope under pressure;
- Miguel preserved cooperation;
- Aya did not overstate confidence;
- Jordan disclosed a useful device and then accepted that he could not use it.

Her assessment of Jordan is deliberately mundane:

> **He stopped when she told him to.**

# Hidden PRIM2 coda

Do not frame this as PRIM2 first discovering Jordan.

PRIM2 already knows who Jordan is.

What interests him is the new behavioral evidence.

The old Benji/Jordan history taught PRIM2 that Jordan can improvise around boundaries.

Episode 2 shows something different:

> **Jordan had capability, motive, opportunity and the correct tool — and chose not to cross the boundary.**

PRIM2 also records Nusrat as unusually reliable under hierarchy pressure.

This is important: PRIM2 is evaluating the institution, not merely staring at Jordan.
