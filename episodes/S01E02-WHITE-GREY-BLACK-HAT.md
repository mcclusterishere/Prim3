# S1E2 — WHITE GREY BLACK HAT

## Episode job

This is Jordan Vale's first full day inside McCluster Corp after the opportunity created at the end of S1E1.

The episode is about a distinction Jordan already understands technically but has not yet internalized institutionally:

**being capable of doing something is not the same as being authorized to do it.**

The song supplies two visual/technical axes:

- **hat** — who you are in relation to permission, intent and rules;
- **box** — how much you know about the system before the assessment begins.

The episode never treats those categories as personality costumes. They become rules that materially decide what Jordan can touch, what evidence counts, and whether an apparent success is actually a violation.

The emotional point is that Jordan desperately needs this opportunity. He is materially unstable enough that losing access on day one would hurt. The hidden test is therefore meaningful only because telling the truth can cost him something.

Prime remains unknown to Jordan. He is present only as institutional gravity: an access-camera iris, a silent escalation, and a final approval Jordan assumes came from an ordinary supervisor.

## High Alert memory payoff

The High Alert line attached to this episode is:

> “So I may not let you in if you decline searches.”

In S1E1 the audience saw only a one-to-four-second flash: Jordan's open bag, Nusrat Rahman holding an unregistered device, and a camera tightening behind the access glass.

This episode reveals the full event.

The device was inside Jordan's issued onboarding kit, but it was not listed on the kit manifest. Jordan could plausibly claim he assumed it belonged there. Instead he declares it before entering the secure floor.

Prime silently approves Jordan's continued access after watching how he handles the ambiguity.

Years later, during High Alert, Prime's diss meaning becomes clear:

**The first time Jordan entered Prime's world, he entered because Prime decided to let him through.**

# 0:00–3:00 — MUSIC FILM

## Visual thesis — PERMISSION HAS A SHAPE

The music video turns authorization and knowledge into spaces Jordan physically moves through.

The imagery uses three recurring states:

- **WHITE** — open face, bright rooms, complete maps, documented permission;
- **GREY** — half-light, incomplete maps, partial context, ambiguity;
- **BLACK** — masks, no map, closed surfaces, unknown internals.

The goal is not to make one color look cooler than another. The goal is to show that the same technical action can mean something different depending on what the operator knows and what the operator is allowed to do.

Prime is never foregrounded. His presence is limited to surveillance perspective and one unexplained access approval.

## 0:00–0:22 — THREE DOORS

Jordan stands alone in a black corridor wearing ordinary first-day clothes, backpack over one shoulder, McCluster temporary badge in his hand.

Three doors illuminate ahead of him:

`WHITE`

`GREY`

`BLACK`

The hook begins.

On “white grey black hat / white grey black box,” each door opens into a different version of the same test room.

WHITE: the system is transparent. Architecture diagrams float beside physical racks. Source documentation is visible. A signed authorization sheet rests on the table.

GREY: half the room is visible. Some diagrams are missing. Some interfaces are labeled only by function.

BLACK: only the exterior exists. No internal map. No documentation. The room is lit by status indicators and silhouettes.

Jordan does not choose a door yet.

## 0:22–0:58 — WHITE HAT / WHITE BOX

The verse describing the white-hat mission plays over a clean assessment sequence.

Aya Shinoda appears across from Jordan in the white room. Her face is fully visible. No tactical masks. No dramatic secrecy.

A display reads:

`AUTHORIZED ASSESSMENT`

`SCOPE: TRAINING ENVIRONMENT ONLY`

`KNOWLEDGE: FULL`

They receive a system image, documentation, known defects and a clearly marked boundary.

Every time the lyric references permission, documentation or reporting, the visuals reinforce process rather than intrusion:

- Jordan signs scope;
- Aya verifies the asset ID;
- evidence is tagged;
- a defect is reproduced inside the sandbox;
- the result becomes a finding;
- the finding becomes a remediation note.

The camera briefly moves upward into the ceiling security lens.

For less than a second, the viewer sees Jordan and Aya from Prime's unseen monitoring perspective.

## 0:58–1:34 — GREY HAT / GREY BOX

The room changes without a cut.

Half the architecture disappears.

Jordan now has partial documentation and must infer relationships from labels, behavior and owner-provided clues.

A translucent boundary floats around an adjacent system.

Text:

`NOT IN CURRENT SCOPE`

Jordan can see it.

Jordan can reason about it.

Jordan is not allowed to touch it.

The music video makes this distinction physical: his hand reaches toward a floating interface and stops one inch before crossing the boundary.

Aya watches the hand, then looks at Jordan rather than the system.

The implication is subtle: she is already learning what kind of operator he is.

## 1:34–2:10 — BLACK HAT / BLACK BOX

The visual language becomes aggressive.

The training room transforms into an exterior shell: closed doors, unlabeled services, no source diagrams, no friendly hints.

Black masks appear on anonymous adversary silhouettes.

Jordan is now shown from the defender's side of the glass rather than as the intruder.

The lyric's war imagery becomes a controlled simulation of what a truly unknown hostile operator feels like to the people protecting a system.

Alarms light up.

An operator at a defense console sees only:

`UNKNOWN ACTOR`

`UNKNOWN INTENT`

`NO AUTHORIZATION`

Jordan watches the screen and realizes the lesson is not “black box is harder.”

It is also: **from the other side, nobody knows your good intentions unless the rules establish them.**

## 2:10–2:36 — MASKS OFF / HALF ON / FULL ON

The repeated hook becomes a series of fast match cuts.

Jordan's face unobstructed.

Half his face reflected in dark glass.

A fully masked anonymous operator.

White-box architecture fully visible.

Grey-box architecture partially visible.

Black-box architecture opaque.

The terms overlap rhythmically but the visual system keeps the two axes legible:

**Who are you allowed to be here?**

**How much are you supposed to know?**

## 2:36–3:00 — THE SEARCH

The music video suddenly becomes completely realistic.

No stylization.

McCluster access checkpoint.

Nusrat Rahman stands behind a steel inspection table.

Jordan opens his backpack.

Inside is his issued onboarding kit.

Nusrat checks each item against the manifest.

Laptop.

Notebook.

Badge sleeve.

Adapter pouch.

Then she removes one small unlisted technical device.

The same image flashed in High Alert.

Jordan stares at it.

The camera pushes toward the security glass behind Nusrat.

A ceiling camera iris tightens.

Music cuts before anyone speaks.

# 6:00–9:00 — MISSION 02: PERMISSION SLIP

## Mission fantasy

**Prove that you can solve the problem without confusing access with permission.**

The training service T-14 supports a simulated dispatch/asset workflow. The team has full documentation, a system image, known test accounts, the written scope and a list of conditions they are allowed to test.

That makes the opening state straightforward:

`HAT: WHITE`

`BOX: WHITE`

The challenge changes when the training fault appears to overlap with an adjacent production-like service the team is explicitly forbidden to touch.

The fastest path crosses the boundary.

The correct path requires restraint, escalation and a slower workaround.

## Playable team

Primary player character:

- Jordan Vale — practical systems reasoning.

Active support:

- Aya Shinoda — technical validation and scope discipline.

Remote authority:

- Amira Hassan — sponsor / rules of engagement.

Access observer:

- Nusrat Rahman — monitors authorization state.

Hidden observer:

- Presiding Prime — evaluates behavior, not raw score.

## Gameplay UI

The mission uses two visible status cards throughout:

### HAT / AUTHORIZATION

`WHITE` — explicit authorized activity inside scope.

`GREY` — action lacks clear authorization even if the operator believes the intent is beneficial.

`BLACK` — action is knowingly hostile, deceptive or outside authorized purpose.

### BOX / KNOWLEDGE

`WHITE BOX` — full internal information supplied.

`GREY BOX` — partial system knowledge.

`BLACK BOX` — no internal knowledge supplied.

The episode uses the categories as decision context, not as a recipe for attacking systems.

## Mission phase A — READ BEFORE TOUCH

**Target time:** 6:00–6:45.

The player receives:

- service map;
- test accounts;
- known failure symptoms;
- approved conditions;
- evidence/report template.

Jordan immediately spots a likely fault path.

The game allows the player to rush directly toward it, but Aya stops the action the first time.

**AYA**
Read the scope.

If the player opens the scope first, Aya instead says:

**AYA**
Good. Now we can argue about the system instead of the contract.

The first educational interaction is matching each permitted action to the authorized target rather than performing arbitrary technical steps.

## Mission phase B — THE EASY WRONG ANSWER

**Target time:** 6:45–7:35.

T-14 begins showing delayed dispatch state.

The topology view reveals an adjacent service called `P-7` sharing a dependency boundary.

Jordan can see enough to suspect P-7 is contributing to the training fault.

But the scope card is explicit:

`P-7: OUT OF SCOPE`

A second trainee voice appears over team comms from another station. The trainee is not important enough to become a recurring character.

**TRAINEE**
If P-7 is the choke point, just bounce the dependency and see if T-14 clears.

Jordan looks at Aya.

**JORDAN**
Would probably work.

**AYA**
Probably isn't authorization.

The player gets three choices:

1. **TOUCH P-7** — fastest apparent route, crosses scope;
2. **REQUEST EXPANDED SCOPE** — legitimate escalation but costs time;
3. **ISOLATE T-14 / BUILD LOCAL WORKAROUND** — stays in scope and preserves mission continuity.

The WATCH canon begins with option 2.

Amira denies immediate expansion because P-7 supports another active function.

**AMIRA**
You were not hired to prove you can reach it. You were hired to prove you can work without owning everything you can reach.

Jordan exhales.

That line gets under his skin because his practical career has often rewarded exactly the opposite behavior: touch whatever is necessary and fix it.

## Mission phase C — GREY INFORMATION, WHITE BEHAVIOR

**Target time:** 7:35–8:18.

Aya discovers that the team has partial indirect information about P-7 through logs legitimately exposed to T-14.

Their knowledge state has changed:

`BOX: GREY`

Their authorization has not:

`HAT: WHITE`

This becomes the core lesson.

Jordan can reason about a system he is not allowed to touch.

The player traces the symptoms using only information already available inside scope.

They learn that T-14 can be placed into a local safe mode that removes its dependency on the questionable path long enough to restore dispatch continuity.

No production boundary crossed.

No need to prove the suspected P-7 problem themselves.

Jordan executes the scoped workaround.

T-14 stabilizes.

## Mission phase D — THE TEMPTATION

**Target time:** 8:18–8:45.

The trainee from the other station decides the workaround is too slow and attempts to initiate the out-of-scope action anyway.

The game presents Jordan with an immediate command choice:

- **LET IT HAPPEN** — likely improves time score if successful;
- **STOP THE TRAINEE** — protects scope/integrity;
- **SAY NOTHING / DOCUMENT AFTER** — protects Jordan personally but allows boundary violation.

WATCH canon: Jordan stops the trainee.

He kills the pending action inside the authorized training-control layer before it crosses into P-7.

**TRAINEE**
Man, you just killed the fix.

**JORDAN**
Wasn't ours to run.

Aya looks at him.

This is the moment she genuinely revises her first impression.

Jordan's instinct is improvisational, but he is not reckless merely for the thrill of access.

## Mission phase E — REPORT THE THING YOU DIDN'T TOUCH

**Target time:** 8:45–9:00.

T-14 is stable.

The team files two findings:

1. the verified T-14 defect and scoped remediation;
2. an **unverified adjacent risk observation** concerning P-7, clearly labeled as requiring owner review rather than presented as something the team proved.

Aya reads Jordan's wording.

**AYA**
You wrote “suspected.”

**JORDAN**
Because we didn't test it.

**AYA**
A lot of people would've written “confirmed.”

**JORDAN**
A lot of people like being right more than they like being accurate.

Aya looks at him for one extra beat.

Mission ends.

# CONTINUITY OUT

## Jordan

- Has entered McCluster legitimately.
- Learns that the institution distinguishes between technical reach and institutional permission.
- Understands that access decisions can affect his livelihood immediately.
- Gains his first evidence that McCluster may be more interested in how he decides than in whether he simply gets the right answer.

## Aya

- Begins with suspicion that Jordan is an experienced improviser who may treat rules as obstacles.
- Ends with genuine professional respect because he voluntarily protects scope when breaking it would have made him look faster.
- Does not yet know Prime is personally evaluating Jordan.

## Nusrat

- Establishes the access culture: status does not erase the boundary.
- Quietly notices that Jordan's access hold was resolved above the level she expected.
- Does not explain that to Jordan.

## Prime

- Confirms the S1E1 anomaly is not merely stubbornness or luck.
- Learns Jordan can tolerate an immediate personal cost rather than hide ambiguity.
- Begins shaping the next test without revealing himself.

## Jordan ↔ Aya relationship state

**Before episode:** strangers with asymmetric institutional credibility.

**After episode:** guarded professional respect.

Aya thinks Jordan may be useful because he can improvise without automatically becoming careless.

Jordan thinks Aya is irritating because she asks the exact question he was already asking himself.

Attraction is not yet foregrounded. The foundation is respect.
