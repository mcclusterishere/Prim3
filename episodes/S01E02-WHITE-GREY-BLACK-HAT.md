# Human problem

Nusrat believes a rule that changes depending on who is standing at the door is not security; it is theater.

Jordan arrives for onboarding with an issued kit containing one device that does not appear on the manifest.

He may be innocent.
He may be careless.
He may be testing the boundary.
The access team does not get to decide based on whether they like him.

Nusrat has to enforce the same process she would enforce against somebody more powerful, while Aya evaluates whether Jordan's technical confidence makes him useful or dangerous.

Jordan desperately wants the opportunity and has every incentive to minimize the discrepancy.

He declares it instead.

# Technical thesis

The song's white/grey/black-hat and white/grey/black-box language becomes two separate axes:

```text
AUTHORIZATION / INTENT
vs
SYSTEM KNOWLEDGE / VISIBILITY
```

Capability never creates permission by itself.

The player learns:
- written scope;
- asset identity;
- least privilege;
- rules of engagement;
- documented exception handling;
- evidence preservation;
- why “probably fine” is not an authorization state.

# WATCH structure

### Human trigger
Jordan's kit fails manifest verification at the secure entry point.

### Music film / brief
White, grey and black spaces visualize changing combinations of permission and knowledge. Nusrat's access station remains the stable reference: the color of the room does not change the need to know what authority actually exists.

### Human escalation
Pressure builds to wave Jordan through because he is expected, useful and already sponsored by people above the desk.

Nusrat refuses an undocumented exception.
Aya backs her even though it delays onboarding.
Jordan voluntarily declares the discrepancy and leaves the item controlled until provenance is established.

### Mission threshold
A second assessment environment begins depending on the delayed credential/kit decision. The access issue now affects real schedules and personnel, but urgency still does not rewrite scope.

### WATCH mission
Nusrat, Aya and Miguel reconcile the manifest, authority chain and safe onboarding state without granting Jordan privileges merely to save time.

# PLAY contract

The player controls the people responsible for deciding what Jordan may touch.

Primary verbs:
- inspect;
- compare;
- verify;
- hold;
- document;
- escalate;
- authorize;
- deny;
- preserve.

The key tactical tension is time versus integrity.

Strong play can keep onboarding moving through legitimate alternatives instead of either:
- blindly admitting Jordan;
- or treating ambiguity as proof of wrongdoing.

Jordan can suggest explanations, but the player cannot simply switch to him and override the process.

# PRIM2 trace

PRIM2 does not need a physical cameo.

After Nusrat's process produces a defensible result, an unexplained higher-authority approval allows Jordan's onboarding path to continue.

Nusrat sees only that the escalation resolved.
Jordan assumes ordinary management approved it.
The audience does not yet know a Mantle holder is evaluating the incident.

The later High Alert memory makes the moment sinister without invalidating Nusrat's ownership.
