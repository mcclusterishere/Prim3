# S4E1 - INSTALLATION TYPES

## Merge contract

This chapter preserves Jordan and Aya making their relationship explicit while
both deepen their commitment to the institution. The story uses installation
and upgrade language as metaphor without implying that either person can erase
or redesign the other.

## Chapter promise

A remote site must be migrated under time pressure. Some systems can be safely
upgraded in place; others must be rebuilt from a trusted baseline. Jordan and
Aya face the same question personally: what can be carried forward, and what
must stop being inherited by default?

## Song payload

- **Technical:** clean installation, in-place upgrade, multiboot/compatibility,
  backup, migration, registry/configuration repair, validation, and reboot.
- **Human:** attraction, compatibility, commitment, frustration, and the desire
  to help someone change without controlling them.
- **Visual:** two environments sharing one machine; old files projected over a
  clean room; personal memories migrating beside system state.
- **Safety:** installation decisions use fictional service components and
  explicit backup/rollback checks, not live destructive commands.

## Nine-minute episode

### 0:00-3:00 - Music film

Jordan and Aya move through parallel rooms labeled preserve, repair, migrate,
and rebuild. The hook asks “what's your type?” while the visual answer shifts
from hardware to the histories each person carries.

### 3:00-6:00 - Human story

They stop pretending their connection is only professional. The admission is
immediately complicated by new institutional commitments and the unresolved
secrets Jordan carries from Season 3. Aya refuses to accept intimacy built on
an incomplete backup of the truth.

### 6:00-9:00 - Watch / Play mission

WATCH divides the environment correctly: fragile compromised systems receive a
clean rebuild while stable systems are upgraded with validation and rollback.
PLAY asks the player to inventory dependencies, choose a migration type per
component, stage backups, validate the restored service, and decide when to
roll back.

## Playable level contract

- **Player role:** Jordan and Aya as a two-person migration lead.
- **Environment:** remote operations site with old workstations, local services,
  a clean staging rack, and users waiting to resume work.
- **Primary objective:** restore the minimum critical environment without
  carrying untrusted state into it.
- **Optional objectives:** preserve user data; minimize downtime; document every
  abandoned component; protect rollback media.
- **Complication:** one familiar legacy feature depends on the least trustworthy
  machine.
- **Exit state:** service returns; Jordan and Aya commit to each other without
  pretending either begins from a clean slate.

## Player verbs

Inventory, classify, back up, choose install type, migrate, validate, roll back,
document.

## Graded outcomes

| Channel | Strong result | Weak but continuing result |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Critical services restored from trusted state | Service restored with a known fragile dependency |
| Team | Users and operators understand the change | Team is surprised by missing behavior/data |
| Exposure | Old environment remains isolated | Legacy state leaks into the new build |
| Integrity | Backups and rollback are tested | Success is accepted without validation |
| Time | Sequence minimizes actual downtime | Fast start creates later rework |

## Persistent state written by the level

Jordan/Aya relationship becomes explicit. The mission records preserved versus
abandoned dependencies, creating a reusable migration history. Concealed
personal information from Season 3 remains pressure for later rupture.

## Prime background trace

- **Visible trace:** Jordan and Aya's separate placement screens are combined
  into one operational region by an unseen approver carrying the `P-00` mark.
- **First-watch meaning:** ordinary staffing chose to keep a productive pair in
  the same region.
- **Ghost payoff:** Prime did not create their relationship, but he modeled it
  as an operational dependency and approved the structure that kept them close.

## Technology accuracy gate

- **Sensor / input:** inventory, health checks, dependency map, user needs,
  backup/restore test results.
- **Compute / reasoning:** select clean build, in-place upgrade, or retirement
  according to trust and dependency state.
- **Energy / infrastructure:** local power, staging hardware, storage, network,
  and backup media.
- **Access / authorization:** owner-approved site migration with scoped admin
  credentials.
- **Human decision owner:** site owner; Jordan/Aya recommend and execute.
- **Failure mode:** untrusted state is preserved, needed data is erased, or
  rollback was assumed rather than tested.
- **Real concept taught:** installation strategy is a risk and continuity
  decision, not merely a preference.
- **Fictional extrapolation:** none required.
