# McCluster Corp Private Client Fashion Guide

> **WEALTH, NOT RICHES.** McCluster is the luxury collection the public never sees and the employee never mistakes for merchandise.

This document controls every McCluster garment, accessory, wardrobe render, vendor brief, costume build, and AI prompt. When another instruction conflicts with this guide, this guide wins unless the brand owner explicitly changes canon.

## 1. The house position

McCluster Corp is a discreet global technology institution with the discipline of a private luxury house. The clothing is corporate issue, but it must feel commissioned—not promotional. It signals access, standards, and internal belonging without asking strangers for attention.

The desired reaction is not “that person bought an expensive outfit.” It is “that person belongs somewhere I cannot enter.”

A Gucci-level reference means material quality, house consistency, cultural confidence, and atelier-grade execution. It never authorizes copying Gucci—or any other real fashion house—marks, patterns, proportions, or signature products.

## 2. North-star distinction

| Wealth | Riches |
|---|---|
| Fabric, cut, drape, restraint | Price signaling and spectacle |
| Private identifiers | Public-status logos |
| Tonal embroidery discovered up close | Giant contrast branding seen across a room |
| One exact mark with intent | Many marks filling empty space |
| Garments that remain credible without a logo | Garments that depend on a logo |
| Institutional confidence | Drop culture, hype, novelty |

If a design reads like merch, a tracksuit, a matching monogram set, a costume, a nightclub uniform, or a social-media flex, reject it.

## 3. Locked identity system

There are only three approved symbols.

| Mark | Meaning | Default fashion behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **M emblem / the House** | McCluster institutional identity | One small, exact, deliberate house signature |
| **Dual Sight** | Peace: camera, observation, record. War: optic, acquisition, red dot. | Tonal black-on-black, often discovered in raking light |
| **Two States `//`** | Peace and wartime operating states | A micro red code, bartack, cuff detail, or controlled state signal |

### Identity fidelity—zero tolerance

- Use the exact master SVG. Never redraw, trace, simplify, auto-vectorize, “clean up,” mirror, stretch, or regenerate a mark.
- A generic outlined letter M is not the McCluster emblem.
- A generic crosshair, flower, cog, compass, camera icon, or gaming reticle is not Dual Sight.
- Two random red strokes are not Two States. Their separation, slant, width, and proportion are locked.
- No designer or AI may invent a fourth glyph to “complete” a garment.
- If exact placement is impossible, **leave it blank**. Blank is luxurious; a fake mark is brand damage.

## 4. The visual hierarchy

Every piece must communicate in this order:

1. **Silhouette** — exact fit, balanced proportion, adult authority.
2. **Material** — the hand, drape, luster, and weight reveal the price.
3. **Construction** — seams, buttons, facings, hems, hardware, and finishing reward inspection.
4. **Identity** — one exact mark confirms the house; it never rescues a weak garment.

From three meters away, the core office wardrobe should look almost unbranded. From one meter, construction and tonal identity appear. At hand distance, the wearer discovers the internal codes.

## 5. Color and finish

| Role | Standard | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Authority black | `#080A0C` | Dominant substrate; matte to low luster |
| House ivory | `#ECE6D8` | Restricted linework or a very small house mark |
| State red | `#BC1B2A` | Micro accent only in the office core |
| Tonal black | Physical thread/finish match | May shift by texture and gloss, never by obvious contrast |

Avoid optic white, bright scarlet, glossy polyester shine, obvious gold plating, rhinestones, loud contrast piping, and decorative hardware with no function.

## 6. Season One office core

### Button-up shirts

- Solid authority black.
- One exact M emblem may appear at the left chest, small enough to remain a signature rather than a billboard.
- One exact Dual Sight may appear black-on-black at a sleeve hem, cuff, back yoke, or under-collar position.
- One exact Two States glyph may appear as a tiny red cuff, placket, collar-tip, or side-seam code.
- Exterior repeat is prohibited. Repeat may appear inside a collar stand, under a cuff, inside the back yoke, or in another private reveal.

### Trousers

- The visual field is 95–99% plain black.
- Use a precise professional rise, pressed crease, clean waistband, controlled break, and excellent pocket construction.
- Optional identity: one tiny tonal M near a pocket or inside the waistband, plus one red Two States bartack at a belt loop or pocket edge.
- No exterior all-over pattern, large M, bright Dual Sight, stripe system, cargo clutter, or matching-shirt monogram.

### Tailored shorts

- Treat as abbreviated trousers, not street shorts.
- Plain black with one optional tonal Dual Sight near the lower hem and one micro Two States bartack.
- No repeat, athletic trim, oversized pockets, drawstrings, or visible tactical webbing in the office core.

### Outerwear, linings, and accessories

- Controlled monogram density is permitted where the object can carry it: a lining, reversible panel, scarf, leather good, luggage interior, special overcoat, or private-issue accessory.
- Only one statement field should be visible at a time. Pair it with quiet garments.
- Full repeat is an exception requiring brand-owner approval, never the default answer to “make it McCluster.”

## 7. Material language

Preferred expressions include cotton-silk poplin, compact long-staple cotton, silk-wool or tropical-wool tailoring, fine-gauge merino, cashmere, dense matte technical fabric, calfskin or lambskin, black mother-of-pearl, horn, and dark gunmetal.

Materials must not merely photograph as expensive. They must survive movement, close-up photography, repeated wardrobe use, and physical handling without exposing cheap sheen or weak construction.

## 8. Mark density and placement budget

- Default office garment: **zero to three identity events total**.
- Default visible contrast mark: **zero or one**.
- Red is punctuation, not a panel color.
- Never place the M, Dual Sight, and Two States at equal visual weight.
- Never center a large chest logo on the office core.
- Never repeat a mark simply because a surface is empty.
- Negative space is part of the identity system.

## 9. Generative-image and AI contract

Image models may develop silhouette, styling, fabric, lighting, and environment. They are **not identity-authoring tools**.

Required workflow:

1. Generate or edit the garment with blank branding zones and the correct quiet-luxury direction.
2. Place the exact master SVG as a separate vector layer after generation.
3. For embroidery visualization, apply texture to that placed vector without altering its silhouette or internal geometry.
4. Compare the final mark against the master at 100% and 400% zoom.
5. Delete any rendering with invented geometry. Do not call it “close enough.”

## 10. Manufacturer and embroidery contract

- Vector masters define geometry; the approved physical sample defines final scale, thread, relief, and hand.
- Digitizers may optimize stitch sequence, underlay, pull compensation, density, and trims. They may not redesign the mark.
- Produce a sew-out on the actual fabric before bulk approval.
- Tonal marks should change through thread luster or relief—not obvious gray contrast.
- Two States red must be tested under warm interior light and camera flash.
- Small-scale failure is solved by enlarging, relocating, changing application method, or omitting the mark—not simplifying it.
- Print strike-offs, embroidery sew-outs, hardware samples, and full garment prototypes require brand-owner approval.

## 11. Language system

Preferred internal terms: **private client**, **corporate issue**, **house**, **wardrobe**, **atelier**, **uniform system**, **state code**, **tonal identity**, **controlled reveal**, **commissioned**.

Avoid in official presentations: **merch**, **drop**, **hype**, **flex**, **logo blast**, **streetwear collab**, **designer-inspired**, **Gucci print**, **Supreme-like**.

Characters may casually call it “swag” in dialogue. The institution does not.

## 12. Automatic rejection checklist

Reject the design if any answer is yes:

- Is any symbol an approximation rather than the master?
- Does the logo carry the garment instead of the cut and material?
- Do the shirt and trousers create a loud matching set?
- Can the red accent be read as a major color block?
- Does it resemble public promotional merchandise?
- Does it borrow another real house's protected visual language?
- Would the piece look cheap or anonymous with the marks removed?
- Does it feel designed for attention instead of access?

If uncertain, remove one visible mark and improve the fabric or construction.

## 13. Final approval sentence

A McCluster piece is approved only when it looks quietly inevitable, remains luxurious without branding, and uses every visible identity mark with exact fidelity.
